***
We claim the only solutions are $f(x)\equiv x$ and $f(x)\equiv 0$, which obviously work. We now show they are the only solutions. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion that $f(x^2+xf(y))=xf(x)+yf(x)$. 
***
Obviously $f\equiv 0$ is a solution. Assume from now on that $f$ is non-constant.

$P(0,2)$ gives $f(0)=0$.

If $f(u)=0$ for some $u\in \mathbb{R}$, then $P(x,0)$ gives $f(x^2)=xf(x)$ while $P(x,u)$ gives $f(x^2)=xf(x)+uf(x)$. Hence, $uf(x)=0$ for all $x\in \mathbb{R}$. Since $f$ is non-constant, $u=0$. This means that $f$ is injective at 0.

Finally, $P(-x,x)$ gives $f(x^2-xf(x))=0$, so $x(f(x)-x)=0$ and hence $f(x)=x$ as desired.
