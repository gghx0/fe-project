Let $P(x,y)$ be the assertion

$$
f(f(x)^2+yf(x))=x^2+f(xy).
$$

First, $f(0)=0$. If $a=f(0)\ne 0$, then $P(0,y)$ says $f(a^2+ay)=a$ for every $y$, so $f$ is constant, which is impossible in the original equation.

From $P(x,0)$,

$$
f(f(x)^2)=x^2,
$$

so $f(k)=0$ implies $k=0$. Also, $P(x,-f(x))$ gives

$$
f(-xf(x))=-x^2.
$$

These two identities show that $f$ attains every nonnegative and every nonpositive real value, so $f$ is surjective.

Using $P(x,1)$ and $P(x,0)$,

$$
f(f(x)^2+f(x))=f(f(x)^2)+f(x).
$$

Since $f$ is surjective, for every real $t$ this gives

$$
f(t^2+t)=f(t^2)+t.
$$

Taking $t=-1$ yields $f(1)=1$. Hence $P(1,y)$ gives

$$
f(y+1)=f(y)+1
$$

for all real $y$.

Now fix $x\ne 0$. Since $f(x)\ne 0$, every real $t$ can be written as $t=f(x)^2+yf(x)$. Applying $P(x,y+1/x)$ and using $f(u+1)=f(u)+1$ gives

$$
f\left(t+\frac{f(x)}{x}\right)=f(t)+1.
$$

At $t=0$, this says

$$
f\left(\frac{f(x)}{x}\right)=1.
$$

If $f(u)=1$, then $P(u,0)$ gives $f(1)=u^2$, so $u=\pm 1$. Therefore $f(x)=x$ or $f(x)=-x$ for each $x\ne 0$.

If $f(x)=-x$ for some nonzero $x$, then the previous translation identity gives $f(t-1)=f(t)+1$ for all $t$, contradicting $f(t)=f(t-1)+1$. Hence $f(x)=x$ for every $x$, and the unique solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x}.
$$
