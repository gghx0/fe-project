Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

Let

$$
A=\{yf(y):y\in\mathbb{R}\}.
$$

The equation says

$$
f(x^2+a)=x^2+a
$$

for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$ and every $a\in A$.

If $A$ is unbounded below, then every real number can be written as $x^2+a$ with $x^2\ge0$ and $a\in A$, so $f(x)=x$ for all $x$.

Now assume $A$ is bounded below, and set

$$
c=\inf A.
$$

Since $0\in A$, we have $c\le0$. The equation gives $f(t)=t$ for every $t>c$. If the infimum is attained, it also gives $f(c)=c$.

Thus the solutions are precisely the following two families.

First,

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\ge c,\\
\dfrac{c}{x}+g(x),&x<c,
\end{cases}}
$$

where $c\le0$ and $g:(-\infty,c)\to(-\infty,0]$ is arbitrary.

Second,

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x>c,\\
\dfrac{c}{x}+g(x),&x\le c,
\end{cases}}
$$

where $c<0$ and $g:(-\infty,c]\to(-\infty,0)$ is arbitrary.

In both families, every value $yf(y)$ is at least $c$ in the first case and greater than $c$ in the second case, so the argument $x^2+yf(y)$ always lies in the region where $f$ is the identity. Hence all listed functions work, and the preceding argument shows there are no others.
