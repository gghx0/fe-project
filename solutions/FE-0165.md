$$
f(f(x)+f(xy))=x+xf(y),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}
$$

Very nice.
Observe first that $f(y)=-1$ for all $y$ is not a solution. So there exists $a$ such that $f(a)\neq -1$.
Therefore

$$
P(x,a):\,f(f(x)+f(ax))=x(f(a)+1),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}
$$

which immediately gives $f$: surjective. Furthermore we have

$$
P(x,1):f(2f(x))=x(f(1)+1),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}\quad (1)
$$

If $f(1)=-1$ surjectivity of $f$ implies $f\equiv 0$ which is not a solution. Thus $f(1)\neq -1$ using $(1)$, it is trivial to show that $f$ is injective.
We shall now prove that $f(-1)=-1$ and $f(0)=0$.
If $f(0)=-1$ then for some $k$ such that $f(k)=1$ we have

$$
P(k,0):\,f(f(k)-1)=0\Rightarrow f(0)=0
$$

contradiction. So for some $t\neq 0$ we have $f(t)=-1$. We have

$$
P(1,t)-P\left(t,\frac{1}{t}\right):\,f(f(1)+f(t))-f(f(t)+f(1))=1+f(t)-t-tf\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)\Rightarrow
$$

$$
\Rightarrow f\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)=-1=f(t)\Rightarrow
$$

$$
\Rightarrow t^2=1\xRightarrow{f(1)\neq -1}t=-1
$$

We also have

$$
P(0,1):\,f(2f(0))=0
$$

If $f(0)\neq 0$ then

$$
P(2f(0),1):\,f(0)=2f(0)(f(1)+1)\Rightarrow 2f(1)+2=1\Rightarrow f(1)=-\frac{1}{2}
$$

However, this implies

$$
P(1,1):\,f(2f(1))=f(1)+1\Rightarrow f(-1)=\frac{1}{2}
$$

contradiction. Thus $f(0)=0$.
Now we have

$$
P(x,-1):\,f(f(x)+f(-x))=0\Rightarrow f(-x)=-f(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}
$$

Thus $f(1)=1$ and so

$$
P(x,1):\,f(2f(x))=2x,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}
$$

Setting $x\to2f(x)$ at the above gives

$$
f(4x)=4f(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R},\quad(2)
$$

To finish off, we have

$$
P\left(x,\frac{y}{x}\right):\,f(f(x)+f(y))=x+xf\left(\frac{y}{x}\right),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R},x\neq 0
$$

and swapping $x$ and $y$ gives

$$
x+xf\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)=y+yf\left(\frac{x}{y}\right),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^*,\quad(3)
$$

For $y=1$ in $(3)$ we have

$$
x+xf\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=f(x)+1\Rightarrow 4x+4xf\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=4f(x)+4,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^*\quad(4)
$$

and for $y=4$ in $(3)$ we have

$$
x+xf\left(\frac{4}{x}\right)=4+4f\left(\frac{x}{4}\right)\xRightarrow{(2)} x+4xf\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)=4+f(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^*\quad(5)
$$

Subtracting $(4)$ and $(5)$ gives is

$$
3f(x)=3x\Rightarrow f(x)=x,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^*
$$

and since $f(0)=0$ we have

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}}
$$

which we can verify it works. $\blacksquare$
