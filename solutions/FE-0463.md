Solution by **jasperE3**.

Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(xf(x)+f(xy))=xf(x)+xy.
$$

From $P(1,y)$,

$$
f(f(1)+f(y))=f(1)+y,
$$

so $f$ is bijective.

We first prove that $f(f(x))=x$. If $f(0)\ne0$, then $P(0,0)$ gives $f(f(0))=0$, and

$$
P\left(f(0),\frac{x}{f(0)}\right)
$$

gives $f(f(x))=x$.

If $f(0)=0$, then $P(1,0)$ and injectivity give $f(1)=1$. Thus $P(1,x)$ gives

$$
f(f(x)+1)=x+1.
$$

Applying this once more yields $f(x+2)=f(x)+2$, so $f(x+4)=f(x)+4$. Since $f(2)=2$, substituting $P(2,x/2)$ gives

$$
f(f(x)+4)=x+4,
$$

and hence $f(f(x))=x$.

Now replace $y$ by $f(y)/x$ when $x\ne0$. Using $f(f(y))=y$, we get

$$
f(xf(x)+y)=xf(x)+f(y).
$$

Equivalently,

$$
f(y+xf(x))=f(y)+xf(x).
$$

By FE-0444, either $f(x)=0$ for all $x\ne0$, or $f(x)=x+c$. The first option contradicts $f(f(x))=x$. In the second option, involutivity gives $c=0$. Therefore $f(x)=x$, which works.
