$$

$$

$P(1,x)\Rightarrow f(f(1)+f(x))=f(1)+x$ so $f$ is bijective.

**Claim:** $f(f(x))=x$.

If $f(0)\ne0$:

$P(0,0)\Rightarrow f(f(0))=0$.

$P\left(f(0),\frac x{f(0)}\right)\Rightarrow f(f(x))=x$.

If $f(0)=0$:

$P(1,0)\Rightarrow f(f(1))=f(1)\Rightarrow f(1)=1$.

$P(1,x)\Rightarrow f(f(x)+1)=x+1\Rightarrow f(x+2)=f(f(f(x)+1)+1)=f(x)+2$ from which we obtain $f(2)=2$ and $f(x+4)=f(x)+4$.

$P\left(2,\frac x2\right)\Rightarrow f(f(x))=x$.

Hence proven.

Now from the original equation:

$$
f(xf(x)+xy)=xf(x)+f(xy)
$$

which is #444. Since $xf(x)=0$ for all $x$ is impossible by $f(f(x))=x$, the only other possibility is $f(x)=x+c$, and testing, we have the unique solution $\boxed{f(x)=x}$.
