Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(f(x)^2+f(xy))=f(x)^2+yf(x).
$$

The zero function is a solution. Now suppose $f$ is not identically zero. Then there exists $a$ with $f(a)\ne0$, and $P(a,y)$ shows that the range of $f$ is all of $\mathbb R$.

Using $P(x,1)$,

$$
f(f(x)^2+f(x))=f(x)^2+f(x).
$$

Since $f$ is surjective, this implies

$$
f(t^2+t)=t^2+t
$$

for every real $t$. In particular, $f(u)=u$ for every $u\ge0$.

Now $P(x,x)$ gives

$$
f(f(x)^2+f(x^2))=f(x)^2+xf(x).
$$

Since $x^2\ge0$, we have $f(x^2)=x^2$. Also $f(x)^2+x^2\ge0$, so the left side is

$$
f(x)^2+x^2.
$$

Therefore

$$
f(x)^2+x^2=f(x)^2+xf(x),
$$

so $xf(x)=x^2$ for all $x$. Hence $f(x)=x$ for $x\ne0$, and also $f(0)=0$. Thus the nonzero solution is $f(x)=x$.

Therefore the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=x}.
$$
