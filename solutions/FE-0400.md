Solution by **OronSH**.
***

Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(xf(x)+yf(x))=xf(x)+xf(y).
$$

The zero function is a solution. We now assume $f$ is not constant.

Taking $x=0$ gives $f(yf(0))=0$ for all $y$, so $f(0)=0$. Taking $y=0$ gives

$$
f(xf(x))=xf(x).
$$

Also, $P(x,-x)$ gives

$$
xf(x)+xf(-x)=0,
$$

so $f$ is odd.

If $f(c)=0$, then $P(c,y)$ gives $cf(y)=0$ for all $y$, so $c=0$. Thus $0$ has the unique preimage $0$.

Now suppose $f(a)=f(b)$. If this common value is $0$, then $a=b=0$. Otherwise, applying $P(a,b-a)$ gives

$$
f(bf(a))=af(a)+af(b-a)=bf(a),
$$

so

$$
(b-a)f(a)=af(b-a).
$$

Similarly,

$$
(a-b)f(b)=bf(a-b).
$$

Using $f(a)=f(b)$ and oddness, we get

$$
af(b-a)=bf(b-a),
$$

hence $a=b$. Therefore $f$ is injective.

From $f(xf(x))=xf(x)$ with $x=1$, we get $f(f(1))=f(1)$, so injectivity gives $f(1)=1$. Thus

$$
P(1,y):\quad f(1+y)=1+f(y).
$$

Replacing $y$ by $y+1$ in $P(x,y)$ gives

$$
f(xf(x)+yf(x)+f(x))=xf(x)+xf(y)+x.
$$

Since $xf(x)\ne0$ for $x\ne0$, the quantity $xf(x)+yf(x)$ ranges over all real values as $y$ varies. Hence

$$
f(z+f(x))=f(z)+x
$$

for all real $z,x$. Setting $z=0$ gives $f(f(x))=x$. Substituting $x=f(t)$ gives

$$
f(z+t)=f(z)+f(t),
$$

so $f$ is additive.

The original equation now becomes

$$
f(yf(x))=xf(y).
$$

Putting $x=f(t)$ gives $f(yt)=f(y)f(t)$, so $f$ is multiplicative. Since $f(x^2)=f(x)^2\ge0$, the additive function $f$ is bounded below on an interval, hence linear. With $f(1)=1$, we get $f(x)=x$.

Together with the zero solution, the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=x}.
$$
