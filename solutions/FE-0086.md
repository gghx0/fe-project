The answer is $f\equiv0$ and $x\mapsto x$.

$P(x, 1)$ gives $f(x+f(x))=2f(x)$.

$P(0,0)$ gives $f(f(0))=f(0)$. But $f(f(0))=f(0+f(0))=2f(0)$, so $2f(0)=f(0)$, therefore, $f(0)=0$.

Case 1. Suppose that there exists $s\neq0$ such that $f(s)=0$. $P\left(x, \frac{s}{x}\right)$ gives for all $x\neq0$ the equality $f(x)=f(x)+\frac{s}{x}\cdot f(x)$, so $f(x)=0$ for all $x\neq0$. With equality $f(0)=0$ we obtain $f\equiv0$.

Case 2. $f(s)=0$ implies $s=0$. Then $P(-x, -1)$ gives $f(-x+f(x))=0$, so $f(x)-x=0$ and so $x\mapsto^{f} x$.
