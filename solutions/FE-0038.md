The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=cx\quad(x\in\mathbb R)}
$$

for arbitrary constants $c\in\mathbb R$. These clearly work.

Assume $f\not\equiv0$. Choose $u$ with $f(u)\ne0$, and put

$$
t=f(u^2),\qquad B=f(u)^2>0.
$$

Then

$$
f(x+t)=f(x)+B,
$$

so $t\ne0$. Comparing the original equation at $y$ and $-y$ gives $f(-y)^2=f(y)^2$. Therefore

$$
(f(-y)+B)^2=f(-y+t)^2=f(y-t)^2=(f(y)-B)^2,
$$

and hence $f(-y)=-f(y)$. In particular, $f(0)=0$.

Let $h=f\circ f$. Taking $x=0$ gives

$$
h(y^2)=f(y)^2.
$$

Thus $h$ is odd and $h(s)\ge0$ for $s\ge0$. The equation extends, using oddness, to

$$
f(x+f(z))=f(x)+h(z)\qquad(x,z\in\mathbb R).
$$

Set $a=|t|>0$. The translation formula gives

$$
f(x+a)=f(x)+b
$$

for some $b\ne0$; since $f(0)=0$, we have $b=f(a)$. Hence

$$
h(x+a)=f(f(x)+f(a))=h(x)+h(a).
$$

Write $d=h(a)\ge0$. For $0\le r<a$, the inequalities $h(r)\ge0$ and $h(r-a)\le0$ imply $0\le h(r)\le d$. Therefore, writing $s=na+r$ with $n\in\mathbb Z$ and $0\le r<a$,

$$
\left|h(s)-\frac da s\right|\le d.
$$

Now fix $y$. Since $f(y+na)=f(y)+nb$, applying the last bound to $s=(y+na)^2$ gives

$$
\left|(f(y)+nb)^2-\frac da (y+na)^2\right|\le d
$$

for all positive integers $n$. The quadratic polynomial inside the absolute value is bounded as $n\to\infty$, so its quadratic and linear coefficients vanish:

$$
b^2=da,\qquad bf(y)=dy.
$$

Thus $f(y)=\frac db y=\frac ba y$ for every real $y$. Including $f\equiv0$, this gives exactly the stated family.
