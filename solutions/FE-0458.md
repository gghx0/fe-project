We claim that the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=-x}.
$$

First, $P(-1,1)$ gives $f(0)=0$. Also $P(1,0)$ and then $P(f(1),0)$ give

$$
f(1)^2=1.
$$

If $f(1)=1$, then

$$
P(1,x):\quad f(f(x)+1)=x+1,
$$

so $f(x+2)=f(x)+2$. The equation becomes, for $x\ne0$,

$$
f(xf(x)+f(y))=x^2+y.
$$

Using the shift by $2$ twice, this gives

$$
f(xf(x)+y)=x^2+f(y),
$$

and hence

$$
f(xf(x)+xy)=x^2+f(xy).
$$

By FE-0439, the only possibilities are $f(x)=x$ and $f(x)=-x$; the condition $f(1)=1$ leaves only $f(x)=x$.

If $f(1)=-1$, then $P(1,0)$ gives $f(-1)=1$, and $P(1,1)$ gives $f(-2)=2$. Also,

$$
P(1,x):\quad f(f(x)-1)=x+1.
$$

Comparing $P(x,1)$ and $P(f(x)-1,1)$ yields

$$
f(x)^2-f(x)=x^2+x,
$$

so $f(x)\in\{-x,x+1\}$ for each $x$. If $f(k)=k+1$ for some $k$, then substituting in $P(k,1)$ forces $k=-\frac12$. Finally,

$$
P\left(2,-\frac74\right)
$$

forces $f(-\frac12)=\frac12$. Thus $f(x)=-x$ for every $x$.

Both listed functions are easily checked, so the proof is complete.
