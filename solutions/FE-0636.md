We claim that the only solutions are $f(x)\equiv0$ and $f(x)=x$. Both functions clearly satisfy $f(f(x^2)+yf(x))=xf(x)+xf(y)$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$. We now show that these are the only solutions. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion $f(f(x^2)+yf(x))=xf(x)+xf(y)$.

**Lemma 1.** Either $f\equiv0$, or $f(0)=0$ and $0$ is the unique zero of $f$.

*Proof.* Assertion $P(0,y)$ gives $f(f(0))=0$. Suppose that there exists some $a\neq0$ such that $f(a)=0$. Then $P(a,y)$ gives $f(f(a^2)+yf(a))=af(a)+af(y)$. Since $f(a)=0$, this becomes $f(f(a^2))=af(y)$ for every $y\in\mathbb{R}$. Taking $y=0$ gives $f(f(a^2))=0$, and therefore $af(y)=0$ for every $y\in\mathbb{R}$. Since $a\neq0$, we conclude that $f(y)=0$ for every $y\in\mathbb{R}$. Hence $f\equiv0$. Thus, for every nonzero solution, $f(x)=0$ implies $x=0$. Since $f(f(0))=0$, the uniqueness of the zero gives $f(0)=0$. Therefore, from now on, assume $f\not\equiv0$. Then $f(0)=0$ and $x\neq0$ implies $f(x)\neq0$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.** Every nonzero solution is odd.

*Proof.* Let $a=f(1)$. Since $1\neq0$, Lemma $1$ gives $a\neq0$. Setting $x=1$ in $P(x,y)$ gives $f(f(1)+yf(1))=f(1)+f(y)$, so.

$f(a+ay)=a+f(y)$. Taking $y=-1$ gives $f(0)=a+f(-1)$. Since $f(0)=0$, we obtain $f(-1)=-a$. Now set assertion $P(-1,y)$. Since $f(1)=a$ and $f(-1)=-a$, we obtain $f(a-ay)=a-f(y)$. On the other hand, assertion $P(-1,-y)$ yields $f(a-ay)=a+f(-y)$. Comparing the two expressions for $f(a-ay)$ gives $a-f(y)=a+f(-y)$, and therefore $f(-y)=-f(y)$ for every $y\in\mathbb{R}$. $\square$

**Lemma 3.** For every $x\in\mathbb{R}$, $f(x^2)=xf(x)$.

*Proof.* Set assertion $P(x,-x)$. By Lemma $2$, we get
$$
f(f(x^2)-xf(x))=xf(x)+xf(-x)=0.
$$
By Lemma $1$, $f(x^2)-xf(x)=0$, so $f(x^2)=xf(x)$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$. $\square$

**Lemma 4.** Every nonzero solution is idempotent for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$.

*Proof.* Setting $P(x,0)$ gives $f(f(x^2))=xf(x)$. By Lemma $3$, $xf(x)=f(x^2)$, so $f(f(x^2))=f(x^2)$. Since every nonnegative real number is of the form $x^2$, we have $f(f(t))=f(t)$ for every $t\geq0$. Now let $t<0$. Since $f$ is odd,
$$
f(f(t))=f(-f(-t))=-f(f(-t)).
$$
Because $-t>0$, the result already proved gives $f(f(-t))=f(-t)$. Therefore $f(f(t))=-f(-t)=f(t)$. The case $t=0$ is immediate because $f(0)=0$. Hence $f(f(x))=f(x)$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$. $\square$

**Lemma 5.** For every $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$, $f(f(x)(x+y))=x(f(x)+f(y))$.

*Proof.* By Lemma $3$, $f(x^2)=xf(x)$. Substituting this into $P(x,y)$ gives
$$
f(xf(x)+yf(x))=xf(x)+xf(y),
$$
where simply factoring proves the lemma. $\square$

**Lemma 6.** For every $x\in\mathbb{R}$, $f(x-f(x))=x-f(x)$.

*Proof.* The statement is immediate when $x=0$. Now let $x\neq0$ and put $a\neq0=f(x)$. By Lemma $4$, $f(a)=f(f(x))=f(x)=a$. Apply Lemma $5$ with $P(a,y)$. For every $u\in\mathbb{R}$, $f(a(a+u))=a(a+f(u))$. Choose $u=x+y-a$. Then $a+u=x+y$, so $f(a(x+y))=a(a+f(x+y-a))$. On the other hand, Lemma $5$ applied to $x$ gives $f(a(x+y))=x(a+f(y))$. Hence
$$
x(a+f(y))=a(a+f(x+y-a)).
$$
Now set $y=0$. Since $f(0)=0$, $xa=a(a+f(x-a))$. Because $a\neq0$, division by $a$ gives $x=a+f(x-a)$. Since $a=f(x)$, this becomes $f(x-f(x))=x-f(x)$.

.$\square$

**Lemma 7.** Every nonzero solution is the identity function.

*Proof.* Let $d=x-f(x)=f(d)$. Since $f(x)=x-d$, we can apply Lemma $3$ to obtain $f(x^2)=x(x-d)=x^2-xd$. Therefore $x^2-f(x^2)=xd$. By Lemma $6$ applied to $x^2$, $f(x^2-f(x^2))=x^2-f(x^2)$. Thus $f(xd)=xd$

for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$. Suppose there exists some $x_0\in\mathbb{R}$ such that $d_0=x_0-f(x_0)\neq0$. Then $f(xd_0)=xd_0$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$. Since $d_0\neq0$, every $r\in\mathbb{R}$ can be written as $r=xd_0$ for some $x\in\mathbb{R}$. Hence $f(r)=r$ for every $r\in\mathbb{R}$. Thus $f(x)=x$ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}$. If no such $x_0$ exists, then $x-f(x)=0$ for every $x$, so again $f(x)=x$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$. $\square$

Therefore the complete solution set is
$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0\text{ or }f(x)=x\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.}
$$
as desired. $\blacksquare$
