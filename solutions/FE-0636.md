Solution by **AIME4096**.
***

The zero function is a solution. We now assume $f$ is not identically zero and prove that $f(x)=x$ for all $x$.

Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(f(x^2)+yf(x))=xf(x)+xf(y).
$$

From $P(0,y)$, if $f(0)\ne0$, then $f$ vanishes everywhere, contrary to our assumption. Hence $f(0)=0$. If $f(a)=0$ for some $a\ne0$, then $P(a,y)$ makes $f(y)$ constant as $y$ varies, again impossible. Thus $0$ is the unique zero of $f$.

Let $a=f(1)$. From $P(1,y)$,

$$
f(a+ay)=a+f(y).
$$

Taking $y=-1$ gives $f(-1)=-a$. Comparing $P(-1,y)$ with $P(1,-y)$ gives $f(-y)=-f(y)$, so $f$ is odd.

Now $P(x,-x)$ gives

$$
f(f(x^2)-xf(x))=0,
$$

so

$$
f(x^2)=xf(x).
$$

Then $P(x,0)$ gives $f(f(x^2))=f(x^2)$, and oddness extends this to

$$
f(f(t))=f(t)\qquad\text{for all }t.
$$

The equation now becomes

$$
f(f(x)(x+y))=x(f(x)+f(y)).
$$

Fix $x\ne0$ and put $b=f(x)$. Since $f(b)=f(f(x))=b$, applying the last identity with $b$ in place of $x$ gives

$$
f(b(b+u))=b(b+f(u)).
$$

Take $u=x+y-b$. Then $b(b+u)=b(x+y)$, so this becomes

$$
f(b(x+y))=b(b+f(x+y-b)).
$$

On the other hand, the last identity with the original $x$ gives

$$
f(b(x+y))=x(b+f(y)).
$$

Comparing these and setting $y=0$, we get

$$
xb=b(b+f(x-b)).
$$

Since $b\ne0$, this yields

$$
f(x-f(x))=x-f(x).
$$

Next compare the transformed equation with $x=1$ and with $x=a=f(1)$. Since $f(a)=a$, the identities

$$
f(a(1+y))=a+f(y)
$$

and

$$
f(a(a+y))=a(a+f(y))
$$

can be compared by replacing $y$ with $a+y-1$ in the first one. This gives

$$
a+f(a+y-1)=a^2+af(y).
$$

Taking $y=0$ gives

$$
f(a-1)=a^2-a.
$$

But $f(1-a)=1-a$, so by oddness $f(a-1)=a-1$. Hence $(a-1)^2=0$, and $a=1$.

Thus $P(1,y)$ gives $f(y+1)=f(y)+1$. Replacing $y$ by $y+1$ in the transformed equation gives

$$
f(t+f(x))=f(t)+x
$$

for all $t$ and all $x\ne0$. Setting $t=0$ yields $f(f(x))=x$. Since we already know $f(f(x))=f(x)$, it follows that $f(x)=x$ for every $x\ne0$, and also for $x=0$.

Therefore the only solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x}.
$$
