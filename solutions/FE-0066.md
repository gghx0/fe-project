***
We claim the only solutions are $f(x)\equiv x$, $f(x)\equiv -x$, and $f(x)\equiv 0$, which obviously work. We now show they are the only solutions. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion that $f(x+f(x)f(y))=f(x)+yf(x)$. 
***
If $f(c)=0$ for some real constant $c$, then $P(x,c): cf(x)=0$. Hence, either $f$ is constantly 0, which is a solution, or $f$ is injective at $0$.

$P(x,-1): f(x+f(x)f(-1))=0$, hence $f(x)f(-1)+x$ is a constant. This shows that $f$ is linear, and by checking only $f(x)=0$, $f(x)=x$ and $f(x)=-x$ work.
