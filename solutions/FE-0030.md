Solution by **AIME4096**.
***

We claim that the complete solution set is the following. For a solution $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$, define

$$
H=\left\langle f(y)^2:y\in\mathbb{R}\right\rangle,
$$

the additive subgroup of $\mathbb{R}$ generated by all numbers $f(y)^2$. Then $f$ is characterized by the two properties

$$
f(x+h)=f(x)+h\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R},\ h\in H,
$$

and

$$
f(x)^2\in H\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

Equivalently, $f(x)-x$ is constant on every coset of $H$. Thus, if $C$ is a coset of $H$, there is a constant $c_C\in\mathbb{R}$ such that

$$
f(x)=x+c_C\qquad\forall x\in C,
$$

and necessarily

$$
(x+c_C)^2\in H\qquad\forall x\in C.
$$

Conversely, every function satisfying these conditions is a solution. We now show that the functions that satisfy these conditions are our only solutions.
Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion $f\bigl(x+f(y)^2\bigr)=f(x)+f(y)^2$. Suppose throughout the first part that $P(x,y)$ holds for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$.

**Lemma 1.** For every $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$, $f\bigl(x-f(y)^2\bigr)=f(x)-f(y)^2$.

*Proof.* Note that the assertion $P\bigl(x-f(y)^2,y\bigr)$ yields

$$
f(x)=f\bigl(x-f(y)^2\bigr)+f(y)^2,
$$

where subtracting $f(y)^2$ from both sides gives

$$
f\bigl(x-f(y)^2\bigr)=f(x)-f(y)^2.
$$

This proves the lemma. $\square$

**Lemma 2.** For every $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$ and every $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, $f\bigl(x+n f(y)^2\bigr)=f(x)+n f(y)^2$.

*Proof.* We proceed separately according to the sign of $n$. For $n=0$, $f(x)=f(x)+0$, so the statement is immediate. For $n>0$, we use induction on $n$. The case $n=1$ is exactly $P(x,y)$. Assume for some $n\geq1$ that

$$
f\bigl(x+n f(y)^2\bigr)=f(x)+n f(y)^2.
$$

Applying $P\bigl(x+n f(y)^2,y\bigr)$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
f\bigl(x+(n+1)f(y)^2\bigr)
&=f\bigl(x+n f(y)^2+f(y)^2\bigr)\\
&=f\bigl(x+n f(y)^2\bigr)+f(y)^2\\
&=f(x)+n f(y)^2+f(y)^2\\
&=f(x)+(n+1)f(y)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence the statement holds for every $n\geq0$. For $n<0$, write $n=-m$ with $m>0$. Lemma 1 gives the case $n=-1$, and repeating the same argument gives

$$
f\bigl(x-m f(y)^2\bigr)=f(x)-m f(y)^2.
$$

Therefore our lemma is proven for all $n\in\mathbb{Z}$ $\square$

**Lemma 3.** Let $H=\left\langle f(y)^2:y\in\mathbb{R}\right\rangle$. Then $f(x+h)=f(x)+h$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$ and every $h\in H$.

*Proof.* By definition of the additive subgroup generated by the set $\{f(y)^2:y\in\mathbb{R}\}$, every $h\in H$ has the form $h=\sum_{i=1}^n m_i f(y_i)^2$ for some $n\in\mathbb{N}$, some $m_1,\ldots,m_n\in\mathbb{Z}$, and some $y_1,\ldots,y_n\in\mathbb{R}$. We prove the required identity by repeatedly applying Lemma $2$. First,

$$
f\bigl(x+m_1f(y_1)^2\bigr)
=f(x)+m_1f(y_1)^2.
$$

Now apply Lemma $2$ again, this time with the starting point $x+m_1f(y_1)^2$. Such yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
f\bigl(x+m_1f(y_1)^2+m_2f(y_2)^2\bigr)
&=f\bigl(x+m_1f(y_1)^2\bigr)+m_2f(y_2)^2\\
&=f(x)+m_1f(y_1)^2+m_2f(y_2)^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Continuing in this manner through all $n$ terms gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x+h)
&=f\left(x+\sum_{i=1}^n m_i f(y_i)^2\right)\\
&=f(x)+\sum_{i=1}^n m_i f(y_i)^2\\
&=f(x)+h.
\end{aligned}
$$

This proves our lemma. $\square$

**Lemma 4.** The function $g(x)=f(x)-x$ is constant on every coset of $H$.

*Proof.* Let $x\in\mathbb{R}$ and $h\in H$. By Lemma $3$, $f(x+h)=f(x)+h$.
Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(x+h)
&=f(x+h)-(x+h)\\
&=f(x)+h-x-h\\
&=f(x)-x\\
&=g(x).
\end{aligned}
$$

Now suppose $x_1,x_2$ belong to the same coset of $H$. Then $x_2-x_1\in H$, so $g(x_2)=g(x_1+(x_2-x_1))=g(x_1)$. Thus our lemma is proven. $\square$

**Lemma 5.** For every coset $C\in\mathbb{R}/H$, there exists a constant $c_C\in\mathbb{R}$ such that $f(x)=x+c_C$ for every $x\in C$. Moreover, $(x+c_C)^2\in H$ for every $x\in C$.

*Proof.* By Lemma 4, $g(x)=f(x)-x$ is constant on $C$. Let this constant be $c_C$. Then $f(x)=x+c_C$. It remains to prove the second assertion. By the definition of $H$, every generator $f(z)^2,z\in\mathbb{R}$ belongs to $H$. Taking $z=x\in C$, we obtain $f(x)^2\in H$, where since $f(x)=x+c_C$, this becomes $(x+c_C)^2\in H$. Thus the lemma follows. $\square$

At this point, we have proved that every solution must have the claimed form. It remains to prove that every function of the claimed form actually works.

Fix arbitrary $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$. Taking $h-f(y)^2$ in the identity $f(x+h)=f(x)+h$ gives $f\bigl(x+f(y)^2\bigr)=f(x)+f(y)^2$. Thus $P(x,y)$ holds. Since $x$ and $y$ were arbitrary, the original functional equation holds for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$ and we are done. $\blacksquare$
