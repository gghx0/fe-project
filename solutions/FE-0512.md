Solution by **AIME4096**.
***

We claim that the complete solution set is $f\equiv0$ and  $f(x)=x\forall x\in\mathbb{R}$, both of which clearly satisfy the equation. We now prove that these are the only solutions. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion  $f(f(x)^2+xf(y))=f(x)^2+yf(x)$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$.

**Lemma 1.** We have $f(0)=0$.

*Proof.* Assertion $P(0,y)$ gives $f(f(0)^2)=f(0)^2+yf(0)$ for every $y\in\mathbb{R}$. The left-hand side and the first term on the right-hand side are independent of $y$, so necessarily $f(0)=0$. From now on, suppose that $f\not\equiv0$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.** The function $f$ is bijective.

*Proof.* Since $f\not\equiv0$, choose $a\in\mathbb{R}$ such that $f(a)\neq0$. Assertion $P(a,y)$ gives $f(f(a)^2+af(y))=f(a)^2+yf(a)$. As $y$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$, the right-hand side ranges over all of $\mathbb{R}$, since $f(a)\neq0$. Thus $f$ is surjective. Now suppose for the sake of contradiction that some $u,v\in\mathbb{R}$ exist such that $u\neq v$ and $f(u)=f(v)$. Applying $P(a,u)$ and $P(a,v)$ gives $f(f(a)^2+af(u))=f(a)^2+uf(a)$ and $f(f(a)^2+af(v))=f(a)^2+vf(a)$. The left-hand sides are equal because $f(u)=f(v)$, contradiction. Thus $f$ is injective, and hence bijective. $\square$

**Lemma 3.** We have $f(t)=t$ for every $t\geq0$.

*Proof.* Assertion $P(0,y)$ gives $f(f(x)^2)=f(x)^2$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$. Since $f$ is surjective, for every $t\in\mathbb{R}$ there exists $x\in\mathbb{R}$ such that $f(x)=t$. Therefore $f(t^2)=t^2$ for every $t\in\mathbb{R}$. Every nonnegative real number is a square, so $f(t)=t$ for every $t\geq0$. $\square$

**Lemma 4.** Every nonzero solution is the identity function.

*Proof.* Let $x\in\mathbb{R}$. If $x=0$, then $f(x)=0=x$ by Lemma $1$. Now suppose $x\neq0$. Since $f$ is injective and $f(0)=0$, we have $f(x)\neq0$. Choose $y>0$ such that $f(x)^2+xy\geq0$. Such a $y$ always exists. For example, if $x>0$, any $y>0$ works, while if $x<0$, it suffices to choose $0<y\leq -\frac{f(x)^2}{x}$. By Lemma $3$, since $y>0$, $f(y)=y$. Therefore $P(x,y)$ gives $f(f(x)^2+xy)=f(x)^2+yf(x)$. But the argument of $f$ on the left is nonnegative, so Lemma $3$ gives $f(f(x)^2+xy)=f(x)^2+xy$. Hence $f(x)^2+xy=f(x)^2+yf(x)$. Thus $xy=yf(x)$. Since $y>0$, we obtain $f(x)=x$. Therefore $f(x)=x$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$. $\square$

Hence the complete solution set is $f\equiv0$ and $f(x)=x\forall x\in\mathbb{R}$ as desired. $\blacksquare$
