Solution by **AIME4096**.
***

The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

First, $P(0,y)$ gives $f(0)=0$.

Assume now that $f\not\equiv0$, and choose $a$ with $f(a)\ne0$. From

$$
P(a,y):\quad f(f(a)^2+af(y))=f(a)^2+yf(a),
$$

the right side runs through all real values, so $f$ is surjective. If $f(u)=f(v)$, then comparing $P(a,u)$ and $P(a,v)$ gives $u=v$, so $f$ is injective.

Now $P(x,0)$ gives

$$
f(f(x)^2)=f(x)^2.
$$

Since $f$ is surjective, this implies

$$
f(t^2)=t^2
$$

for every real $t$, hence $f(t)=t$ for all $t\ge0$.

Fix $x\ne0$. Since $f$ is injective and $f(0)=0$, we have $f(x)\ne0$. Choose $y>0$ so that $f(x)^2+xy\ge0$. Then $f(y)=y$, and $P(x,y)$ gives

$$
f(f(x)^2+xy)=f(x)^2+yf(x).
$$

The input on the left is nonnegative, so the left side is $f(x)^2+xy$. Thus $xy=yf(x)$, and because $y>0$, $f(x)=x$. Hence every nonzero solution is the identity, and both listed functions work.
