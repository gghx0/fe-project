Find all functions $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ such that $f(f(x)^2+f(x)f(y))=xf(x)+xy$ for any reals $x,y$.

Let $P(x,y)$ denote the functional equation above.

Then, $P(0,y)$ gives us $f(f(0)^2+f(0)f(y))=0$, meaning that there exists $c$ so that $f(c)=0$. After that, $P(c,y)$ gives $cy=f(0)$ for all $y$. Hence, $c=f(0)=0$.

Because $P(1,y)$ gives us $f(f(1)^2+f(1)f(y))=y+f(1)$, we can easily see that $f(x)$ is bijective.

Hence, for all real number $z\neq 0$, there exists exactly one pair of $(x_0,y_0)$ so that $f(x_0)=z$ and $f(y_0)=-z$.

Then, $P(x_0,y_0)$ gives $x_0(z+y_0)=f(0)=0$.

However, because $f(x_0)=z\neq 0$, $x_0\neq 0$ which implies $z+y_0=0$ or $y_0=-z$.

Hence, for all $z \neq 0$, $f(-z)=-z$, which means for all $z \neq 0$, $f(z)=z$.

However, since $f(0)=0$, we see that $f(x)=x$ for all $x$ (which does satisfy the original functional equation).

To conclude, $f(x)=x$ for all $x$ is the only solution.
