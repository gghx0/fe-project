We claim the only solution is $f(x)\equiv cx$ for some real constant $c$, which obviously works. We now show it is the only solution. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion that $f(x+y^2)=f(x)+yf(y)$. 

***
Let $g(x)=f(x)-f(0)$

Subtracting $P(0,y)$ from $P(x,y)$, we get $g(x+y^2)=g(x)+g(y^2)$

So $g(x+y)=g(x)+g(y)$ $\forall x$ and $\forall y\ge 0$.

Hence, $0=g(0)=g(-|x|+|x|)=g(-|x|)+g(|x|)$ and $g(x)$ is odd, and so additive.

Back to original equation, we get $g(y^2)=yg(y)$. 

This means that $g(y^2+2y+1)=(y+1)g(y+1)$ which simplifies to $g(y)=g(1)y$

So, $f(x)=ax+b$ for some $a,b$. Plugging this in original equation, we get $b=0$, and so $\boxed{f(x)=ax\quad\forall x}$.
***
Note: $f(0)=0$ can also be proved from $P(-1,-1)$.
