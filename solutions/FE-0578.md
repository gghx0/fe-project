The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

Let $c=f(1)$. If $c=0$, then $P(1,y)$ gives $f(y)=0$ for all $y$, so $f\equiv0$. Now assume $f$ is nonconstant, so $c\ne0$.

From $P(1,y)$,

$$
f(y+c^2)=cf(y)+c. \tag{1}
$$

Next compare $P(x,y+c^2)$ with $cP(x,y)$. Since

$$
f(y+c^2)=cf(y)+c,
$$

we get, for $x\ne0$ and all $y$,

$$
f(c^2x+y)=cf(y)+(1-c)f(x^2)+cf(x). \tag{2}
$$

Putting $x=-1$ in (2), and using from (1) that $f(y-c^2)=\frac1c f(y)-1$, gives

$$
\frac1c f(y)-1=cf(y)+cf(-1)+c-c^2.
$$

Since $f$ is nonconstant, $c^2=1$.

Putting $x=2$ in (2), and using (1) twice, gives

$$
c^2f(y)+c^2+c=cf(y)+(1-c)f(4)+cf(2).
$$

Again $f$ is nonconstant, so $c^2=c$. Together with $c^2=1$, this yields $c=1$.

Now (2) becomes

$$
f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)\qquad(x\ne0),
$$

and the case $x=0$ is trivial, so $f$ is additive. Also $P(x,0)$ gives $f(f(x)^2)=f(x^2)$. Using additivity in the original equation, we get

$$
f(f(x)^2)+f(xy)=f(x^2)+f(x)f(y),
$$

so $f(xy)=f(x)f(y)$. A nonzero additive and multiplicative function $\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ with $f(1)=1$ is the identity. Hence $f(x)=x$.

Both listed functions are directly checked.
