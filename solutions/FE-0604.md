Let $P(x,y)$ be the given assertion $f(f(x^2) + f(y^2)) = x^2 + f(y^2).$

Comparing $P(x,y)$ and $P(y,x)$, we get $x^2+f(y^2) = y^2+f(x^2).$ So 
$$
f(x^2) = x^2 + C
$$
for some real constant $C.$ Plugging this into the given equation we get 
$$
f(x^2 + y^2 + 2C) = x^2 + y^2 + C. 
$$
Note that $f(x)=x+C$ for all $x \ge 0.$ There exist sufficiently large $x$ and $y$ such that $x^2+y^2+2C \ge 0$, and $x^2+y^2 + 3C = x^2+y^2+C$ for all such large $x$ and $y.$ This means $C = 0$, and therefore $f(x^2) = x^2.$ Plugging this into the given equation, we get 
$$
f(x^2+y^2) = x^2+y^2 
$$
As $f(x)=x$ for all $x \ge 0$, and $x^2+y^2 \ge 0$, this is true. So all $f$ that work are characterized by the form 
$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}x & x \ge 0 \\ g(x) & x < 0\end{cases} 
$$
where $g : (-\infty, 0) \to \mathbb R$ is an arbitrary function.
