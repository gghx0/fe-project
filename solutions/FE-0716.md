$f(2xf(y))=2yf(x)$

We claim that the solution set is $\boxed{f(x)=0}$ and

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }x=0\\\frac12\operatorname{sgn}(x)\exp(v_1(\ln\lvert2x\rvert)-v_2(\ln\lvert2x\rvert))&\text{if }x\ne0\end{cases}}
$$

where $(V_1,V_2)$ are supplementary subvectorspaces of the $\mathbb{Q}$-vectorspace $\mathbb{R}$ and $v_1:\mathbb{R}\to V_1$ and $v_2:\mathbb{R}\to V_2$ are the projections of a real in $(V_1,V_2)$.

$\boxed{f(x)=0}$ is a solution, otherwise there is some $j$ with $f(j)\ne0$ and by $P(j,x)$ $f$ is injective.

$P\left(\frac12,\frac12\right)\Rightarrow f\left(f\left(\frac12\right)\right)=f\left(\frac12\right)\Rightarrow f\left(\frac12\right)=\frac12$

$P\left(\frac12,x\right)\Rightarrow f(f(x))=x$

$P(x,f(y))\Rightarrow f(2xy)=2f(x)f(y)$

Let $g(x)=2f\left(\frac x2\right)$, this equation rewrites as $Q(x,y):g(xy)=g(x)g(y)$, and we can calculate that $g(g(x))=x$. This is well-known, we demonstrate a reduction to #6.

Note that $g$ is bijective and that $g\equiv1$ isn't a solution; if $g(\ell)\ne1$ then

$Q(\ell,0)\Rightarrow g(0)=0$

$Q(2j,1)\Rightarrow g(1)=1$

$Q(-1,-1)\Rightarrow g(-1)=-1$ (since $g(-1)=1$ contradicts $g(1)=1$)

$Q(-1,x)\Rightarrow g(-x)=-g(x)$

Since $g$ is bijective (since involutive), we have $g(x)\ne0$ for all $x>0$, so:

$Q(x,x)\Rightarrow g\left(x^2\right)=g(x)^2>0$ for all $x\ne0$, which implies $g(x)>0$ for all $x>0$

Therefore $h(x)=\ln\left(g\left(e^x\right)\right)$ is a function $\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$, and from the above properties we can calculate that $g(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)\exp(h(\ln\lvert x\rvert))$ for all $x\ne0$. Finally, $h(h(x))=x$ for all $x$ and $h(x+y)=h(x)+h(y)$, which is just #6. Solutions as above.
