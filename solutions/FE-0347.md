The solutions are

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
-x,&x\in S,\\
x,&x\notin S,
\end{cases}}
$$

where either $S=\emptyset$, or there is a constant $c\in(0,1)$ such that

$$
S\subseteq[-c,-c^2)\qquad\text{or}\qquad S\subseteq(-c,-c^2].
$$

These functions work. Indeed, $f(x)^2=x^2$, so it is enough to check

$$
f(xf(x)+y^2)=xf(x)+y^2.
$$

If $S\subseteq[-c,-c^2)$, then $xf(x)+y^2\ge -c^2$, hence the input is not in $S$. If $S\subseteq(-c,-c^2]$, then $xf(x)+y^2>-c^2$, again outside $S$.

We now prove that there are no other solutions. From $P(0,x)$,

$$
f(f(x)^2)=x^2,
$$

and hence $f(x^4)=f(x)^4$. Applying this at $x=0,1$ gives $f(0),f(1)\in\{0,1\}$. If $f(0)=f(1)$, then $P(x,0)$ and $P(x,1)$ contradict each other. The remaining possibility $f(0)=1,f(1)=0$ also contradicts the equation after comparing $P(0,xf(x)+1)$ and $P(f(x)^2,1)$. Thus

$$
f(0)=0,\qquad f(1)=1.
$$

Now $f(x)\ge0$ for $x\ge0$, and $P(1,f(x)^2)$ gives

$$
f(x+1)=f(x)+1\qquad(x\ge0).
$$

Using this shift and $f(x^4)=f(x)^4$, one obtains $f(x)\ge x$ for $x\ge0$ by applying the inequality to $x+n$ and letting $n\to\infty$. On the other hand,

$$
f(x)^2\le f(f(x)^2)=x^2
$$

for $x\ge0$, so $f(x)\le x$. Hence

$$
f(x)=x\qquad(x\ge0).
$$

Then $P(0,x)$ gives $f(x)^2=x^2$ for all $x$. If $u\le-1$ and $f(u)=-u$, then $u^2+u\ge0$, and

$$
P\left(u,\sqrt{u^2+u}\right)
$$

forces $f(u)=u$, impossible. Therefore $f(x)=x$ for all $x\le-1$.

It remains only to consider $x\in(-1,0)$. Let

$$
S=\{x\in(-1,0):f(x)=-x\}.
$$

If $S=\emptyset$, we have the identity solution. Otherwise set

$$
c=\sup_{x\in S}(-x).
$$

Then $c\in(0,1)$. Since $S\subseteq[-c,0)$, if some $u\in S$ satisfied $-c^2<u<0$, we could choose $v\in S$ with $v^2>-u$, and then

$$
P\left(v,\sqrt{u+v^2}\right)
$$

would force $f(u)=u$, contradiction. Thus

$$
S\subseteq[-c,-c^2].
$$

Finally, if both $-c$ and $-c^2$ belonged to $S$, then $P(-c,0)$ would force $f(-c^2)=-c^2$, contradiction. Hence at least one endpoint is absent, giving exactly the two interval alternatives above.
