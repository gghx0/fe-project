Find all functions $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ such that:

$$
f(xf(x)+yf(y))=x^2+f(y)^2
$$

for all $x,y\in\mathbb R$.

$P(x,0)\Rightarrow f(xf(x))=x^2+f(0)^2$.

$P(0,x)\Rightarrow f(xf(x))=f(x)^2$.

Comparing, we obtain $f(x)^2=x^2+f(0)^2$, so:

$$
\begin{align*}
x^4+f(0)^2x^2+f(0)^2&=x^2f(x)^2+f(0)^2\\
&=f(xf(x))^2\\
&=\left(x^2+f(0)^2\right)^2\\
&=x^4+2f(0)^2x^2+f(0)^4
\end{align*}
$$
which implies $f(0)=0$. Hence $f(x)^2=x^2+f(0)^2$ implies $f(x)\in\{-x,x\}$ for each $x$.

Suppose there are some $a,b\ne0$ with $f(a)=a$ and $f(b)=-b$.

$P(a,b)\Rightarrow f\left(a^2-b^2\right)=a^2+b^2$, if $f\left(a^2-b^2\right)=a^2-b^2$ we have $b=0$ whereas if $f\left(a^2-b^2\right)=b^2-a^2$ then $a=0$, contradiction.

Therefore we either have that for all $x\ne0$, $f(x)=x$, or that for all $x\ne0$, $f(x)=-x$. These give the solutions $\boxed{f(x)=-x}$ and $\boxed{f(x)=x}$ which fit.
