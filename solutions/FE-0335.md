Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

We claim that the only solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=-x}.
$$

Both are easily checked. Let $c=f(0)$. From $P(x,0)$ and $P(0,x)$,

$$
f(xf(x))=x^2+c^2
$$

and

$$
f(xf(x))=f(x)^2.
$$

Thus

$$
f(x)^2=x^2+c^2.
$$

Applying this to the input $xf(x)$ gives

$$
f(xf(x))^2=x^2f(x)^2+c^2=x^2(x^2+c^2)+c^2.
$$

But $f(xf(x))=x^2+c^2$, so

$$
(x^2+c^2)^2=x^2(x^2+c^2)+c^2
$$

for all $x$. Comparing coefficients forces $c=0$. Hence

$$
f(x)\in\{x,-x\}
$$

for every $x$.

If there were nonzero $a,b$ with $f(a)=a$ and $f(b)=-b$, then $P(a,b)$ would give

$$
f(a^2-b^2)=a^2+b^2.
$$

Since $f(t)=\pm t$, this is impossible unless $a=0$ or $b=0$. Therefore the sign is constant on all nonzero inputs, and together with $f(0)=0$ we get exactly

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=-x}.
$$
