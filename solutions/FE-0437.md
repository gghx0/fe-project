$$
f(xf(x)+xy)=x^2+f(x)f(y).
$$

$P(0,x)\Rightarrow f(0)=f(0)f(x)$, since there are no constant solutions we must have $f(0)=0$.

$P(1,0)\Rightarrow f(f(1))=1$.

$P(f(1),0)\Rightarrow f(1)^2=1$.

If $f(1)=-1$ then $P(1,1)\Rightarrow0=2$, so we have to have $f(1)=1$.

$P(1,x)\Rightarrow f(x+1)=f(x)+1$.

$P(x,y+1)\Rightarrow f(xf(x)+xy+x)=x^2+f(x)f(y)+f(x)=f(xf(x)+xy)+f(x)$.

For $x\ne0$, $xf(x)+xy$ can take any value and so $f(y+x)=f(y)+f(x)$ for all $x\ne0$. Since it's also true for $x=0$, $f$ is additive.

The equation now becomes:
$$
f(xf(x))+f(xy)=x^2+f(x)f(y).
$$
$P(x,0)\Rightarrow f(xf(x))=x^2$, so substituting this back into the equation, $f$ is multiplicative as well. Well-known that the only additive and multiplicative functions are $f(x)=0$ (which doesn't satisfy $P(x,y)$) and $\boxed{f(x)=x}$.
