Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

We characterize all solutions. Let $S$ be a nonempty subset of $\mathbb{R}$ with the closure property

$$
a,b\in S\implies a+b^2\in S.
$$

Then every solution is of the form

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\in S+S^2,\\
g(x),&x\notin S+S^2,
\end{cases}}
$$

where

$$
S+S^2=\{a+b^2:a,b\in S\}
$$

and $g:\mathbb{R}\setminus(S+S^2)\to S$ is arbitrary.

Indeed, if $f$ has this form, then $f(x),f(y)\in S$ for all $x,y$, so

$$
f(x)+f(y)^2\in S+S^2.
$$

Thus

$$
f(f(x)+f(y)^2)=f(x)+f(y)^2,
$$

as required.

Conversely, suppose $f$ satisfies

$$
f(f(x)+f(y)^2)=f(x)+f(y)^2.
$$

Let $S=f(\mathbb{R})$. Then $S$ is nonempty, and the equation shows that $f$ fixes every element of $S+S^2$. Also, since $S$ is the range of $f$, the values of $f$ outside $S+S^2$ must lie in $S$. Hence $f$ has exactly the form above.
