$$
f\left(f(x)+f(y)^2\right)=f(x)+f(y)^2.
$$

Let $\emptyset\ne S\subseteq\mathbb R$ be any set with the closure property that if $a,b\in S$ then $a+b^2\in S$, then all functions $f$ are characterized as:

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if }x\in S^2+S\\g(x)&\text{if }x\notin S^2+S\end{cases}}
$$

where $S^2+S=\left\{a^2+b\mid a,b\in S\right\}$ and $g:\mathbb R\setminus\left(S^2+S\right)\to S$ is any function.

Easy to verify that these functions work, and to show that all functions with $f\left(f(x)+f(y)^2\right)=f(x)+f(y)^2$ are of this form we choose $S=f(\mathbb R)$.

For instance with $S=[1,\infty)$ and $g(x)=x^2+1$ we get $f(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if }x\ge2\\x^2+1&\text{if }x<2\end{cases}$.
