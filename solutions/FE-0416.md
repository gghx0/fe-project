Solution by **isache**.
***

The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x},\qquad \boxed{f(x)=-x},\qquad \boxed{f(x)=1-x}.
$$

First, $f$ is injective: if $f(a)=f(b)$, comparing $P(x,a)$ and $P(x,b)$ for some $x$ with $f(x)\ne0$ gives $a=b$.

Suppose $f(0)=0$. From $P(x,0)$,

$$
f(xf(x))=x^2,
$$

and comparing $x$ with $-x$ gives $f(-x)=-f(x)$. Also $f(x)\ne0$ for $x\ne0$.

Putting $y=-x^2/f(x)$ in $P(x,y)$ gives

$$
f\left(\frac{x^2}{f(x)}\right)=x.
$$

Replacing $x$ by $f(x)$ and using injectivity yields

$$
xf(f(x))=f(x)^2. \tag{1}
$$

Also $P(1,0)$ gives $f(f(1))=1$, so $f(1)=\pm1$.

If $f(1)=1$, then

$$
f(f(x)+1)=x+1,\qquad f(f(x)-1)=x-1.
$$

Hence $f(x+2)=f(x)+2$. Then $P(2,x)$ gives $f(2f(x))=2x$, and using the shift by $2$ gives $f(x+1)=f(x)+1$. Therefore $f(f(x))=x$, and (1) gives $f(x)=\pm x$ pointwise. The shift $f(x+1)=f(x)+1$ rules out any nonzero point with $f(x)=-x$, so $f(x)=x$.

If $f(1)=-1$, the same argument gives

$$
f(x+1)=f(x)-1,
$$

and again $f(f(x))=x$, so $f(x)=\pm x$ pointwise. This shift forces $f(x)=-x$ for all $x$.

Now suppose $f(0)=c\ne0$. From $P(0,0)$ and $P(0,c^2)$, we get $c^3=c$, so $c=\pm1$, and $f(1)=0$. Then $P(1,0)$ forces $c=1$. Thus $P(0,x)$ gives

$$
f(f(x))=x.
$$

Comparing $P(x,1)$ and $P(f(x),1)$ gives

$$
f(x)^2-f(x)=x^2-x,
$$

so $f(x)\in\{x,1-x\}$ for every $x$. Since $f(0)=1$ and $f(1)=0$, the pair $\{0,1\}$ is swapped.

If $f(a)=a$ and $a\ne\frac12$, then $P(a,0)$ gives

$$
f(a^2+a)=a^2.
$$

As every value is either fixed or reflected by $x\mapsto 1-x$, this forces $a=-1$ or $a=\frac12$. Thus the only possible fixed point other than $\frac12$ is $-1$. But if $f(-1)=-1$, injectivity forces $f(2)=2$, and then applying the previous argument to $a=2$ is impossible. Hence no such $a$ exists, and $f(x)=1-x$ for every $x$.

All three listed functions are directly checked.
