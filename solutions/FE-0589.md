Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

We claim that the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=-x}.
$$

Both work. From $P(1,x)$,

$$
f(f(1)^2+f(x))=f(1)+x,
$$

so $f$ is bijective. From $P(0,0)$ and $P(f(0)^2+f(0),0)$, injectivity gives $f(0)=0$.

Let $h=f^{-1}$. For $u\ne0$, substitute $x=h(u)$ and choose $y$ so that $h(u)y=h(v)$. Then

$$
f(u^2+v)=uh(u)+h(v).
$$

Thus

$$
h(uh(u)+h(v))=u^2+v.
$$

Since $h(0)=0$, this also gives

$$
h(uh(u)+h(uv))=u^2+uv
$$

for all $u,v$. This is FE-0458 for $h$, whose solutions are $h(x)=x$ and $h(x)=-x$. Hence $f(x)=x$ or $f(x)=-x$.
