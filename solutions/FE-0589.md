$$
f\left(f(x)^2+f(xy)\right)=xf(x)+xy.
$$

$P(1,x)\Rightarrow f\left(f(1)^2+f(x)\right)=f(1)+x$ so $f$ is bijective.

$P(0,0)\Rightarrow f\left(f(0)^2+f(0)\right)=0$.

$P\left(f(0)^2+f(0),0\right)\Rightarrow f(f(0))=0$, so by injectivity $f\left(f(0)^2+f(0)\right)=f(f(0))$ implies $f(0)=0$.

So for all $x\ne0$, we have $f^{-1}(x)\ne0$ and so:

$P\left(f^{-1}(x),\frac{f^{-1}(y)}{f^{-1}(x)}\right)\Rightarrow f\left(x^2+y\right)=xf^{-1}(x)+f^{-1}(y)$, so $f^{-1}$ satisfies:

$$
f^{-1}\left(xf^{-1}(x)+f^{-1}(y)\right)=x^2+y
$$

for all $x\ne0$. Combined with the fact that $f^{-1}(0)=0$, we have:

$$
f^{-1}\left(xf^{-1}(x)+f^{-1}(xy)\right)=x^2+xy
$$

for all $x,y\in\mathbb R$, which by #458 has solutions $f^{-1}(x)=x$ and $f^{-1}(x)=-x$. These correspond to $\boxed{f(x)=-x}$ and $\boxed{f(x)=x}$ which do satisfy the original equation.
