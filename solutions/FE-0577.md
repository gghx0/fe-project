This is a trivial problem. 
***

We claim the only solutions are $f(x)\equiv x$, $f(x)\equiv -x$, and $f(x)\equiv 0$, which obviously work. We now show they are the only solutions.
***

It is easy to check that the only linear functions are $f(x)=x$, $f(x)=-x$, and $f(x)=0$.

We now prove a more general statement.
***
**Statement: Suppose $f,g,h:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ are functions such that for any reals $x,y$, $f(xy+g(x))=yf(x)+h(x)$. Show that $f$ is linear.**
***
_Proof._ Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion that $f(xy+g(x))=yf(x)+h(x)$.

$P(1,y-g(1)): f(y)=(y-g(1))\cdot f(1)+h(1)$, hence $f$ is linear as desired.
