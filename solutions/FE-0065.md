***
We claim the only solutions are $f(x)\equiv x$, $f(x)\equiv -x$ and $f(x)\equiv 0$, which obviously work. We now show they are the only solutions. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion that $f(x+f(x)f(y))=f(x)+xf(y)$. 
***
$P(-1,-1)$ gives $f(-1+f(-1)^2)=0$ so there exists $t$ such that $f(t)=0$.

$P(t,y)$ implies $f(t)=tf(y)$, so unless $f$ is identically 0 (which is a solution), $f(0)=0$ and $f$ is injective at $0$.

Hence, $f(-1)^2=1$.

***


From $P(1,-1)$, $ f(1+f(-1)f(1)) = f(1)+f(-1)$.

Now $P(-1,1+f(-1)f(1))$ gives $ f(-1+f(-1)f(-1)+f(-1)f(1)) = f(-1)-(f(1)+f(-1))$, simplify to get
$ f(f(-1)f(1)) =-f(1)$ (*)

Now $P(1,f(-1)f(1))$ gives $ f(1-f(1)f(1)) = 0$, so $ f(1)^{2}= 1$. This means that $ f(1) =\pm f(-1)$.

 Note that if $ f(-1) = f(1)$, then $ f(f(-1)f(1)) =-f(1)$ implies that $ f(1) =-f(1)$, contradicting $ f(1)^{2}= 1$. So $ f(1) =-f(-1)$.

***

Let us suppose that we have $ a$ such that $ f(a) = af(-1)$. From $P(-1,a)$,
$ f(-1+f(-1)f(-1)a) = f(-1)-f(-1)a$, or
$ f(a-1) = f(1)(a-1)$.
Now from $P(1,a-1)$, we get
$ f(1+f(1)f(1)(a-1)) = f(1)+f(1)(a-1)$, or
$ f(1+a-1) = f(1)+f(1)a-f(1)$, or
$ f(a) = af(1)$.
But $ f(a) = af(-1)$, so $ af(-1) = af(1)$ and $ a = 0$ (**).

***

From $P(x,-1)$, $ f(x+f(-1)f(x)) = f(x)+xf(-1)$. Observe that if $ a = x+f(-1)f(x)$, this is of the form $ f(a) = af(-1)$. Therefore by (**) $ a = 0$, so $ x+f(-1)f(x) = 0$. But then $ x =-f(-1)f(x)$, or $ x = f(1)f(x)$.

Since $ f(1)$ is its own reciprocal, $ f(x) = xf(1)$. Since $ f(1) = 1\text{ or }-1$, $ f(x) = x$ or $ f(x) =-x$. We also have the solution $ f(x) = 0$ that we assumed away earlier. These are the three answers, and we are done.
