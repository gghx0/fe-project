Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(f(x^2)+yf(x))=x^2+f(xy).
$$

If $f(k)=0$ for some $k\ne 0$, then $P(k,y)$ makes $f(ky)$ constant as $y$ varies, so $f$ would be constant on $\mathbb R$. No constant function satisfies the equation. Hence $f(k)=0$ implies $k=0$.

Let $a=f(0)$. From $P(x,0)$,

$$
f(f(x^2))=x^2+a,
$$

so $[a,\infty)\subseteq f(\mathbb R)$. For $x\ne 0$, we also have $f(x)\ne 0$, and substituting

$$
y=-\frac{f(x^2)}{f(x)}
$$

gives

$$
f\left(-\frac{xf(x^2)}{f(x)}\right)=a-x^2.
$$

Thus $(-\infty,a)\subseteq f(\mathbb R)$, so $f$ is surjective. Since $0$ is in the image and only $0$ can map to $0$, we get $f(0)=0$.

Suppose now that $f(k)\ne k$ for some $k$. Then $k\ne 0$. Set $h=f(k^2)$ and

$$
y=\frac{h}{k-f(k)}.
$$

The argument on the left of $P(k,y)$ is

$$
h+\frac{hf(k)}{k-f(k)}=\frac{kh}{k-f(k)}=ky.
$$

Therefore $P(k,y)$ gives $f(ky)=k^2+f(ky)$, impossible. Hence $f(k)=k$ for every $k$, and

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x}.
$$

Direct substitution verifies the solution.
