Solution by **megarnie**.
***

We claim that the only solutions are

$$
\boxed{f\equiv 0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad \boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

Both are checked directly. Now suppose $f$ is not identically zero, and let

$$
P(x,y):\qquad f(xf(y)+f(xy))=yf(x)+f(x)f(y).
$$

Also write

$$
g(x)=x+f(x).
$$

From $P(0,y)$,

$$
f(f(0))=f(0)f(y).
$$

If $f(0)\ne0$, then $f$ is constant, and the equation immediately forces this constant to be $0$, contradiction. Hence

$$
f(0)=0.
$$

Next we show that $f(1)\ne0$. Suppose instead that $f(1)=0$. From $P(1,y)$ and $P(x,1)$ we get

$$
f(2f(y))=0,\qquad f(f(x))=f(x). \tag{1}
$$

Choose $a$ with $f(a)\ne0$, and put $z=f(a)$. Then $f(z)=z$ and $z\ne0$, while $f(2z)=0$. Applying $P(x,2z)$ gives

$$
f(f(2xz))=2zf(x).
$$

Using (1), this becomes

$$
f(2xz)=2zf(x).
$$

Putting $x=\frac12$ yields $f(\frac12)=\frac12$. Then $P(2,\frac12)$ gives $f(2)=0$, while $P(\frac12,2)$ gives $0=1$, a contradiction. Thus

$$
f(1)\ne0.
$$

Now $f$ is injective. Indeed, if $f(c)=f(d)$, then comparing $P(1,c)$ and $P(1,d)$ gives

$$
f(1)(c+f(c))=f(1)(d+f(d)),
$$

so $c=d$.

The function $g$ is injective as well. If $g(c)=g(d)$, then $P(1,c)$ and $P(1,d)$ give

$$
f(2f(c))=f(2f(d)).
$$

By injectivity of $f$, we get $f(c)=f(d)$, hence $c=d$.

Finally, by $P(y,x)$ and $P(x,y)$,

$$
f(yf(x)+f(xy))=f(y)g(x),
$$

and

$$
f(xf(y)+f(xy))=f(x)g(y).
$$

Therefore

$$
g(yf(x)+f(xy))=yf(x)+f(xy)+f(y)g(x),
$$

and

$$
g(xf(y)+f(xy))=xf(y)+f(xy)+f(x)g(y).
$$

The two right-hand sides are equal, so injectivity of $g$ gives

$$
yf(x)+f(xy)=xf(y)+f(xy).
$$

Thus

$$
yf(x)=xf(y)
$$

for all real $x,y$. For nonzero $x,y$, this means $f(x)/x=f(y)/y$, so $f(x)=cx$ for all $x$, with $f(0)=0$ already known.

Substituting $f(x)=cx$ into the original equation gives

$$
2c^2xy=cxy+c^2xy,
$$

so $c^2=c$. Hence $c=0$ or $c=1$, giving exactly the two listed solutions.
