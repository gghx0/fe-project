Solution by **TYT**, **maromex** and **gghx**.
***

We claim the only solutions are $f(x)\equiv x$ and $f(x)\equiv 0$, which obviously work. We now show they are the only solutions. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion that $f(x+f(y^2))=f(x)+yf(y)$.
***
**Claim 1.** $f(-y) = -f(y)$ for all $y ≠ 0$.

_Proof._ Comparing $P(x, y)$ and $P(x, -y)$ we get $yf(y) = -yf(-y) \,\forall y \in \mathbb{R}$. So if $y \neq 0$ then $f(-y) = -f(y)$.
***
**Claim 2.** $f(0) = 0$.

_Proof._ Let $a = f(0)$.
$P(0, 0) \Rightarrow f(a) = a$.
$P(-a, 0) \Rightarrow f(-a) = a$.
If $a \neq 0$ then from **Claim 1** $-a = -f(a) = f(-a) = a$ so $a = 0$, contradiction. Hence $f(0) = a = 0$.
***
**Claim 3.** $f$ is an odd function.

_Proof._ Immediate consequence of **Claim 1** and **Claim 2**.
***
**Claim 4.** $f(f(y^2)) = yf(y)$ for all $y \in \mathbb{R}$.

_Proof._ $P(0, y)$ implies $f(f(y^2)) = f(0) + yf(y) = yf(y)$ for all $y \in \mathbb{R}$.
***
**Claim 5.** $f(x + f(y)) = f(x) + f(f(y))$ for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$.

_Proof._ $P(x, y)$ and **Claim 4** give
$$
    f(x + f(y^2)) = f(x) + f(f(y^2)) \,\forall x, y \in \mathbb{R} \quad (1)
$$
Replacing $x$ by $x - f(y^2)$ in $(1)$ yields
$$
    f(x - f(y^2)) = f(x) - f(f(y^2)) \,\forall x, y \in \mathbb{R} \quad (2)
$$
Since $f$ is odd, $-f(y^2) = f(-y^2)$ and $-f(f(y^2)) = f(-f(y^2)) = f(f(-y^2))$. Therefore $(2)$ implies
$$
    f(x + f(-y^2)) = f(x) + f(f(-y^2)) \,\forall x, y \in \mathbb{R} \quad (3)
$$
From $(1)$ and $(3)$ we conclude $f(x + f(y)) = f(x) + f(f(y))$ for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$.
***
**Claim 6.** $f(1) = 0$ or $f(1) = 1$.

_Proof._ Let $b = f(1)$. Putting $y = 1$ in **Claim 4** yields $f(b) = b$. Hence putting $y = b$ in **Claim 5** yields $f(x + b) = f(x) + b \,\forall x \in \mathbb{R}$. By induction $f(x + nb) = f(x) + nb \,\forall x \in \mathbb{R}, \forall n \in \mathbb{N}$. Moreover $f(1 + b) = f(1) + b = 2b$ and $f(1) = f(1 - b) + b$, i.e $f(1 - b) = 0$.
Thus
$$
    f(f((1 + b)^2)) = f(f((1 - b)^2 + 4b)) = f(f((1 - b)^2) + 4b) = f(f((1 - b)^2)) + 4b \quad (4)
$$
From **Claim 4** we get $f(f((1 + b)^2)) = (b + 1)f(b + 1) = 2b(b + 1)$ and $f(f((1 - b)^2)) = (1 - b)f(1 - b) = 0$. As a result we obtain from $(4)$ that
$$
    2b(b + 1) = 4b,
$$
which means $b = 0$ or $b = 1$.
***
Let $S := \{ s \in \mathbb{R} \mid f(x + s) = f(x) + f(s) \,\forall x \in \mathbb{R}\}$ then

**Claim 7.**

$1.$ $S$ is closed under negation, addition and subtraction.

$2.$ $f(y) \in S$ for all $y \in \mathbb{R}$.

$3.$ $yf(y) \in S$ for all $y \in \mathbb{R}$.

$4.$ $z^2yf(y) - y^2zf(z) \in S$ for all $y, z \in \mathbb{R}$.

_Proof._

$1.$ If $s, t \in S$ then $f(s + t) = f(s) + f(t)$ and for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$, $f(x + s + t) = f(x + s) + f(t) = f(x) + f(s) + f(t) = f(x) + f(s + t)$. Hence $s + t \in S$. Moreover since $t \in S$ we get $f(x) = f(x - t) + f(t) \,\forall x \in \mathbb{R}$ so $f(x + (-t)) = f(x) + f(-t) \,\forall x \in \mathbb{R}$. This means $-t \in S$. Thus $s - t = s + (-t) \in S$.

$2.$ Immediate by **Claim 5**.

$3.$ From $P(x, y)$ we get $yf(y) = f(x + f(y^2)) - f(x) \in S$ because $f(x), f(x + f(y^2)) \in S$ (by item $2$) and $S$ is closed under subtraction.

$4.$ From $P(0, y + f(z^2))$ and $P(y, z)$ we obtain
$$
    f(f((y + f(z^2))^2)) = [y + f(z^2)][f(y) + zf(z)] = yf(y) + yzf(z) + f(y)f(z^2) + zf(z)f(z^2).
$$
Note that $zf(z)f(z^2) = f(z^2)f(f(z^2)) \in S$. Hence taking out all the terms that are known to be in $S$ we get
$$
    yzf(z) + f(y)f(z^2) = f(f((y + f(z^2))^2)) - yf(y) - zf(z)f(z^2) \in S \,\forall y, z \in \mathbb{R} \quad (5)
$$
Replacing $y$ by $y^2$ in $(5)$ gives
$$
    y^2zf(z) + f(y^2)f(z^2) \in S \,\forall y, z \in \mathbb{R} \quad (6)
$$
Swapping $y$ and $z$ in $(6)$ gives
$$
    z^2yf(y) + f(z^2)f(y^2) \in S \forall y, z \in \mathbb{R} \quad (7)
$$
From $(6)$ and $(7)$
$$
    z^2yf(y) - y^2zf(z) = [z^2yf(y) + f(z^2)f(y^2)] - [y^2zf(z) + f(y^2)f(z^2)] \in S \,\forall y, z \in \mathbb{R}
$$
***
**Claim 8.** If $S = \mathbb{R}$ then $f(x) = bx \,\forall x \in \mathbb{R}$ where $b = f(1) \in \{0, 1\}$.

_Proof._ So $f$ is additive over $\mathbb{R}$. Replacing $y$ by $y + 1$ in **Claim 4** we get
$$
\begin{align*}
    f(f((y + 1)^2)) = (y + 1)f(y + 1) &\Rightarrow f(f(y^2 + 2y + 1)) = (y + 1)[f(y) + b] \\
    &\Rightarrow f(f(y^2)) + 2f(f(y)) + b = (y + 1)[f(y) + b] \\
    &\Rightarrow 2f(f(y)) = by + f(y) \,\forall y \in \mathbb{R} \quad (8)
\end{align*}
$$
Replacing $y$ by $y^2$ in $(8)$ we get
$$
    by^2 + f(y^2) = 2f(f(y^2)) = 2yf(y) \,\forall y \in \mathbb{R} \quad (9)
$$
Since $b \in \{0, 1\}, f(by^2) = bf(y^2) \,\forall y \in \mathbb{R}$. Thus $P(by^2, y)$ implies
$$
\begin{align*}
    f(by^2 + f(y^2)) = f(by^2) + yf(y) &\Rightarrow f(2yf(y)) = bf(y^2) + yf(y) \\
    &\Rightarrow 2f(yf(y)) = b[2yf(y) - by^2] + yf(y) \\
    &\Rightarrow 2f(yf(y)) = (2b + 1)yf(y) - b^2y^2 \,\forall y \in \mathbb{R} \quad (10)
\end{align*}
$$
For $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$ we compute $f((x + y)^2)$ in two ways
$$
\begin{align*}
    f((x + y)^2) &= 2(x + y)f(x + y) - b(x + y)^2 = 2(x + y)[f(x) + f(y)] - b(x + y)^2 \quad (11) \\
    f((x + y)^2) &= f(x^2 + 2xy + y^2) = f(x^2) + f(y^2) + 2f(xy) = [2xf(x) - bx^2] + [2yf(y) - by^2] + 2f(xy) \quad (12)
\end{align*}
$$
Comparing $(11)$ and $(12)$ we obtain $f(xy) + bxy = xf(y) + yf(x) \,\forall x, y \in \mathbb{R}$. In particular for $x = f(y)$ we get
$$
\begin{align*}
    f(yf(y)) + byf(y) = f(y)^2 + yf(f(y)) &\Rightarrow \frac{(2b + 1)yf(y) - b^2y^2}{2} + byf(y) = f(y)^2 + y\cdot\frac{f(y) + by}{2} \\
    &\Rightarrow 4byf(y) = 2f(y)^2 + (b^2 + b)y^2 \\
    &\Rightarrow 4byf(y) = 2f(y)^2 + 2b^2y^2 \quad (b^2 = b \text{ because } b \in \{0, 1\}) \\
    &\Rightarrow 2[f(y) - by]^2 = 0 \\
    &\Rightarrow f(y) = by \,\forall y \in \mathbb{R},
\end{align*}
$$
as claimed.
***
**Claim 9.** If $f(1) = 1$ then $f(x) = x \,\forall x \in \mathbb{R}$.

_Proof._ First, $f(x + 1) = f(x) + f(1) = f(x) + 1 \,\forall x \in \mathbb{R}$ so $1 \in S$. Take $y \in \mathbb{R}$. It follows from **Claim 7** that $(y + 1)[f(y) + 1] = (y + 1)f(y + 1) \in S$. Thus as $S$ is closed under subtraction, $y = (y + 1)[f(y) + 1] - yf(y) - f(y) - 1 \in S$. We conclude that $S = \mathbb{R}$. Thus by **Claim 8**, $f(x) = xf(1) = x$ for all $x\in \mathbb{R}$.
***
**Claim 10.** If $f(1) = 0$ then $f \equiv 0$.

_Proof._ Consider arbitrary $u, v \neq 0$ such that $u^2, v^2 \in S$. Applying item $4$ of **Claim 7** we get, for all $y, z \in \mathbb{R}$
$$
\begin{align*}
    (y + u^2)^2zf(z) - z^2(y + u^2)f(y + u^2) \in S &\Rightarrow (y^2 + 2u^2y + u^4)zf(z) - z^2(y + u^2)[f(y) + f(y^2)] \in S \\
    &\Rightarrow [y^2zf(z) - z^2yf(y)] + [u^4zf(z) - z^2u^2f(u^2)] + 2u^2yzf(z) - u^2z^2f(y) - z^2yf(u^2) \in S
\end{align*}
$$
Since $y^2zf(z) - z^2yf(y) \in S$ and $u^4zf(z) - z^2u^2f(u^2) \in S$, subtraction shows
$$
    2u^2yzf(z) - u^2z^2f(y) - z^2yf(u^2) \in S \,\forall y, z \in \mathbb{R} \quad (13)
$$
Replacing $z$ by $vz$ in $(13)$ yields
$$
    2u^2v yz f(vz) - u^2v^2z^2f(y) - v^2f(u^2)z^2y \in S \,\forall y, z \in \mathbb{R} \quad (14)
$$
Since the roles of $u$ and $v$ are symmetric, swapping $u$ and $v$ in $(14)$ yields
$$
    2uv^2yzf(uz) - u^2v^2z^2f(y) - u^2f(v^2)z^2y \in S \,\forall y, z \in \mathbb{R} \quad (15)
$$
Subtracting the terms in $(14)$ and $(15)$ gives
$$
    2uv^2yz f(uz) - 2u^2vyzf(vz) - [u^2f(v^2) - v^2f(u^2)]yz^2 \in S \,\forall y, z \in \mathbb{R},
$$
or $z \alpha_{u, v}(z)y \in S \,\forall y, z \in \mathbb{R}$ where $\alpha_{u, v}(z) := 2uv^2f(uz) - 2u^2vf(vz) - [u^2f(v^2) - v^2f(u^2)]z$.

If there exists $z^* \neq 0$ such that $z^*\alpha_{u, v}(z^*) \neq 0$ then $z^*\alpha_{u, v}(z^*)y$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$ when $y$ varies over $\mathbb{R}$. Thus $S = \mathbb{R}$ so by **Claim 8**, $f \equiv 0$. But this contradicts $z^*\alpha_{u, v}(z^*) \neq 0$.

***

Thus $z\alpha_{u, v}(z) = 0 \,\forall z \neq 0$ so $\alpha_{u, v}(z) = 0 \,\forall z \neq 0$. Since $\alpha_{u, v}(0) = 0$, we get
$$
    2uv^2f(uz) - 2u^2vf(vz) - [u^2f(v^2) - v^2f(u^2)]z = 0 \,\forall z \in \mathbb{R} \quad (16)
$$
Replacing $z$ by $\frac{z}{v}$ in $(16)$ gives
$$
    2uv^2f\left(\frac{u}{v}z\right) - 2u^2vf(z) - \frac{u^2f(v^2) - v^2f(u^2)}{v}z = 0 \,\forall z \in \mathbb{R},
$$
or
$$
    f\left(\frac{u}{v}z\right)- \frac{u}{v}f(z) - \frac{u^2f(v^2) - v^2f(u^2)}{2uv^3}z = 0 \,\forall z \in \mathbb{R},
$$
or, by letting $c := \frac{u}{v} \neq 0$ and $d := \frac{u^2f(v^2) - v^2f(u^2)}{2uv^3}$
$$
    f(cz) = cf(z) + dz \,\forall z \in \mathbb{R} \quad (17)
$$
Consequently
$$
    f(c^2z) = cf(cz) + cdz = c[cf(z) + dz] + cdz = c^2f(z) + 2cdz \,\forall z \in \mathbb{R} \quad (18)
$$
$P(c^2x, cy)$ together with $(18)$ give
$$
\begin{align*}
    f(c^2x + f(c^2y^2)) = f(c^2x) + cyf(cy) &\Rightarrow f(c^2x + c^2f(y^2) + 2cdy^2) = [c^2f(x) + 2cdx] + cy[cf(y) + dy] \\
    &\Rightarrow f(c^2x + c^2f(y^2) + 2cdy^2) = c^2[f(x) + yf(y)] + 2cdx + cdy^2 \,\forall x, y \in \mathbb{R} \quad (19)
\end{align*}
$$
Also
$$
\begin{align*}
    f(c^2x + c^2f(y^2)) = c^2f(x + f(y^2)) + 2cd[x + f(y^2)] = c^2[f(x) + yf(y)] + 2cdx + 2cdf(y^2) \,\forall x, y \in \mathbb{R} \quad (20)
\end{align*}
$$
Subtracting $(20)$ from $(19)$ we get
$$
    f(c^2x + c^2f(y^2) + 2cdy^2) - f(c^2x + c^2f(y^2)) = cd[y - f(y^2)] \,\forall x, y \in \mathbb{R} \quad (21)
$$
Putting $x = -f(y^2)$ in $(21)$ gives $cd[y - f(y^2)] = f(2cdy^2)$ so $(21)$ becomes
$$
    f(c^2x + c^2f(y^2) + 2cdy^2) - f(c^2x + c^2f(y^2)) = f(2cdy^2) \,\forall x, y \in \mathbb{R} \quad (22)
$$
For each fixed $y \in \mathbb R$ it can be observed that $w := c^2x + c^2f(y^2)$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}$ as $x$ varies over $\mathbb{R}$. Hence $(22)$ implies
$$
    f(w + 2cdy^2) = f(w) + f(2cdy^2) \,\forall w \in \mathbb{R}
$$
so $2cdy^2 \in S$ by the definition of $S$. Thus
$$
2cdy^2 \in S \,\forall y \in \mathbb{R}
$$
If $d \neq 0$ then $2cdy^2$ ranges over $\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}$ (or $\mathbb{R}_{\leqslant 0}$) when $y$ varies over $\mathbb{R}$. Since $S$ is closed under negation, we conclude that $S = \mathbb{R}$. So again by **Claim 8**, $f \equiv 0$. But then $d = \frac{u^2f(v^2) - v^2f(u^2)}{2uv^3} = 0$, a contradiction.
Thus $d = 0$ and so $f(cz) = cf(z) \,\forall z \in \mathbb{R}$. In particular $f(c) = cf(1) = 0$ and $f(c^2) = cf(c) = 0$, which means $f\left( \frac{u^2}{v^2} \right) = 0$.
***
Thus for all $s, t \in S$ such that $s, t > 0$
$$
    f\left(\frac{s}{t}\right) = 0
$$
Owing to the facts $0 \in S$, $f(0) = 0$, $f$ is odd and $S$ is closed under negation we conclude that
$$
    f\left(\frac{s}{t}\right) = 0
$$
for all $s, t \in S$ where $t \neq 0$.

Now suppose that $f(y) \neq 0$ for some $y \in \mathbb{R}$ then from **Claim 4**
$$
    f(y) = f\left(\frac{f(f(y^2))}{f(y)}\right) = 0
$$
since $f(y), f(f(y^2)) \in S$. But this is a contradiction. So indeed it must be the case that $f \equiv 0$.
