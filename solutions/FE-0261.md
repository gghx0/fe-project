This is a trivial problem. 
***

We claim the only solutions are $f(x)\equiv x$ and $f(x)\equiv -x$, which obviously work. We now show they are the only solutions.
***

It is easy to check that the only linear functions are $f(x)=x$ and $f(x)=-x$.

We now prove a more general statement.
***
**Statement: Suppose $f,g,h:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ are functions such that for any reals $x,y$, $f(yf(x)+g(x))=xy+h(x)$. Show that $f$ is linear.**
***
_Proof._ Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion that $f(yf(x)+g(x))=xy+h(x)$.

Obviously, $f$ cannot be constantly $0$. Hence, there exists some real constant $u$ such that $f(u)\ne 0$.

Now $P(u,\frac{y-g(u)}{f(u)}):f(y)=u\cdot \frac{y-g(u)}{f(u)}+h(u)$ which is linear, as desired.
