As $xy$ can take any real value, this is equivalent to 
$$
f(2x) = 2f(x)
$$ 
for any real $x.$

Let $g: [1,2) \cup (-2,1] \to \mathbb R$ be any function, let $L(x) = 2^{\lfloor \log_2(|x|) \rfloor}$ for all nonzero real $x$, and let 
$$
f(x) = \begin{cases}0 & x=0 \\ L(x)g\left(\frac{x}{L(x)}\right) & x \neq 0.\end{cases}
$$
We claim that this form characterizes all $f$ that work.

First, $f$ is of this form because $f(2 \cdot 0) = 2f(0) \implies f(0)=0$, and we can show that $f(x) = L(x)f\left(\frac{x}{L(x)}\right)$ for all nonzero real $x$ by induction on the integer $\log_2(L(x)).$ The base case $\log_2(L(x)) = 0$ is trivial, and the induction step can be easily checked with $f(2x)=2f(x).$ Note that $\frac{x}{L(x)} \in [1,2) \cup (-2,1]$ for all nonzereo real $x.$

Now we show that all $f$ of this form work. The case $x=0$ is clear. For nonzero $x$, note that $L(2x) = 2L(x)$ and $\frac{x}{L(x)} = \frac{2x}{L(2x)}$, so $L(2x)g\left(\frac{2x}{L(2x)}\right)=f(2x) = 2f(x) = L(x)g\left(\frac{x}{L(x)}\right).$
