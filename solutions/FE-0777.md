The answer is $f(x)\equiv x$ and $f(x)\equiv-x$.

Claim. $f$ is injective. 

Proof. Suppose that $f(y_1)=f(y_2)=t$. Then $P(1,y_1)$ gives $f(t+t)=f(1)t+y_1$ and $P(1,y_2)$ gives $f(t+t)=f(1)t+y_2$, so $y_1=y_2$. $\square$ 

Note that $P(y,x)$ gives $f(yf(x)+f(yx))=f(y)f(x)+yx=f(x)f(y)+xy=f(xf(y)+f(xy))$. Since $f$ is injective, we obtain $yf(x)+f(yx)=xf(y)+f(xy)$ or $yf(x)=xf(y)$. Taking $y=1$, we get $f(x)=xf(1)=xa$ (here $a=f(1)$) for all real numbers $x$.

Now we have $a(xay+axy)=axay+xy$, i.e. $2a^2=a^2+1$, so $a=\pm1$.
