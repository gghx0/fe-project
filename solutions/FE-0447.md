Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(xf(x)+xy)=f(x)^2+f(x)f(y).
$$

The functions

$$
f(x)\equiv0,\qquad f(x)\equiv\frac12,\qquad f(x)=x
$$

all work. We prove these are the only ones.

Taking $P(0,y)$ gives

$$
f(0)=f(0)^2+f(0)f(y).
$$

If $f(0)\ne0$, then $f(y)$ is constant, and this gives $f\equiv\frac12$. Hence, apart from the constant solutions, we may assume

$$
f(0)=0.
$$

If $f(c)=0$, then $P(c,y)$ gives

$$
f(cy)=0
$$

for every real $y$. In the nonconstant case this forces $c=0$, so $0$ has the unique preimage $0$.

Now $P(x,-f(x))$ gives

$$
0=f(x)\bigl(f(x)+f(-f(x))\bigr).
$$

Thus, whenever $f(x)\ne0$,

$$
f(-f(x))=-f(x). \tag{1}
$$

For $f(x)=0$, the same equality follows from $f(0)=0$, so (1) holds for every $x$.

Finally, applying $P(-f(y),y)$ and using (1) gives

$$
f\bigl(-yf(y)+f(y)^2\bigr)=0.
$$

By the uniqueness of the zero,

$$
f(y)^2=yf(y)
$$

for every $y$. Hence either $f(y)=0$, which implies $y=0$, or $f(y)=y$. Therefore $f(y)=y$ for all real $y$.

So the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0,\qquad f(x)\equiv\frac12,\qquad f(x)=x}.
$$
