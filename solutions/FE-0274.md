Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(x^2+f(x)f(y))=x^2+yf(x).
$$

Clearly $f(x)=x$ works. We prove it is the only solution.

First,

$$
P(0,0):\quad f(f(0)^2)=0.
$$

Then

$$
P(0,f(0)^2):\quad f(0)=f(0)^3,
$$

so $f(0)^2=f(0)^4$. Also,

$$
P(f(0)^2,y):\quad f(f(0)^4)=f(0)^4.
$$

Thus

$$
f(0)^4=f(f(0)^4)=f(f(0)^2)=0,
$$

so $f(0)=0$.

Now $P(x,0)$ gives

$$
f(x^2)=x^2.
$$

Hence $f(t)=t$ for every $t\ge0$.

Fix $x>0$. If $f(y)\ge0$, then $x^2+xf(y)>0$, and therefore

$$
x^2+xf(y)=f(x^2+xf(y))=x^2+yf(x)=x^2+xy.
$$

Thus $f(y)=y$. In particular, if $y<0$, then $f(y)<0$.

Now fix $y<0$. Choose $x>-f(y)$ with $x>0$. Then $x^2+xf(y)>0$, so again

$$
x^2+xf(y)=f(x^2+xf(y))=x^2+xy.
$$

Thus $f(y)=y$ for every $y<0$. Together with the nonnegative case,

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb R}.
$$
