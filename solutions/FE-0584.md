Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

We claim that the unique solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

From $P(1,x)$,

$$
f(f(1)^2+f(x))=1+x,
$$

so $f$ is bijective. Also $P(0,0)$ and then $P(f(0)^2+f(0),1)$ give

$$
f(0)=0.
$$

From $P(x,0)$,

$$
f(f(x)^2)=x^2.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
f^{-1}(u)^2=f(u^2).
$$

For $u\ne0$, put $x=f^{-1}(u)$ and choose $y$ so that $xy=f(v)^2$. Then the original equation gives

$$
f(u^2+v^2)=f(u^2)+f(v)^2.
$$

By FE-0183, and since $f\not\equiv0$, this implies $f(t)=t$ for all $t\ge0$ and $f(t)\in\{t,-t\}$ for all $t<0$.

If some $k<0$ had $f(k)=-k$, then $f(k)=f(-k)$, contradicting injectivity. Therefore every negative input is fixed as well, and

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$
