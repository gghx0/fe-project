Solution by **IcaRoNek0**.
***

All solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0},
$$

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x+c\qquad(x\in\mathbb R,\ c\in\mathbb R)},
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x-1,&x\ne0,\\
a,&x=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad a\in\mathbb R.}
$$

Put $F(x)=1+f(x)$. The equation becomes

$$
F(xF(y))=F(x)+xF(y)-x. \tag{1}
$$

If $F(y_0)=0$ for some $y_0$, then (1) gives $F(x)=x+F(0)$, which is the second family.

Now suppose $F$ never vanishes. Let

$$
S=F(\mathbb R),\qquad c(x)=F(x)-x.
$$

Then $0\notin S$, and every affine map

$$
T_x(t)=xt+c(x)
$$

maps $S$ into $S$.

We first show that $S$ has no nonzero additive period. The map $T_{-1}$ is a reflection, so it maps $S$ onto itself. Hence if $S+r\subseteq S$, then also $S-r\subseteq S$, and consequently $S+r=S$. Let $P$ be the additive subgroup of periods of $S$.

For $x\ne0$, the composition $T_xT_{1/x}$ is a translation; hence its translation amount $q_x$ lies in $P$. If $p\in P$, then

$$
T_x\circ(t\mapsto t+p)\circ T_{1/x}
$$

is translation by $q_x+xp$ and maps $S$ into itself, so $xp\in P$. A nonzero $p$ would force $P=\mathbb R$, and then $S=\mathbb R$, contradicting $0\notin S$. Thus $P=\{0\}$.

So $q_x=0$, and each $T_x$ with $x\ne0$ is a bijection of $S$. The maps $T_xT_{-1}$ and $T_{-1}T_x$ have the same slope; composing one with the inverse of the other gives a translation by

$$
2c(x)+(x-1)c(-1),
$$

which must be zero. If $k=c(-1)/2$, then

$$
c(x)=k(1-x),\qquad F(x)=(1-k)x+k\qquad(x\ne0).
$$

If $k\notin\{0,1\}$, this expression vanishes at a nonzero $x$, impossible.

If $k=0$, then $F(x)=x$ for $x\ne0$, giving the third family. If $k=1$, then $F(x)=1$ for $x\ne0$, and (1) with $x=1,y=0$ gives $F(0)=1$, hence $f\equiv0$.

Direct substitution verifies all three families.
