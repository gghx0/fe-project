Since $xy$ ranges over all real numbers, the original equation is equivalent to solving

$$
f\bigl(x+f(x)\bigr)=2f(x)\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.
\tag{1}
$$

Find all functions $f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfying (1), with no assumption on $f$ (in particular, no continuity).

**The Key Invariant**.

For a function $f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ put

$$
T(x)=x+f(x),\qquad a(x)=x-f(x),
$$

so that (1) reads $f(T(x))=2f(x)$.

**Lemma 1.**

If $f$ satisfies (1), then $a(T(x))=a(x)$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$.

Consequently, for all $n\ge 0$,

$$
f\bigl(T^n(x)\bigr)=2^n f(x),\qquad
T^n(x)=a(x)+2^n\bigl(x-a(x)\bigr).
$$

*Proof.*

We compute

$$
a(T(x))=T(x)-f(T(x))=x+f(x)-2f(x)=x-f(x)=a(x).
$$

Applying (1) at the point $T^n(x)$ gives $f(T^{n+1}(x))=2f(T^n(x))$, so $f(T^n(x))=2^nf(x)$ by induction. Since $T^{k+1}(x)=T^k(x)+f(T^k(x))$,

$$
T^n(x)=x+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}2^kf(x)=x+(2^n-1)f(x)=a(x)+2^n\bigl(x-a(x)\bigr).
$$

Thus $a(x)$ is constant along the $T$-orbit of $x$, and $T$ doubles the distance from this ``anchor'' $a(x)$.

**The Complete Characterization**.

**Theorem 1.**

Let $(D_a)_{a\in\mathbb{R}}$ be a family of subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ (some possibly empty) such that

1. each $D_a$ is closed under doubling: $d\in D_a\implies 2d\in D_a$;
2. the sets $a+D_a=\{a+d:\ d\in D_a\}$, $a\in\mathbb{R}$, are pairwise disjoint and their union is $\mathbb{R}$.

Then the function $f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ defined by

$$
f(x)=x-a\qquad\text{for } x\in a+D_a
\tag{2}
$$

is well defined and satisfies (1). Conversely, every solution $f$ of (1) arises in this way from exactly one such family, namely

$$
D_a=\{\,f(x):\ x\in\mathbb{R},\ x-f(x)=a\,\}.
$$

*Proof.*

($\Leftarrow$).

By (ii), every $x\in\mathbb{R}$ lies in $a+D_a$ for exactly one $a$, so (2) defines $f$ unambiguously. Let $x\in a+D_a$ and put $d=x-a=f(x)\in D_a$. By (i), $2d\in D_a$, hence

$$
x+f(x)=a+2d\in a+D_a,
$$

and therefore $f(x+f(x))=(a+2d)-a=2d=2f(x)$.

($\Rightarrow$).

Let $f$ be a solution and define $D_a=\{f(x):\ a(x)=a\}$. Since $f(x)=x-a$ whenever $a(x)=a$, we get

$$
a+D_a=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\ a(x)=a\}.
$$

These are the fibres of the map $x\mapsto a(x)$, so they are pairwise disjoint and cover $\mathbb{R}$; this is (ii).

For (i), let $d\in D_a$, say $d=f(x)$ with $a(x)=a$. By Lemma 1, $a(T(x))=a$ and $f(T(x))=2d$, so $2d\in D_a$.

Finally, if $x\in a+D_a$ then $a(x)=a$, so $f(x)=x-a$; that is, $f$ is the function (2) built from $(D_a)$.

*Uniqueness.*

Suppose a family $(D'_a)$ satisfying (i) and (ii) produces $f$ via (2). If $x\in a+D'_a$, then $f(x)=x-a$, i.e. $a(x)=a$; hence $a+D'_a\subseteq\{x:\ a(x)=a\}$. The sets $a+D'_a$ cover $\mathbb{R}$ and the fibres $\{x:\ a(x)=a\}$ are pairwise disjoint, so equality holds. Therefore $D'_a=\{x-a:\ a(x)=a\}=\{f(x):\ a(x)=a\}$, which is the family constructed above.

**Remark.**

Writing $g(x)=x-f(x)$, equation (1) is equivalent to

$$
g\bigl(2x-g(x)\bigr)=g(x)\qquad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

Theorem 1 says that this holds exactly when every fibre $g^{-1}(a)=a+D_a$ is invariant under the homothety $x\mapsto 2x-a$ of ratio $2$ centred at $a$.

**Examples**.

- $f\equiv 0$: here $D_a=\{0\}$ for every $a$.
- $f(x)=x+c$ for a constant $c$: here $D_{-c}=\mathbb{R}$ and $D_a=\varnothing$ for $a\neq -c$.
- $f(x)=x$ for $x\in S$ and $f(x)=0$ otherwise, where $S\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ is any set with $2S\subseteq S$ (for example $S=\mathbb{Q}$, $S=[0,\infty)$, or $S=\{2^n:\ n\ge 0\}$).
- $f(2^n-1)=2^n$ for $n=0,1,2,\dots$ and $f(x)=0$ for all other $x$; here $D_{-1}=\{0\}\cup\{2^n:\ n\ge 0\}$.
