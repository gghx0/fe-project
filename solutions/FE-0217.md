$f(x+f(y))=x+y$ for $x,y\ge0$

We claim the solutions are of the form $\boxed{f(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if }x\ge0\\g(x)&\text{if }x<0\end{cases}}$ where $g:(-\infty,0)\to\mathbb{R}$ any function. Easy to verify that they work, so we just need to show that assuming the original equation, $f(x)=x$ for all $x\ge0$.

Fix $x\ge0$, if $y\ge\max\{f(x),f(0)\}$ then:

$P(y-f(x),x)\Rightarrow f(y)=y+x-f(x)$

$P(y-f(0),0)\Rightarrow f(y)=y-f(0)$

so comparing, $f(x)=x+f(0)$ for all $x\ge0$. It remains to prove that $f(0)=0$.

Take $x\ge\max\{0,-f(0)\}$, we have:

$$
P(0,x)\Rightarrow f(f(x)=x\Rightarrow f(x+f(0))=x\Rightarrow x+2f(0)=x,
$$

and we have the result, so $f(x)=x$ for all $x\ge0$ as desired.
