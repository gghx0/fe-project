$$
f(2f(x)f(y))=2f(x)f(y).
$$

Let $\emptyset\ne S\subseteq\mathbb R$ be any set with the closure property that if $a,b\in S$ then $2ab\in S$. Then we claim that all solutions are of the form:

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if }x\in T\\g(x)&\text{if }x\notin T\end{cases}}
$$

where $T=\{2ab\mid a,b\in S\}$ and $g:\mathbb R\setminus T\to S$ is any function.

All such functions work: we can first see that $f(x)\in S$ for all $x$ whether $x\in T$ or $x\notin T$, then that $2f(x)f(y)\in T$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb R$ so $f(2f(x)f(y))=2f(x)f(y)$ as desired.

To show that all functions with $f(2f(x)f(y))=2f(x)f(y)$ are of this form, define $S=f(\mathbb R)$ and note that $S$ has the aforementioned closure property that $2f(x)f(y)\in S$. It's clear that $f(x)=x$ for $x\in T$, and that $f(x)\in S$ for $x\notin T$, hence the result.

For instance, with $S=\left\{2^n\mid n\in\mathbb N\right\}$ and $g(x)=2^{\lfloor\lvert x\rvert\rfloor+1}$ we have $f(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if }x=2^n,3\le n\in\mathbb N\\2^{\lfloor\lvert x\rvert\rfloor+1}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}$.
