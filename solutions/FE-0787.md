Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

We characterize all solutions. Let $S$ be a nonempty subset of $\mathbb{R}$ such that

$$
a,b\in S\implies 2ab\in S.
$$

Then every solution is of the form

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\in 2S^2,\\
g(x),&x\notin 2S^2,
\end{cases}}
$$

where

$$
2S^2=\{2ab:a,b\in S\}
$$

and $g:\mathbb{R}\setminus 2S^2\to S$ is arbitrary.

Every such function works: all values of $f$ lie in $S$, so $2f(x)f(y)\in2S^2$, and hence

$$
f(2f(x)f(y))=2f(x)f(y).
$$

Conversely, suppose $f$ is a solution and set $S=f(\mathbb{R})$. Then for any $a,b\in S$, say $a=f(x)$ and $b=f(y)$, the equation gives

$$
f(2ab)=2ab,
$$

so $2ab\in S$ and $f$ fixes every element of $2S^2$. Outside $2S^2$, the values of $f$ are simply elements of its range $S$. This is exactly the stated form.
