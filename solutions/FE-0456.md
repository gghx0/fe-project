Let $P(x,y)$ be the assertion

$$
f(xf(x)+f(xy))=x^2+yf(x).
$$

Putting $x=0$ shows that $f(0)=0$. Then $P(-1,1)$ gives $f(-1)=-1$, and $P(-1,0)$ gives $f(1)=1$.

From $P(1,y)$,

$$
f(1+f(y))=1+y,
$$

so $f$ is bijective. Also $P(1,1)$ gives $f(2)=2$. Using $P(2,y/2)$,

$$
f(4+f(y))=4+y.
$$

Comparing this with $P(1,y+3)$ and using injectivity, we get

$$
f(y+3)=f(y)+3
$$

for every real $y$.

Now take any $x\ne 0$ and replace $y$ by $y+3/x$ in $P(x,y)$. Using the shift identity twice,

$$
f(xf(x)+f(xy+3))
=f(xf(x)+f(xy)+3)
=f(xf(x)+f(xy))+3.
$$

Thus the left side becomes $x^2+yf(x)+3$, while the right side is

$$
x^2+\left(y+\frac3x\right)f(x).
$$

Hence $3=3f(x)/x$, so $f(x)=x$ for every $x\ne 0$. Since $f(0)=0$, the unique solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x}.
$$
