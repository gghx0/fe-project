Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(f(x)+xf(y))=x+yf(x).
$$

There are no constant solutions, so choose $u$ with $f(u)\ne0$. Then $P(u,y)$ shows that $f$ is surjective. Also, if $f(a)=f(b)$, comparing $P(u,a)$ and $P(u,b)$ gives $a=b$, so $f$ is injective. Hence $f$ is bijective.

From $P(0,y)$, the left side is constant while the right side is $yf(0)$, so $f(0)=0$. Therefore $P(x,0)$ gives

$$
f(f(x))=x.
$$

Let $a=f(1)$. Then $f(a)=1$. From $P(1,a)$,

$$
f(1+a)=1+a^2,
$$

while $P(a,a)$ gives

$$
f(1+a)=2a.
$$

Thus $1+a^2=2a$, so $a=1$. Hence $f(1)=1$.

Now $P(1,f(y))$ gives

$$
f(y+1)=f(y)+1.
$$

We prove additivity. Fix $x\ne0$ and $s\in\mathbb R$, and set

$$
v=\frac{s-f(x)}{x}.
$$

Using $P(x,f(v))$,

$$
f(s)=x+f(x)f(v).
$$

Next apply $P(x,f(v)+1)$. Since $f(t+1)=f(t)+1$ and $f(f(v))=v$,

$$
f(f(v)+1)=v+1.
$$

Therefore

$$
f(s+x)=x+f(x)(f(v)+1)=f(s)+f(x).
$$

The case $x=0$ is immediate from $f(0)=0$, so $f$ is additive.

Finally, $P(x,f(y))$ gives

$$
f(f(x)+xy)=x+f(x)f(y).
$$

By additivity and $f(f(x))=x$, the left side is $x+f(xy)$. Hence

$$
f(xy)=f(x)f(y).
$$

Thus $f$ is additive and multiplicative, with $f(1)=1$. For $t\ge0$, write $t=r^2$ to get $f(t)=f(r)^2\ge0$, so $f$ is order-preserving. Since $f$ fixes every rational number, the order-preserving property forces $f(x)=x$ for every real $x$. Therefore the unique solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x}.
$$

Direct substitution verifies that it works.
