$$
f(f(x)+f(xy))=f(x)+f(xy).
$$

By $P\left(x,\frac yx\right)$ we have that $f(f(x)+f(y))=f(x)+f(y)$ for all $x\ne0$, and by $P(y,0)$ we have that $f(f(x)+f(y))=f(x)+f(y)$ for $x=0$. On the other hand $f(f(x)+f(y))=f(x)+f(y)$ for all $x,y$ implies $f(f(x)+f(xy))=f(x)+f(xy)$, so we have demonstrated that this equation is equivalent to equation #92 and our solutions are:

Let $\emptyset\ne S\subseteq\mathbb R$ be closed under addition, then:

$$
f(x)=\boxed{\begin{cases}x&\text{if }x\in S+S\\g(x)&\text{if }x\notin S+S\end{cases}}
$$

where $S+S=\{s_1+s_2\mid s_1,s_2\in S\}$ and $g:\mathbb R\setminus(S+S)\to S$ is any function.

For instance, with $S=[1,\infty)$ and $g(x)=x^2+1$ we get $f(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if }x\ge2\\x^2+1&\text{if }x<2\end{cases}$.
