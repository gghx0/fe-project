The zero function is a solution. Now suppose $f$ is not identically zero, and choose $a$ with $f(a)\ne 0$. Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(f(x)^2+f(x)f(y))=f(x^2)+yf(x).
$$

If $f(u)=f(v)$, then comparing $P(a,u)$ and $P(a,v)$ gives $u=v$, so $f$ is injective. Also, as $y$ varies in $P(a,y)$, the right side $f(a^2)+yf(a)$ takes all real values, so $f$ is surjective.

Since $f$ is surjective, let $b$ be the unique real number such that $f(b)=0$. From $P(b,y)$,

$$
f(0)=f(b^2).
$$

By injectivity, $b^2=0$, so $b=0$. Hence $f(0)=0$, and $0$ is the unique zero of $f$.

Now $P(x,0)$ gives

$$
f(f(x)^2)=f(x^2).
$$

By injectivity, $f(x)^2=x^2$, so for every $x$ we have $f(x)=x$ or $f(x)=-x$.

Let $c=f(1)$, so $c=\pm1$. From $P(1,y)$,

$$
f(1+cf(y))=c(1+y).
$$

Taking absolute values using $f(t)^2=t^2$, we obtain

$$
|1+cf(y)|=|1+y|.
$$

Since $f(y)=\pm y$, this forces $f(y)=cy$ for every $y$. Therefore $f(x)=x$ for all $x$, or $f(x)=-x$ for all $x$.

Together with the zero function, the full solution set is

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0},\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x},\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=-x\text{ for all }x}.
$$

All three are checked directly to work.
