Solution by **Seungjun Lee**.
***

Let $\mathbb R^\times=\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$. For a multiplicative subgroup $G\le \mathbb R^\times$, define

$$
A_G=\{a\in\mathbb R:1+ag\in G\cup\{0\}\text{ for all }g\in G\}.
$$

We claim that all solutions are obtained as follows. Choose a transversal $T$ of $\mathbb R^\times/G$, and for each $t\in T$ choose $c_t\in A_G$. Define

$$
f(0)=0,\qquad f(tg)=gc_t\quad(t\in T,\ g\in G).
$$

Then these, and only these, satisfy

$$
f(x(1+f(y)))=f(x)(1+f(y))
$$

for all $x,y\in\mathbb R$.

Now, lets prove that this is the only solution. Let $f$ be a solution. Setting $x=0$ gives $f(0)f(y)=0$ for every $y$. If $f(0)\ne0$, then $f\equiv0$, a contradiction; hence $f(0)=0$.

Let

$$
G=\langle\,1+f(y):y\in\mathbb R,\ 1+f(y)\ne0\,\rangle.
$$

For every generator $a=1+f(y)\ne0$, the equation gives $f(ax)=af(x)$. Replacing $x$ by $a^{-1}x$ also gives $f(a^{-1}x)=a^{-1}f(x)$. Therefore

$$
f(gx)=gf(x)\qquad(g\in G,\ x\in\mathbb R). \tag{1}
$$

Choose a transversal $T$ of $\mathbb R^\times/G$, and put $c_t=f(t)$. By (1), $f(tg)=gc_t$. Also, for every $g\in G$,

$$
1+gc_t=1+f(tg)\in G\cup\{0\},
$$

so $c_t\in A_G$. Hence every solution has the stated form.

Conversely, suppose $f$ is defined in that form. If $y=tg$, then $f(y)=gc_t$, so $m:=1+f(y)\in G\cup\{0\}$. If $m=0$, both sides are $0$. If $m\in G$, then for $x=sh$ with $s\in T,\ h\in G$,

$$
f(mx)=f(smh)=mh\,c_s=m f(x).
$$

Thus $f(x(1+f(y)))=f(x)(1+f(y))$ for all $x,y$, completing the classification. $\square$
