Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(2f(x)f(y))=xf(y)+yf(x).
$$

The zero function is a solution. Now assume $f$ is not identically zero, and choose $j$ such that $f(j)\ne 0$.

If $f(a)=f(b)$, then comparing $P(j,a)$ and $P(j,b)$ gives $a=b$, so $f$ is injective. From $P(1/2,1/2)$,

$$
f\left(2f(1/2)^2\right)=f(1/2),
$$

so injectivity gives $2f(1/2)^2=1/2$, hence $f(1/2)=\pm 1/2$.

Also, $f$ is a solution if and only if $-f$ is a solution. It is enough to solve the case $f(1/2)=1/2$; the other sign will give the negative solution.

In this case, $P(x,1/2)$ gives

$$
f(f(x))=\frac{x+f(x)}2.
$$

Next,

$$
P(x,f(x)):\quad
f(xf(x)+f(x)^2)=\frac{x^2+xf(x)}2+f(x)^2,
$$

and

$$
P(f(x),f(x)):\quad
f\left(\frac{x^2+2xf(x)+f(x)^2}{2}\right)=xf(x)+f(x)^2.
$$

Let

$$
A=\frac{x^2+2xf(x)+f(x)^2}{2}.
$$

Applying $P(A,1/2)$, equivalently the formula for $f(f(A))$, and using the previous displayed identity for $f(A)$, we get

$$
f(xf(x)+f(x)^2)=\frac{A+xf(x)+f(x)^2}{2}.
$$

Comparing this with the formula from $P(x,f(x))$ gives

$$
(x-f(x))^2=0.
$$

Thus $f(x)=x$ in the normalized case. Undoing the sign choice, the nonzero solutions are $f(x)=x$ and $f(x)=-x$. Together with the zero function, the full answer is

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv 0},\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x},\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=-x\text{ for all }x}.
$$
