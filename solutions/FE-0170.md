Let $P(x,y)$ denote the given assertion $f(f(x)+f(xy)) = f(x)+xf(y).$

Claim: For any real numbers $a$ and $b$ with $f(a)=f(b)$, either $a=b$ or $f(a)=f(b)=0$ (or both).
Proof: Use $P(a, b)$ and $P(b,a)$ to get 

$$
\begin{align*}(1+a)f(a) &= f(a)+af(b) \\&= f(f(a)+f(ab)) \\&= f(f(b)+f(ab)) \\&= f(b)+bf(a) \\&= (1+b)f(a).\end{align*}
$$

 This means $f(a)=0$ or $1+a=1+b.$ $\square$

$P(0,0) : f(2f(0)) = f(0).$ By the Claim, we have $2f(0) = 0$ or $f(2f(0)) = f(0) = 0$; in both cases, $f(0) =0.$
$P(x,0) : f(f(x)) = f(x).$ By the Claim, we have $f(x)=x$ or $f(f(x)) = f(x) = 0$. This means 

$$
f(x) \in \{0,x \}
$$

for all real $x.$

Case 1: $f(x) = 0$ for all real $x$
This function works.

Case 2: $f(x) = 0$ for all real $x \neq -1$, and $f(-1) \neq 0$
We have $f(-1) = -1.$
$P(-1, 1) : f(-2) = f(-1)$, a contradiction.

Case 3: $f(y_0) \neq 0$ for some real $y_0 \neq -1$
We have $f(y_0) = y_0 \not\in \{-1,0\}.$
$P(x, y_0) : f(f(x)+f(xy_0)) = f(x) + xy_0$
We have $xy_0 \not\in \{-x, 0\}$ and $f(x) \in \{0, x\}$, so $f(x) + xy_0 \neq 0.$ This means $f(f(x)+f(xy_0)) \neq 0$ and therefore $f(f(x)+f(xy_0)) = f(x)+f(xy_0).$
So $f(x)+f(xy_0) = f(x)+xy_0$, which means $f(xy_0) = xy_0$ for all real $x.$ As $y_0 \neq 0$ this means $f(x)=x$ for all real $x.$ This function works.

Combining cases, the two functions that work are $f(x)=0$ for all real $x$ and $f(x)=x$ for all real $x.$
