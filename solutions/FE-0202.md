A function $f$ solves the equation iff there is a subset $S$ of the negative real numbers such that

$$
f(z)=0
$$

for all $z\in S$, while

$$
f(z)=z
$$

otherwise. It is trivial to check that any such function solves the equation.

Suppose $f$ solves the equation. $P(0,0)$ implies

$$
P(f(0)^2)=0.
$$

$P(x,f(0)^2)$ implies

$$
f(x^2)=x^2.
$$

So we see that $f$ is the identity on the nonnegative numbers.

Because

$$
x^2+f(y)^2
$$

is nonnegative, it follows that

$$
f(y)^2=y\cdot f(y)
$$

for all $y$. Thus, for all $y$, either

$$
f(y)=0
$$

or

$$
f(y)=y.
$$
