Solution by **OronSH**.
***

We claim $f(x)=x$ is the only solution.

Denote the assertion $P(x,y)$. Comparing $P(x,y)$ with $P(-x,-y)$ gives $f$ is odd. Hence $f(0)=0$. Now $y=0$ gives $f(f(x^2))=x^2$.

Take $P(x^{1/3}y^{2/3},x)$ to get $f(f(x^{2/3}y^{4/3})+f(x^{4/3}y^{2/3}))=x^{2/3}y^{4/3}+x^{1/3}y^{2/3}f(x)$. By symmetry this equals the same thing with $x,y$ swapped. Hence dividing out by $(xy)^{2/3}$ gives $y^{2/3}+x^{-1/3}f(x)=x^{2/3}+y^{-1/3}f(y)$, hence $x^{-1/3}f(x)-x^{2/3}=c$ for all $x$. Thus $f(x)=x+cx^{1/3}$.

Now let $x$ be positive and very large. If $c>0$ then $f$ is increasing, and if $c<0$ then $f$ is decreasing but still positive. In either case $f(f(x))=x$ is impossible. Hence $c=0$ so $f(x)=x$ as desired.
