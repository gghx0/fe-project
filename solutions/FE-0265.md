The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=0},\qquad \boxed{f(x)=x},\qquad \boxed{f(x)=\frac12}.
$$

First,

$$
P(0,0):\qquad f(0)=2f(0)^2,
$$

so $f(0)\in\{0,\frac12\}$.

Suppose $f(0)=0$. Then

$$
f(x^2)=f(x)^2. \tag{1}
$$

If $f(a)=f(b)\ne0$, comparing $P(a,y)$ and $P(b,y)$ gives that $f$ has period $a^2-b^2$. If this period were nonzero, (1) would imply $f(t)\ge0$ for all $t$, while

$$
P\left(x,-\frac{x^2}{f(x)}\right)
$$

would give a negative value whenever $f(x)\ne0$, a contradiction. Hence $a^2=b^2$. Thus

$$
f(a)=f(b)\ne0\implies a=\pm b. \tag{2}
$$

If $f(1)=1$, then $P(1,y)$ gives $f(y+1)=f(y)+1$. Comparing $P(x,y+1)$ and $P(x,y)$ gives

$$
f(t+f(x))=f(t)+f(x)
$$

for every $t$ whenever $f(x)\ne0$. In particular, $f(f(x))=f(x)$ for such $x$, so (2) gives $f(x)=\pm x$. The negative option is impossible, because if $f(k)=-k$ with $k\ne0$, then $P(k,k)$ gives $0=2k^2$. A nonzero root is also impossible: if $f(k)=0$ with $k\ne0$, then comparing $P(x,k)$ and $P(x,0)$ for $x$ with $f(x)\ne0$ forces $k=-2$ from $x=1$, contradicting $f(4)=4$. Therefore $f(x)=x$.

If $f(1)=0$, then $P(x,1)$ and (2) imply $f(x)\in\{0,-2x^2\}$ for every $x$. If $f(k)\ne0$, then $P(k,k)$ gives a positive value $8k^4$, impossible for a function whose values are only $0$ and nonpositive numbers. Hence $f\equiv0$.

Now suppose $f(0)=\frac12$. From $P(1,0)$, $f(1)\in\{0,\frac12\}$. If $f(1)=0$, then

$$
P(0,2): f(2)=-\frac12,\qquad
P(0,4): f(4)=-\frac32,
$$

but $P(2,0)$ gives $f(4)=0$, a contradiction. Thus $f(1)=\frac12$.

Then $P(1,y)$ and $P(0,y)$ give

$$
f\left(1+\frac y2\right)=\frac14+\frac12f(y)=f\left(\frac y2\right),
$$

so $f$ is periodic with period $1$. Also

$$
P(x,0):\qquad f(x^2)=f(x)^2+\frac12f(x)\ge -\frac1{16}.
$$

By periodicity, $f$ is bounded below by $-\frac1{16}$ on all of $\mathbb R$. If $f(x)\ge a_n$ for all $x$, then $P(0,2x)$ gives

$$
f(x)=\frac14+\frac12f(2x)\ge \frac14+\frac12a_n.
$$

Starting with $a_1=-1/16$ and iterating, $a_n\to1/2$, so $f(x)\ge1/2$ for all $x$.

Finally,

$$
P\left(x,-\frac{x^2}{f(x)}\right)
$$

is valid because $f(x)>0$, and gives

$$
\frac12=f(x)^2+f(x)f\left(-\frac{x^2}{f(x)}\right)\ge\frac14+\frac14.
$$

Equality forces $f(x)=1/2$ for every $x$. All three listed functions work.
