This is a classic problem. 
***
Since $f$ is injective, $P(x,0)$ implies $f(0)=0$. Then, $P(0,x)$ gives $f(f(x))=x$ and thus from $P(x,f(y))$, $f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)$.
***
The characterisation of general solutions to all additive and involutive functions over $\mathbb{R}$ is as follows:

Let $\mathbb{R}=A\oplus B$ be a decomposition of $\mathbb{R}$ into two $\mathbb{Q}$-vector spaces (so $x=a+b$ has a unique solution $a\in A$ and $b\in B$ for each real $x$). Then, $f(a+b)=a-b$.
