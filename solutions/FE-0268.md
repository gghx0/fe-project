***

$f\left(x^2+yf(x)\right)=f\left(x^2\right)+xf(y)$

If $f(k)=0$ for some $k\ne0$:


$P(k,x)\Rightarrow\boxed{f(x)=0}$ for all $x$ which is indeed a solution

Otherwise, assume $f(x)\ne0$ for all $x\ne0$
***
If $f(1)\ne1$:

$P\left(1,\frac1{1-f(1)}\right)\Rightarrow f(1)=0$, contradiction

So $f(1)=1$.

$P(1,x)\Rightarrow f(x+1)=f(x)+1$

$P(x,y+1)\Rightarrow f\left(x^2+yf(x)+f(x)\right)=f\left(x^2\right)+xf(y)+x=f\left(x^2+yf(x)\right)+x$
For $x\ne0$, $x^2+yf(x)$ can take any value so:

$$
f(y+f(x))=f(y)+x.
$$

Using the result of #6, $f$ is additive with $f(f(x))=x$, so the original equation reduces to $f(yf(x))=xf(y)$. Then simply substitute $x\mapsto f(x)$ to get that $f$ is multiplicative as well, well-known that the only field isomorphisms of $\mathbb{R}$ are $f(x)=0$ (which doesn't have $f(1)=1$) and $\boxed{f(x)=x}$
