We claim the unique solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

We use the following standard observation. If

$$
f(g(x)+f(xy))=x^2+f(xy)
$$

for all real $x,y$, then $g(x)-x^2$ is constant for $x\ne0$, and $f(t)-t$ is eventually constant. Indeed, from $P(x,0)$ the range of $f$ contains $[f(0),\infty)$. Then $P(1,y)$ gives

$$
f(g(1)+u)=1+u\qquad(u\ge f(0)),
$$

so $f(t)=t+1-g(1)$ for all sufficiently large $t$. Finally, for $x\ne0$,

$$
f(g(x)+f(y))=x^2+f(y),
$$

and taking $f(y)$ sufficiently large gives $g(x)-x^2=g(1)-1$.

For the present equation, $g(x)=xf(x)$. Hence $xf(x)-x^2$ is constant for $x\ne0$, while $f(x)-x$ is eventually constant. If $f(x)=x+a$ for all large $x$, then $xf(x)-x^2=ax$ is constant for all large $x$, so $a=0$. Thus $xf(x)-x^2=0$ for every $x\ne0$, and $f(x)=x$ for every $x\ne0$.

Finally, $P(-1,1)$ gives

$$
f(0)=1+f(-1)=0,
$$

so $f(x)=x$ for all real $x$. This function is directly checked.
