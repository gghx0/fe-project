Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(xf(x)+f(xy))=x^2+f(x)f(y).
$$

First, $P(0,0)$ gives

$$
f(f(0))=f(0)^2,
$$

while $P(0,y)$ gives

$$
f(f(0))=f(0)f(y).
$$

If $f(0)\ne0$, then $f$ is constant, which is impossible because the term $x^2$ appears on the right-hand side. Hence

$$
f(0)=0.
$$

Then $P(x,0)$ gives

$$
f(xf(x))=x^2. \tag{1}
$$

In particular, if $f(u)=0$, then $0=f(uf(u))=u^2$, so

$$
f(u)=0\iff u=0. \tag{2}
$$

Next,

$$
P(-1,1):\qquad f(-f(-1)+f(-1))=1+f(-1)f(1),
$$

so, using $f(0)=0$,

$$
f(-1)f(1)=-1. \tag{3}
$$

Also,

$$
P(1,-1):\qquad f(f(1)+f(-1))=1+f(1)f(-1)=0.
$$

By (2),

$$
f(1)+f(-1)=0.
$$

Together with (3), this gives

$$
f(1)^2=1.
$$

We split into two cases.

If $f(1)=1$, then $f(-1)=-1$. From $P(1,y)$ we get

$$
f(f(y)+1)=f(y)+1. \tag{4}
$$

Also, comparing $P(-1,y)$ with $P(1,-y)$ gives

$$
1-f(y)=f(f(-y)+1)=f(-y)+1,
$$

so

$$
f(-y)=-f(y).
$$

Thus $f$ is odd. From (1), every nonnegative real number lies in the range of $f$, and by oddness every nonpositive real number also lies in the range. Hence $f$ is surjective. In (4), let $f(y)+1$ range over all real numbers. We get

$$
f(x)=x
$$

for all real $x$.

If $f(1)=-1$, then $f(-1)=1$. From $P(1,y)$ we get

$$
f(f(y)-1)=1-f(y). \tag{5}
$$

Comparing $P(-1,y)$ with $P(1,-y)$ gives

$$
1+f(y)=f(f(-y)-1)=1-f(-y),
$$

so again $f$ is odd. As before, (1) and oddness imply that $f$ is surjective. In (5), let $f(y)-1$ range over all real numbers. We get

$$
f(x)=-x
$$

for all real $x$.

Both functions are directly checked, so the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=-x}.
$$
