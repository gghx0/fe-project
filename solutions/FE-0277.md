Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

We claim that the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=|x|}.
$$

Both are immediate to check. Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(x^2+f(x)f(y))=x^2+f(xy).
$$

From $P(x,0)$, the range of $f$ contains $[f(0),\infty)$. If $f(0)<0$, then there is $k\ne0$ with $f(k)=0$, and $P(k,x)$ forces $f(kx)$ to be constant in $x$, impossible for a nonconstant solution. Hence $f(0)\ge0$.

Now $P(0,x)$ gives

$$
f(f(0)f(x))=f(0).
$$

Using the fact that $[f(0),\infty)$ is in the range, this implies

$$
f(tf(0))=f(0)
$$

for all $t\ge f(0)$. Putting this into $P(xf(0),0)$ forces $f(0)=0$.

Then $P(x,0)$ gives

$$
f(x^2)=x^2,
$$

so $f(t)=t$ for every $t\ge0$. Also, $P(x,x)$ gives

$$
f(x^2+f(x)^2)=x^2+f(x^2)=2x^2.
$$

The input is nonnegative, hence

$$
x^2+f(x)^2=2x^2,
$$

so $f(x)\in\{x,-x\}$ for every $x$.

If $f(-k)=k$ for some $k>0$, then comparing $P(x,k)$ and $P(x,-k)$ gives

$$
f(xk)=f(-xk)
$$

for all $x$, so $f$ is even. Since $f(t)=t$ for $t\ge0$, this gives $f(x)=|x|$.

Otherwise $f(-k)=-k$ for every $k>0$, and together with $f(t)=t$ for $t\ge0$ we get $f(x)=x$.
