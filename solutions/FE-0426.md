$$
f(xf(x)+f(x)f(y))=f(x)^2+yf(x).
$$

First, $\boxed{f(x)=0}$ is a solution, and so we relegate our search to functions for which some $j$ exists such that $f(j)\ne0$.

$P(j,x)\Rightarrow f(jf(j)+f(j)f(x))=f(j)^2+xf(j)$.

From this alone, $f$ is bijective: if $f(a)=f(b)$ then:

$$
f(j)^2+af(j)=f(jf(j)+f(j)f(a))=f(jf(j)+f(j)f(b))=f(j)^2+bf(j)
$$

so $a=b$, and $f(j)^2+xf(j)$ can take any real value for $x\in\mathbb R$.

$P\left(f^{-1}(x),f^{-1}(y)\right)\Rightarrow f\left(xf^{-1}(x)+xy\right)=x^2+xf^{-1}(y)$, so $f^{-1}$ satisfies:

$$
f^{-1}\left(x^2+xf^{-1}(y)\right)=xf^{-1}(x)+xy
$$

which is equation #241 with solutions $f^{-1}(x)=x$ and $f^{-1}(x)=-x$, and therefore $\boxed{f(x)=x}$ or $\boxed{f(x)=-x}$ which are indeed solutions.
