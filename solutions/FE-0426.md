Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

The zero function is a solution. Assume now that $f$ is not identically zero, and choose $j$ with $f(j)\ne0$.

From $P(j,x)$,

$$
f(jf(j)+f(j)f(x))=f(j)^2+xf(j).
$$

This shows that $f$ is injective and surjective: if $f(a)=f(b)$, the displayed equation gives $a=b$, while its right-hand side takes every real value as $x$ varies.

Let $h=f^{-1}$. Substituting $x=h(u)$ and $y=h(v)$ in the original equation gives

$$
f(uh(u)+uv)=u^2+uh(v).
$$

Applying $h$ to both sides,

$$
h(u^2+uh(v))=uh(u)+uv.
$$

Thus $h$ satisfies FE-0241, whose solutions are $h(x)=x$ and $h(x)=-x$. Therefore $f(x)=x$ or $f(x)=-x$.

Including the zero solution, the full solution set is

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0,\qquad f(x)=x,\qquad f(x)=-x}.
$$
