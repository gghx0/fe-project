Let $P(x,y)$ be

$$
f(2xf(y))=f(x)f(y)+xy.
$$

First, $f(0)=0$. Indeed, $P(0,y)$ gives $f(0)=f(0)f(y)$ for all $y$; if $f(0)\ne0$, then $f$ would be the constant function $1$, which is not a solution.

The zero function is not a solution, so choose some $j$ with $f(j)\ne0$. If $f(a)=f(b)$, then comparing $P(j,a)$ and $P(j,b)$ gives $a=b$. Hence $f$ is injective.

Swapping $x$ and $y$ in the original equation gives

$$
f(2xf(y))=f(x)f(y)+xy=f(2yf(x)).
$$

By injectivity,

$$
xf(y)=yf(x)
$$

for all $x,y$. Taking $y=1$ gives $f(x)=cx$, where $c=f(1)$. Substituting into the equation gives

$$
2c^2xy=(c^2+1)xy,
$$

so $c^2=1$. Thus

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x}
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=-x\text{ for all }x}.
$$

Both functions work.
