Solution by **NamelyOrange**.
***

We claim that the only solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=x}.
$$

Both clearly work. Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(2xf(y))=xf(y)+yf(x).
$$

Putting $x=0$ gives $f(0)=0$. Let $a=f(1)$. From $P(x,1)$,

$$
f(2ax)=ax+f(x).
$$

If $a=0$, this gives $f(x)=0$ for all $x$, so assume $a\ne0$.

Comparing $P(1,y)$ with $P(f(y)/a,1)$ gives

$$
f\left(\frac{f(y)}a\right)=ay.
$$

Hence $f$ is surjective. It is also injective: if $f(u)=f(v)$, then the last displayed identity gives $au=av$, so $u=v$.

Now put $x=\frac1{2a}$ in $P(x,y)$. Using the previous identity,

$$
ay=\frac{f(y)}{2a}+y f\left(\frac1{2a}\right).
$$

Thus $f(y)$ is linear in $y$, say $f(y)=cy$. Since $f(1)=a$, we have $c=a$, so $f(y)=ay$ for all $y$.

Substituting $f(y)=ay$ into the original equation gives

$$
2a^2xy=2axy
$$

for all $x,y$. Since $a\ne0$, we get $a=1$. Therefore the nonzero solution is $f(x)=x$.

The complete solution set is

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=x}.
$$
