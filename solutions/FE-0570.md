Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(f(x)^2+xy)=xf(x)+f(xy).
$$

If $f(a)=f(b)\ne0$, then $P(a,0)$ and $P(b,0)$ give

$$
af(a)+f(0)=bf(a)+f(0),
$$

so $a=b$.

Also,

$$
P(0,0):\quad f(f(0)^2)=f(0).
$$

If $f(0)\ne0$, the injectivity statement above forces $f(0)^2=0$, a contradiction. Thus $f(0)=0$.

Now for $x\ne0$, replacing $y$ by $y/x$ gives

$$
f(f(x)^2+y)=xf(x)+f(y).
$$

For $x=0$, the same identity is also true because $f(0)=0$. Therefore this is exactly FE-0029, whose solutions are

$$
f(x)\equiv0,\qquad f(x)=x,\qquad f(x)=-x.
$$

Direct substitution verifies that all three work here, so the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0,\qquad f(x)=x,\qquad f(x)=-x}.
$$
