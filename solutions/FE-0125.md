Let $P(x,y)$ be

$$
f(f(x)+xf(y))=x+xf(y).
$$

There is some $y_0$ with $f(y_0)\ne -1$, since $f\equiv -1$ is not a solution. Then the right side of $P(x,y_0)$ is $x(1+f(y_0))$, which ranges over all real numbers as $x$ varies. Hence $f$ is surjective.

Choose $k$ such that $f(k)=0$. From $P(x,k)$,

$$
f(f(x))=x,
$$

so $f$ is injective. Now choose $a$ with $f(a)\ne 0$. In $P(f(a),y)$, using $f(f(a))=a$, we get

$$
f(a+f(a)f(y))=f(a)+f(a)f(y).
$$

Since $f$ is surjective and $f(a)\ne 0$, the expression $a+f(a)f(y)$ ranges over all real numbers. Therefore, for some constant $c=f(a)-a$,

$$
f(t)=t+c
$$

for all real $t$. But $f(f(x))=x$ gives $x+2c=x$, so $c=0$. Hence

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x}.
$$

Direct substitution verifies that this function works.
