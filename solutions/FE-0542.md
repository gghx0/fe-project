$$
f\left(f(x)^2+f(x)f(y)\right)=x^2+yf(x).
$$

Note that $f\equiv0$ isn't a solution, so there is some $j$ such that $f(j)\ne0$.

$P(j,x)\Rightarrow f\left(f(j)^2+f(j)f(x)\right)=j^2+xf(j)$.

From this alone, $f$ is bijective: if $f(a)=f(b)$ then:

$$
j^2+af(j)=f\left(f(j)^2+f(j)f(a)\right)=f\left(f(j)^2+f(j)f(b)\right)=j^2+bf(j)
$$

so $a=b$, and $j^2+xf(j)$ can take any real value for $x\in\mathbb R$.

$P\left(f^{-1}(x),f^{-1}(y)\right)\Rightarrow f\left(x^2+xy\right)=f^{-1}(x)^2+xf^{-1}(y)$, so $f^{-1}$ satisfies:

$$
f^{-1}\left(f^{-1}(x)^2+xf^{-1}(y)\right)=x^2+xy
$$

which is equation #504 with unique solution $f^{-1}(x)=x$, and therefore $\boxed{f(x)=x}$.
