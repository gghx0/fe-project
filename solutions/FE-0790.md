$$
f(2f(x)f(y))=2xy.
$$

We claim that the solutions are:

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\begin{cases}c\operatorname{sgn}(x)\exp(v_1(\ln\lvert x\rvert)-v_2(\ln\lvert x\rvert))&\text{if }x\ne0\\0&\text{if }x=0\end{cases}}
$$

where $c\in\mathbb R^+$ is a constant such that $\log_2c\notin\mathbb Q\setminus\{0,-2\}$, $V_1,V_2$ are two supplementary subvectorspaces of the $\mathbb Q$-vectorspace $\mathbb R$ such that $\ln c+2\ln2\in V_1$ and $3\ln c\in V_2$, and $v_1:\mathbb R\to V_1$ and $v_2:\mathbb R\to V_2$ are the projections of a real in $V_1,V_2$. This includes the particular solutions $f(x)=x$ when $c=1$ and $V_1=\mathbb R$ and $f(x)=\begin{cases}\frac1{4x}&\text{if }x\ne0\\0&\text{if }x=0\end{cases}$  when $c=\frac14$ and $V_2=\mathbb R$.

$P\left(x,\frac12\right)\Rightarrow f\left(2f\left(\frac12\right)f(x)\right)=x$ so $f$ is injective.

$P(xy,1)\Rightarrow f(2f(xy)f(1))=2xy=f(2f(x)f(y))$ so $f(xy)f(1)=f(x)f(y)$ by injectivity. If $f(1)=0$ then setting $x=y$ gives $f(x)=0$ for all $x$ which is not a solution. So $f(1)\ne0$ and we can define a function $g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ as $g(x)=\frac{f(x)}{f(1)}$ such that $g(xy)=g(x)g(y)$.

$P(x,1)\Rightarrow f\left(\frac1{2f(1)^2}\right)f(2f(1)f(x))=2f\left(\frac1{2f(1)^2}\right)x\Rightarrow g(g(x))=ax$ where $a=\frac{2f\left(\frac1{2f(1)^2}\right)}{f(1)^2}$. Since $f$ is injective, $g$ is as well.

Let $Q(x,y)$ be the assertion $g(xy)=g(x)g(y)$.

$Q(x,0)\Rightarrow g(0)(g(x)-1)=0\Rightarrow g(0)=0$ since $g$ is injective.

$Q(x,1)\Rightarrow g(x)(g(1)-1)=0\Rightarrow g(1)=1$ since $g$ is injective.

Now $a=g(g(1))=1$.

$Q(-1,-1)\Rightarrow g(-1)^2=1\Rightarrow g(-1)=-1$ by injectivity.

$Q(x,-1)\Rightarrow g(-x)=-g(x)$ so $g$ is odd.

For all $x\ne0$, we have $g(x)\ne0$ (injectivity) and so:

$Q\left(\sqrt x,\sqrt x\right)\Rightarrow g(x)=g\left(\sqrt x\right)^2>0$.

Now we can define $h:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ such that $h(x)=\ln g\left(e^x\right)$, the above equations transform into:

$$
h(x+y)=h(x)+h(y)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad h(h(x))=x.
$$

which implies #6. Therefore, $h(x)=v_1(x)-v_2(x)$ where $V_1=\{x\mid h(x)=x\}$ and $V_2=\{x\mid h(x)=-x\}$ are two $\mathbb Q$-supplementary subvectorspaces of $\mathbb R$ and $v_1:\mathbb R\to V_1$ and $v_2:\mathbb R\to V_2$ are the projections of a real in $V_1,V_2$, and we can go further back: $g(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)\exp(v_1(\ln\lvert x\rvert)-v_2(\ln\lvert x\rvert))$ and therefore

$$
f(x)=c\operatorname{sgn}(x)\exp(v_1(\ln\lvert x\rvert)-v_2(\ln\lvert x\rvert)).
$$

where $c=f(1)$. It remains to deal with $c$.

Substituting $f(x)=cg(x)$ into $P(x,y)$, we have:

$$
cg\left(2c^2g(x)g(y)\right)=2xy\Rightarrow cg\left(2c^2\right)g(g(xy))=2xy
$$

using multiplicativity. Recall $g(g(x))=x$, so setting $xy\ne0$ this is $g\left(2c^2\right)=\frac2c.$ From $g(x)>0$ for all $x>0$ and the oddness of $g$, $\operatorname{sgn}\left(2c^2\right)=\operatorname{sgn}\left(\frac2c\right)$. This restricts us to $c>0$.

Let $u=\ln\left\lvert2c^2\right\rvert=2\ln c+\ln2$ and $v=\ln\left\lvert\frac2c\right\rvert=-\ln c+\ln2$. We have $h(u)=v$ by its definition, and since $h$ is an involution, $h(v)=u$. By additivity $h(u+v)=u+v$ so $u+v\in V_1$, and $h(u-v)=v-u$ so $u-v\in V_2$.

Since $h(u)=v$ and $h(v)=u$, $u=0\Leftrightarrow v=0$. If $u=v=0$ then $\frac2c=2c^2=1$ which has no solutions, so we must have $u,v\ne0$.

Suppose $u,v$ are $\mathbb Q$-linearly dependent with $u\notin\{v,-v\}$.

Let $q=\frac uv\ne1$, then $u+v=\frac{q+1}{q-1}(u-v)$ so since $u-v\in V_2$, $u+v\in V_2$ as well and since $V_1\cap V_2=\{0\}$ we must have $u+v=0$.

Therefore they are either $\mathbb Q$-linearly independent (where we have $\frac uv=\frac{2\ln c+\ln2}{-\ln c+\ln2}\notin\mathbb Q$ iff $\log_2c\notin\mathbb Q$ and $u+v=\ln c+2\ln2\in V_1$ and $u-v=3\ln c\in V_2$) or $\mathbb Q$-linearly dependent with $\frac uv\in\{-1,1\}$ iff $\log_2c\in\{0,-2\}$. Verification of the solution sets follows by working backwards.
