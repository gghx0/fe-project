**Claim:** if $x^2+xf(y)\in f(\mathbb R)$ for all $x,y$ then $f$ is surjective.

**Proof.** $x=0$ gives $0\in f(\mathbb R)$ while $f(y)=0$ gives $[0,\infty)\subseteq f(\mathbb R)$. Now for any $x\in\mathbb R$ we can choose $a=-\lvert x\rvert-1$ and $b=-a+\frac xa=\frac{(\lvert x\rvert+1)^2-x}{\lvert x\rvert+1}\ge0$, then $x=a^2+ab\in f(\mathbb R)$.

$f\left(f\left(x^2\right)+f(x)f(y)\right)=x^2+xf(y)$.

From the claim, equation becomes #634 with solutions $\boxed{f(x)=x}$ and $\boxed{f(x)=-x}$ (which fits).
