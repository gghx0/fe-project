We use the equivalent form

$$
P(x,y):\qquad f(xy+yf(x))=xy+f(xy).
$$

The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}\qquad\text{and}\qquad \boxed{f(x)=-x}.
$$

First, $P(0,y)$ gives $f(yf(0))=f(0)$ for every $y$. If $f(0)\ne0$, then $f$ is constant, which is impossible in the original equation. Hence

$$
f(0)=0.
$$

Also,

$$
P(x,1):\qquad f(x+f(x))=x+f(x). \tag{1}
$$

Suppose there is some $u$ such that $f(u)\ne -u$. Let

$$
a=u+f(u)\ne0.
$$

By (1), $f(a)=a$. Substituting $x=a$ and $y/a$ in $P$ gives

$$
f(2y)=y+f(y) \tag{2}
$$

for every real $y$.

Now apply $P(x,2y)$. Using (2), the left-hand side is

$$
f(2y(x+f(x)))=y(x+f(x))+f(y(x+f(x))).
$$

Using $P(x,y)$ on the second term and then (2) on the right-hand side of $P(x,2y)$, we get

$$
2xy+yf(x)+f(xy)=3xy+f(xy).
$$

Thus $yf(x)=xy$ for all $x,y$, so $f(x)=x$ for all $x$.

If no such $u$ exists, then $f(u)=-u$ for every $u$. Both listed functions are checked directly.
