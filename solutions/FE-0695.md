Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

Again use the observation from FE-0459: if

$$
f(g(x)+f(xy))=x^2+f(xy),
$$

then $g(x)-x^2$ is constant for $x\ne0$, and $f(t)-t$ is eventually constant.

Here $g(x)=f(x^2)$. Thus

$$
f(x^2)-x^2=C\qquad(x\ne0)
$$

for some constant $C$. Fix $y$ and take $x$ so large that both $x^2+C+f(y)$ and $x^2+f(y)$ lie in the eventual identity region. Then

$$
x^2+f(y)+2C
=f(x^2+C+f(y))
=f(f(x^2)+f(y))
=x^2+f(y),
$$

so $C=0$. Hence $f(t)=t$ for all $t>0$, and the equation reduces to FE-0317.

Using the FE-0317 classification and the additional condition $f(t)=t$ for every $t>0$, the solutions are:

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x},
$$

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\ge0,\\
g(x),&x<0,
\end{cases}}
$$

where $g:(-\infty,0)\to[0,\infty)$ is arbitrary, and, for any constant $c<0$,

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x>c,\\
g(x),&x\le c,
\end{cases}}
$$

where $g:(-\infty,c]\to[c,\infty)$ is arbitrary.

Each listed function is in the FE-0317 family and also satisfies $f(x^2)=x^2$, so it works.
