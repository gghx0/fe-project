Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(f(x)+f(xy))=x+f(x)f(y).
$$

There are no constant solutions. From $P(0,y)$,

$$
f(2f(0))=f(0)f(y).
$$

Thus, if $f(0)\ne 0$, then $f$ would be constant, impossible. Hence $f(0)=0$.

Now $P(x,0)$ gives

$$
f(f(x))=x,
$$

so $f$ is bijective. Let $a=f(1)$. In $P(1,y)$,

$$
f(a+f(y))=1+af(y).
$$

Since $f$ is bijective, we may put $y=f(x-a)$, giving $f(y)=x-a$. Therefore

$$
f(x)=1+a(x-a)=ax+1-a^2.
$$

Using $f(0)=0$, we get $a^2=1$. Hence the only possible functions are $f(x)=x$ and $f(x)=-x$, and both satisfy the original equation. Thus

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x}
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=-x\text{ for all }x}.
$$
