Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f\left(f(x)+f\left(y^2\right)\right)=x+f(y)^2.
$$

If $f(u)=f(v)$, then comparing $P(u,y)$ and $P(v,y)$ gives $u=v$, so $f$ is injective. Let $a=f(0)$. From $P(x,0)$,

$$
f(f(x)+a)=x+a^2,
$$

so $f$ is surjective.

Next,

$$
P(0,y):\quad f(a+f(y^2))=f(y)^2,
$$

while

$$
P(f(y)^2-a^2,0):\quad f\left(f(f(y)^2-a^2)+a\right)=f(y)^2.
$$

By injectivity,

$$
f(y^2)=f(f(y)^2-a^2),
$$

and then injectivity again gives

$$
f(y)^2=y^2+a^2.
$$

Because $f$ is surjective, some $y$ has $f(y)=0$, forcing $a=0$. Thus $f(f(x))=x$ and $f(y)^2=y^2$ for all $x,y$.

With $a=0$, substituting $x\mapsto f(x)$ gives

$$
f(x+f(t))=f(x)+t\qquad(t\ge 0).
$$

For every $t\ge 0$, we know $f(t)=t$ or $f(t)=-t$. There cannot be positive $u,v$ with $f(u)=u$ and $f(v)=-v$: otherwise the last identity with $x=-v$ and $t=u$ gives

$$
f(u-v)=f(-v)+u=u+v,
$$

contradicting $f(u-v)^2=(u-v)^2$. Hence the sign is constant on the positive reals, and $f(f(x))=x$ then determines the negative reals as well.

Thus the only solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=-x\text{ for all }x}.
$$

Both are easily verified in the original equation.
