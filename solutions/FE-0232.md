The characterisation of all solutions is as follows.

Let $F$ be any solution to FE #7. Equivalently, let $V$ be a subgroup of $(\mathbb R,+)$ and let $S$ be a set of coset representatives for $\mathbb R/V$. Write every $x\in\mathbb R$ uniquely as $x=s+v$, where $s\in S$ and $v\in V$. Choose an arbitrary function $t:S\to V$ and define

$$
F(s+v)=t(s)+v.
$$

Let

$$
T=F([0,\infty)),\qquad D=[0,\infty)\cup\bigl([0,\infty)+T\bigr).
$$

Then every solution is obtained by defining

$$
\boxed{f(x)=F(x)\quad\text{for }x\in D}
$$

and assigning arbitrary values to $f(x)$ for $x\notin D$.

Indeed, if $x,y\in\mathbb R$, then $x^2,y^2\ge0$, so

$$
f(x^2)=F(x^2),\qquad f(y^2)=F(y^2).
$$

Moreover, $x^2+F(y^2)\in[0,\infty)+T\subseteq D$. Since $F$ is a solution to FE #7,

$$
f\left(x^2+f(y^2)\right)
=F\left(x^2+F(y^2)\right)
=F(x^2)+F(y^2)
=f(x^2)+f(y^2).
$$

It remains to show that all solutions arise this way. Let $f$ be a solution and put

$$
A=[0,\infty),\qquad h=f|_A,qquad T=h(A),qquad V=\langle T\rangle,
$$

where $\langle T\rangle$ is the additive subgroup generated by $T$. Replacing $x^2,y^2$ by arbitrary $a,b\in A$, the equation becomes

$$
f(a+h(b))=h(a)+h(b). \tag{1}
$$

If $u\in T$ and $a,a+u\in A$, then (1) gives

$$
h(a+u)=h(a)+u.
$$

The reverse translation follows by applying this at $a-u$. By composing such translations, ordering the positive steps before the negative ones so that all intermediate points remain in $A$, we obtain

$$
h(a+v)=h(a)+v \tag{2}
$$

whenever $v\in V$ and $a,a+v\in A$.

If $V=\{0\}$, then $h=0$, which extends to the zero solution of FE #7. Otherwise, every coset of $V$ meets $A$. Choose one representative $s\in A$ from each coset and define

$$
F(s+v)=h(s)+v\qquad(v\in V).
$$

By the characterisation in FE #7, $F$ is a solution to FE #7, and (2) shows that $F=h$ on $A$. Finally, (1) gives

$$
f(a+h(b))=h(a)+h(b)=F(a+F(b)),
$$

so $f=F$ on $A+T$ as well. No point outside $A\cup(A+T)$ occurs in the original equation, so all remaining values are arbitrary.
