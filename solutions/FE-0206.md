Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

The zero function is a solution. Assume now that $f$ is not identically zero, and choose $j$ with $f(j)\ne0$.

Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(x^2+f(y)^2)=xf(x)+yf(y).
$$

From $P(0,x)$,

$$
f(f(x)^2)=xf(x).
$$

In particular, $P(0,0)$ gives $f(f(0)^2)=0$, and then $P(0,f(0)^2)$ gives $f(0)=0$. Hence $j\ne0$.

If $f(a)=f(b)\ne0$, then $P(0,a)$ and $P(0,b)$ give

$$
af(a)=bf(b),
$$

so $a=b$. Thus $f$ is injective on nonzero values.

If $f(k)=0$, then comparing $P(k,j)$ and $P(0,j)$ gives

$$
f(k^2+f(j)^2)=f(f(j)^2)=jf(j)\ne0.
$$

By injectivity on nonzero values, $k^2+f(j)^2=f(j)^2$, so $k=0$. Hence $f(x)=0$ iff $x=0$.

For $x\ne0$, comparing $P(x,0)$ and $P(0,x)$ gives

$$
f(x^2)=f(f(x)^2)\ne0.
$$

Again by injectivity on nonzero values,

$$
f(x)^2=x^2.
$$

This also holds for $x=0$. Therefore the equation becomes

$$
f(x^2+y^2)=xf(x)+yf(y).
$$

By FE-0179, the only solutions of this reduced equation are $f(x)=cx$. Combining with $f(x)^2=x^2$ gives $c=\pm1$. Including the zero solution, the full solution set is

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0,\qquad f(x)=x,\qquad f(x)=-x}.
$$
