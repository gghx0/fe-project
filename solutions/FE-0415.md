We use the following simple claim.

**Claim.** If $x^2+xf(y)\in f(\mathbb R)$ for all real $x,y$, then $f$ is surjective.

Indeed, taking $x=0$ shows $0\in f(\mathbb R)$. Hence there is some $u$ with $f(u)=0$, and then $x^2+xf(u)=x^2$ shows $[0,\infty)\subseteq f(\mathbb R)$. For any real $t$, choose

$$
a=-|t|-1,\qquad b=-a+\frac ta.
$$

Then $b\ge0$ and $t=a^2+ab$, so $t\in f(\mathbb R)$.

Now let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(xf(x)+f(x)f(y))=x^2+xf(y).
$$

By the claim, $f$ is surjective. Thus, as $f(y)$ ranges over all real values, the equation becomes FE-0398:

$$
f(xf(x)+zf(x))=x^2+xz.
$$

FE-0398 has solutions $f(x)=x$ and $f(x)=-x$, and direct substitution shows both work here. Therefore

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=-x}.
$$
