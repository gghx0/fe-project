We claim that the unique solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

Let $c=f(0)$. We first note that if $af(a)=bf(b)$, then $a^2=b^2$. Indeed, comparing $P(a,0)$ and $P(b,0)$ gives $f(a)^2=f(b)^2$, and then comparing $P(0,a)$ and $P(0,b)$ gives $a^2=b^2$.

Now $P(0,0)$ gives $f(c^2)=c^2$. Also

$$
P(c^2,0):\quad f(c^4+c^2)=c^4,
$$

and

$$
P(0,c^2):\quad f(c^4)=c^4+c^2.
$$

Applying the first observation to $c^4+c^2$ and $c^4$ gives $(c^4+c^2)^2=c^8$, hence $c=0$.

Thus

$$
P(0,x):\qquad f(f(x)^2)=x^2. \tag{1}
$$

From this, $f(x^4)=f(x)^4$, so $f(1)\in\{0,1\}$. Since $f(1)=0$ would make $P(1,1)$ read $0=1$, we have $f(1)=1$.

Next, (1) and the implication $f(a)=f(b)\Rightarrow a^2=b^2$ give $f(-x)^2=f(x)^2$. Comparing $P(x,f(y)^2)$ and $P(-x,f(y)^2)$, and varying $y$, gives

$$
xf(x)=-xf(-x),
$$

so $f$ is odd.

We also have $xf(x)\ge0$ for all $x$. Otherwise, choose $v$ with $f(v)^2=-uf(u)$; then $P(u,v)$ gives

$$
0=f(u)^2+v^2,
$$

a contradiction unless $u=0$.

Equation (1) also shows that every nonnegative number is attained by $f$ at a nonnegative input. Hence, for every $X\ge0$, we may choose $z$ with $f(z)^2=X$. Then (1) gives $z^2=f(X)$, and substituting this $z$ into $P(y,z)$ yields, for all $X\ge0$,

$$
f(X+yf(y))=f(X)+f(y)^2. \tag{2}
$$

In particular, $f(X+1)=f(X)+1$ for $X\ge0$. For $x,y>0$, equation (2) gives

$$
f(x+y+f(y))=f(x)+2f(y). \tag{3}
$$

To see this, apply (2) first to $(x,y+1)$ and then simplify with (2) at $(x+y+f(y)+1,y)$ and $(x+y+f(y),1)$.

Now $f$ maps positive numbers onto positive numbers: from (1), every positive value occurs as $f(f(t)^2)$. Fix $u>0$. Choose $x>0$ with $f(x)=1-\{u\}$, where $\{u\}$ is the fractional part of $u$, and put $n=\lfloor u\rfloor+1$. Taking $y=n-f(x)>0$ in (3), with $x$ and $y$ interchanged, gives

$$
f(x+n)=2f(x)+f(n-f(x)).
$$

But $f(x+n)=f(x)+n$, so

$$
f(n-f(x))=n-f(x)=u.
$$

Thus $f(u)=u$ for every $u>0$. Oddness and $f(0)=0$ then give $f(x)=x$ for all real $x$, and this function works.
