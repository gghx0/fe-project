$f(xf(x)+xf(y))=x^2+yf(x)$

Easy to establish $f(0)=0$ and $f$ is bijective:

$P(0,0)\Rightarrow f(0)=0$

Since $f(x)=0$ isn't a solution, there is some $j$ with $f(j)\ne0$

If $f(a)=f(b)$, $P(j,a),P(j,b)\Rightarrow a=b$ and $P(j,x)$ also implies $f$ is surjective.

$P(x,0)\Rightarrow f(xf(x))=x^2$

$P(-x,0)\Rightarrow f(-xf(-x))=x^2$

Comparing these, we can get $f(-x)=-f(x)$ for $x\ne0$ so $f$ is odd (we already got $f(0)=0$).

For all $x\ne0$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P\left(x,-\frac{x^2}{f(x)}\right)&\Rightarrow f\left(xf(x)-xf\left(\frac{x^2}{f(x)}\right)\right)=0\\
&\Rightarrow xf(x)-xf\left(\frac{x^2}{f(x)}\right)=0\\
&\Rightarrow f(x)=f\left(\frac{x^2}{f(x)}\right)\\
&\Rightarrow x=\frac{x^2}{f(x)}\\
&\Rightarrow f(x)=x
\end{aligned}
$$

for all $x\ne0$, and since $f(0)=0$ we have $\boxed{f(x)=x}$ for all $x$.
