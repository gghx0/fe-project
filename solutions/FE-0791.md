Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

We solve

$$
P(x,y):\qquad f(2f(x)f(y))=xy+f(xy).
$$

The only solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

First, $P(x,1)$ gives

$$
f(2f(1)f(x))=x+f(x). \tag{1}
$$

Thus $f$ is injective: if $f(a)=f(b)$, then (1) gives $a+f(a)=b+f(b)$, so $a=b$.

Also, comparing $P(xy,1)$ and $P(x,y)$ gives

$$
f(2f(xy)f(1))=xy+f(xy)=f(2f(x)f(y)).
$$

By injectivity,

$$
f(xy)f(1)=f(x)f(y). \tag{2}
$$

If $f(1)=0$, then taking $x=y$ in (2) gives $f(x)^2=0$ for every $x$, which is not a solution. Hence $f(1)\ne0$. Define

$$
g(x)=\frac{f(x)}{f(1)}.
$$

Then

$$
g(xy)=g(x)g(y). \tag{3}
$$

Let

$$
d=f\left(\frac1{2f(1)^2}\right).
$$

Multiplying (1) by $d$ and using (2), we get

$$
d\,f(2f(1)f(x))=d(x+f(x)),
$$

while

$$
d\,f(2f(1)f(x))
=
f\left(\frac1{2f(1)^2}\right)f(2f(1)f(x))
=
f(1)f\left(\frac{f(x)}{f(1)}\right).
$$

After dividing by $f(1)^2$, this gives

$$
g(g(x))=ag(x)+bx, \tag{4}
$$

where

$$
b=\frac{d}{f(1)^2},\qquad a=bf(1).
$$

From (3), $g(0)=0$ and $g(1)=1$. Substituting $x=1$ into (4) gives

$$
b=1-a,
$$

so

$$
g(g(x))=ag(x)+(1-a)x. \tag{5}
$$

If $g(k)=0$ for some $k\ne0$, then $g(x)=g(x/k)g(k)=0$ for every $x$, impossible. Thus $g(x)\ne0$ for $x\ne0$. Also $a\ne0,1$: if $a=0$, then by definition $b=0$, contradicting $b=1-a$; if $a=1$, then $b=0$, again forcing $a=0$.

For $x\ne0$, multiplicativity gives

$$
g\left(\frac1x\right)=\frac1{g(x)}.
$$

Applying this to (5),

$$
\frac1{ag(x)+(1-a)x}
=
\frac1{g(g(x))}
=
g\left(\frac1{g(x)}\right)
=
\frac a{g(x)}+\frac{1-a}{x}.
$$

After clearing denominators, this becomes

$$
a(1-a)\left(x-g(x)\right)^2=0.
$$

Since $a\ne0,1$, we get $g(x)=x$ for every $x\ne0$, and also $g(0)=0$. Therefore $f(x)=cx$ for some constant $c=f(1)$.

Substituting $f(x)=cx$ into the original equation gives

$$
2c^3xy=xy+cxy.
$$

Thus

$$
2c^3=c+1.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
(c-1)(2c^2+2c+1)=0.
$$

The quadratic factor has negative discriminant, so $c=1$. Hence $f(x)=x$, which is directly checked.
