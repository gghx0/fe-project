We solve

$$
f(f(x)^2+f(x)f(y))=f(x)^2+f(x)f(y).
$$

Let $\emptyset\ne S\subseteq\mathbb R$ have the closure property that

$$
a,b\in S\Rightarrow a^2+ab\in S.
$$

Put

$$
T=\{a^2+ab\mid a,b\in S\}.
$$

Then all solutions are

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\in T,\\
g(x),&x\notin T,
\end{cases}}
$$

where $g:\mathbb R\setminus T\to S$ is arbitrary.

First, such functions work. Since $T\subseteq S$, we have $f(x)\in S$ for every $x$. Hence

$$
f(x)^2+f(x)f(y)\in T,
$$

so

$$
f(f(x)^2+f(x)f(y))=f(x)^2+f(x)f(y).
$$

Conversely, let $f$ be any solution and set

$$
S=f(\mathbb R).
$$

Then $S$ is nonempty, and if $a,b\in S$, write $a=f(x)$ and $b=f(y)$. The equation gives

$$
f(a^2+ab)=a^2+ab,
$$

so $a^2+ab\in S$ and $S$ has the required closure property. Moreover, for

$$
T=\{a^2+ab\mid a,b\in S\},
$$

the same identity shows $f(t)=t$ for all $t\in T$, while for $x\notin T$ we only need $f(x)\in S$. This gives exactly the stated family.
