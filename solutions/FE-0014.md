We claim the only solutions are $f(x)\equiv x$ and $f(x)\equiv 0$, which obviously work. We now show they are the only solutions. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion that $f(x+y^2)=f(x)+f(y)^2$. 
***
$P(0,0): f(0)=0$. 

$P(0,y): f(y^2)=f(y)^2$, which means $f$ is bounded below by 0 over the positive reals. Also, this means $f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)$ for positive $y$.

$P(-y^2,y): f(-y^2)=-f(y^2)$ so $f$ is odd. 

Hence, $f$ is additive. Since it is bounded over an interval, $f$ is linear. Checking, only $f(x)=x$ and $f(x)=0$ works.
