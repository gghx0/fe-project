Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(xf(x)+f(xy))=x^2+xf(y).
$$

We first show that $f$ is surjective. Since $x^2+xf(y)$ lies in the range of $f$ for all $x,y$, taking $x=0$ shows $0\in f(\mathbb R)$. Hence there is some $u$ with $f(u)=0$, and then $x^2+xf(u)=x^2$ shows $[0,\infty)\subseteq f(\mathbb R)$. Finally, every real number can be written in the form $a^2+ab$ with $b\ge0$, so every real number lies in the range of $f$.

Then $P(1,x)$ gives

$$
f(f(1)+f(x))=1+f(x).
$$

Since $f$ is surjective,

$$
f(f(1)+t)=1+t
$$

for every real $t$. Hence $f$ is linear. Testing $f(x)=cx+d$ in the original equation gives the unique solution

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$
