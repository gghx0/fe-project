**Note: we show the solution for the statement of Kyrgyzstan MO 2012 Q4, which has $x$ and $y$ swapped.**
***
Solution by **Particle**.
***

We claim the only solutions are $f(x)\equiv x$ and $f(x)\equiv -x$, which obviously work. We now show they are the only solutions. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion that $f(f(x)^2+f(y))=xf(x)+y$. 
***

$P(0,y)\implies f(f(0)^2+f(y))=y$ so $f$ is bijective. Hence assume $f(c)=0$. 

$P(c,y)\implies f(f(y))=y\quad (1)$

Using (1), $P(f(x),y)\implies f(x^2+f(y))=xf(x)+y=f(f(x)^2+f(y))$

$\implies x^2+f(y)=f(x)^2+f(y)\implies f(x)=\pm x$. 

Now suppose $\exists ab\neq 0:f(a)=a,f(b)=-b$.

$P(a,b)\implies \pm (a^2-b)=f(a^2-b)=a^2+b$ which is impossible for non-zero $ab$.
