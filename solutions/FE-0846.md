Solution by **AIME4096**.
***

We claim that the complete solution set consists precisely of the functions obtained as follows. Define $g=2f$, partition $\mathbb{R}$ into components of the four types described below, let $g$ act by shifting one step forward along every chain and by interchanging the two elements of every two-cycle, with $g(0)=0$, and then set $f=\frac{g}{2}$. Equivalently, every non-cyclic chain is of the form $x_n=2^nA+(-1)^nB$ for either all $n\geq0$ or all $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, with the terms pairwise distinct, and $f(x_n)=\frac{x_{n+1}}2$. These functions all work for every $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$. We now wish to prove that these are the only solutions. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion $f(f(xy)+f(xy))=xy+f(xy)$. Since $xy$ ranges over all of $\mathbb{R}$, the original equation is equivalent to $f(2f(x))=x+f(x)$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$. Define $g(x)=2f(x)$. Then the equation becomes $g(g(x))=g(x)+2x$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$.

**Lemma 1.** The function $g$ is injective and $g(0)=0$.

*Proof.* Suppose $g(a)=g(b)$. Applying the functional equation to $a$ and $b$ gives $g(g(a))=g(a)+2a$ and $g(g(b))=g(b)+2b$. Since $g(a)=g(b)$, the left-hand sides are equal, and therefore $2a=2b$. Hence $a=b$, so $g$ is injective. Substituting $x=0$ gives $g(g(0))=g(0)$. Since $g(g(0))=g(0)$, injectivity gives $g(0)=0$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.** For every $x\in\mathbb{R}$, if we define $x_n=g^n(x)$ for $n\geq0$, then $x_{n+2}=x_{n+1}+2x_n$ for every $n\geq0$. Consequently, there exist unique $A,B\in\mathbb{R}$ such that $x_n=2^nA+(-1)^nB$ for every $n\geq0$.

*Proof.* Since $x_{n+1}=g(x_n)$, applying the equation for $g$ to $x_n$ gives

$$
x_{n+2}=g(g(x_n))=g(x_n)+2x_n=x_{n+1}+2x_n.
$$

The characteristic polynomial of this recurrence is $r^2-r-2=(r-2)(r+1)$. Thus its general solution is $x_n=2^nA+(-1)^nB$. The constants are uniquely determined by $x_0$ and $x_1$, namely $A=\frac{x_0+x_1}{3}$ and $B=\frac{2x_0-x_1}{3}$. $\square$

**Lemma 3.** Every connected component of the functional graph of $g$ is exactly one of the following:
- the singleton $\{0\}$, with $g(0)=0$;

- a two-cycle $\{a,-a\}$ with $a\neq0$, where $g(a)=-a$ and $g(-a)=a$;

- a one-sided infinite chain $(x_n)_{n\geq0}$ of pairwise distinct real numbers satisfying $x_{n+2}=x_{n+1}+2x_n$;

- a two-sided infinite chain $(x_n)_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}$ of pairwise distinct real numbers satisfying $x_{n+2}=x_{n+1}+2x_n$.

*Proof.* Since $g$ is injective, every element has at most one predecessor. Therefore every connected component of the directed graph of $g$ is either a cycle, a one-sided chain, or a two-sided chain. Suppose a component is a cycle. Then its elements form a periodic sequence satisfying $x_{n+2}=x_{n+1}+2x_n$. By Lemma $2$, $x_n=2^nA+(-1)^nB$. If this sequence is periodic, then the term involving $2^n$ must vanish, so $A=0$. Hence $x_n=(-1)^nB$, which has period $2$ when $B\neq0$, and gives the singleton $\{0\}$ when $B=0$. Thus the only possible cycles are ${0}$ and two-cycles $\{a,-a\}$. If the component is not a cycle, injectivity implies that it is either a ray indexed by $\mathbb{N}_{\ge 0}$ or a bi-infinite chain indexed by $\mathbb{Z}$. The terms must be pairwise distinct, since otherwise the same real number would occur at two different positions and $g$ would not define a single-valued map on the component. This proves the lemma, meaning we can now state the complete classification. $\square$

We now claim that a function $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ satisfies $f(f(xy)+f(xy))=xy+f(xy)$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$ if and only if, after defining $g=2f$, the real line can be partitioned into components of the four types in Lemma $3$, with $g$ acting by $g(x_n)=x_{n+1}$ on every chain and by $g(a)=-a,g(-a)=a$ on every two-cycle. Equivalently, every non-cyclic chain is of the form $x_n=2^nA+(-1)^nB$ for either all $n\geq0$ or all $n\in\mathbb{Z}$, with the terms pairwise distinct, and $f(x_n)=\frac{x_{n+1}}2$. Note that necessity follows from Lemmas $1-3$. Conversely, suppose that $\mathbb{R}$ is partitioned into components of the stated types, and define $g$ by shifting one step forward along every chain and interchanging the two elements of every two-cycle, with $g(0)=0$. For a chain, the recurrence gives $g(g(x_n))=x_{n+2}=x_{n+1}+2x_n=g(x_n)+2x_n$. For a two-cycle, if $x=a$, then $g(g(a))=a=-a+2a=g(a)+2a$, and the same holds for $x=-a$. It is also immediate for $x=0$. Therefore $g(g(x))=g(x)+2x$ for every $x\in\mathbb{R}$.

Thus the complete solution set is the above family of functions. In particular, there are many nonlinear solutions. For example, let

$$
g(x)=\begin{cases}2x,&x\in\mathbb{Q},\\-x,&x\notin\mathbb{Q}.\end{cases}
$$

Both $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ are invariant under multiplication by $2$ and by $-1$, so $g(g(x))=g(x)+2x$ for every $x$. Therefore

$$
f(x)=\begin{cases}x,&x\in\mathbb{Q},\\-\frac{x}{2},&x\notin\mathbb{Q}.\end{cases}
$$

is a nonlinear solution. The two linear solutions are obtained from the two homogeneous choices $\boxed{f(x)=x,-\frac{x}{2}}$ Note that these are only two members of the full solution family described above. $\blacksquare$
