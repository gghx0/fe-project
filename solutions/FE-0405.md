Solution by **OronSH**.
***

We claim that the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=x}.
$$

Both work. If $f$ is constant, substitution gives $f\equiv0$. Assume from now on that $f$ is not constant.

From $P(0,y)$,

$$
f(yf(0))=f(0)^2.
$$

Since $f$ is not constant, $f(0)=0$.

Putting $y=-x$ gives

$$
0=f(x)^2+xf(-x),
$$

so for $x\ne0$,

$$
f(-x)=-\frac{f(x)^2}{x}.
$$

Putting $x$ replaced by $-x$ in the same relation gives

$$
f(x)=\frac{f(-x)^2}{x}.
$$

Combining these,

$$
f(x)=\frac{f(x)^4}{x^3},
$$

so for every $x\ne0$,

$$
f(x)=0\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=x.
$$

If there is $a\ne0$ with $f(a)=a$, then for any $b$ with $f(b)=0$,

$$
P(a,b):\quad f(a^2+ab)=a^2.
$$

The value $a^2$ is nonzero, so the pointwise alternative above forces $a^2+ab=a^2$, hence $b=0$. Thus no nonzero root exists, and $f(x)=x$ for all $x$.

If no nonzero $a$ satisfies $f(a)=a$, then $f(x)=0$ for every $x\ne0$, and also $f(0)=0$, so $f\equiv0$.
