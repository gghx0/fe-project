***

Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(x^2+yf(y))=x^2+f(y)^2.
$$

From $P(x,0)$, for every $t\ge 0$ we have

$$
f(t)=t+f(0)^2.
$$

Putting $t=0$ gives $f(0)=f(0)^2$, so $f(0)=0$ or $f(0)=1$.

If $f(0)=1$, then $f(t)=t+1$ for $t\ge 0$. For any fixed $y$, choose $x$ sufficiently large so that $x^2+yf(y)\ge 0$. Then

$$
x^2+yf(y)+1=f(x^2+yf(y))=x^2+f(y)^2,
$$

so $f(y)^2-yf(y)=1$ for all $y$. Taking any $y>0$ and using $f(y)=y+1$ gives $y+1=1$, impossible. Hence $f(0)=0$.

Therefore

$$
f(t)=t\qquad(t\ge 0).
$$

Again fixing $y$ and taking $x$ sufficiently large gives

$$
x^2+yf(y)=x^2+f(y)^2,
$$

so $yf(y)=f(y)^2$. Thus $f(y)=0$ or $f(y)=y$ for every real $y$.

Since $f(t)=t$ is already forced for $t\ge 0$, the complete solution set is

$$
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x, & x\ge 0,\\
x\text{ or }0, & x<0.
\end{cases}
$$

Equivalently, choose any subset $S\subseteq(-\infty,0)$ and set $f(x)=x$ for $x\in S\cup[0,\infty)$ and $f(x)=0$ for $x\in(-\infty,0)\setminus S$.

For every such function, $yf(y)=f(y)^2\ge 0$, so $x^2+yf(y)\ge 0$ and direct substitution verifies the equation.
