Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

We characterize all solutions. Let $S$ be a nonempty subset of $\mathbb{R}$ with the closure property

$$
a,b\in S\implies a^2+b^2\in S.
$$

Then every solution is of the form

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\in S^2+S^2,\\
g(x),&x\notin S^2+S^2,
\end{cases}}
$$

where

$$
S^2+S^2=\{a^2+b^2:a,b\in S\}
$$

and $g:\mathbb{R}\setminus(S^2+S^2)\to S$ is arbitrary.

Indeed, any such function has all values in $S$. Therefore

$$
f(x)^2+f(y)^2\in S^2+S^2,
$$

so the definition gives

$$
f(f(x)^2+f(y)^2)=f(x)^2+f(y)^2.
$$

Conversely, if $f$ is any solution, take $S=f(\mathbb{R})$. The equation shows that $S$ has the required closure property and that $f$ fixes every element of $S^2+S^2$. Outside this set, the only condition is that the values of $f$ remain in its range $S$, which gives the stated form.
