$$
f\left(f(x)^2+f(y)^2\right)=f(x)^2+f(y)^2.
$$

Let $\emptyset\ne S\subseteq\mathbb R$ with the closure property that if $a,b\in S$ then $a^2+b^2\in S$. Then define:

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if }x\in S^2+S^2\\g(x)&\text{if }x\notin S^2+S^2\end{cases}}
$$

where $S^2+S^2=\left\{a^2+b^2\mid a,b\in S\right\}$ and $g:\mathbb R\setminus\left(S^2+S^2\right)\to S$ is any function.

For this choice of $f$, $f(x)\in S$ for all $x\in\mathbb R$ so $f(x)^2+f(y)^2\in S^2+S^2$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb R$, and therefore $f$ indeed satisfies $f\left(f(x)^2+f(y)^2\right)=f(x)^2+f(y)^2$.

To see that all such functions are of this form, take $S=f(\mathbb R)$.
