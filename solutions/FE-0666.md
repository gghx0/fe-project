Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(f(x^2)+f(x)f(y))=f(x^2)+xf(y).
$$

The zero function is a solution. From now on, assume $f$ is not constant.

First, $P(-1,1)$ shows that $0$ lies in the image of $f$. Let $f(z)=0$. Then $P(z,y)$ gives

$$
f(f(z^2))=f(z^2)+zf(y).
$$

The left side and $f(z^2)$ are constant as $y$ varies, so $zf(y)$ is constant. Since $f$ is not constant, we must have $z=0$. Thus

$$
f(x)=0\iff x=0,
$$

and in particular $f(0)=0$.

Now $P(x,0)$ gives

$$
f(f(x^2))=f(x^2)
$$

for all $x$. Also, comparing $P(x,-x)$ and $P(-x,x)$ gives

$$
f(-x)=-f(x),
$$

so $f$ is odd. Hence the previous identity extends to

$$
f(f(t))=f(t)\qquad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb R.
$$

Let $R=f(\mathbb R)$. The last identity says exactly that $R$ is the set of fixed points of $f$. Since $f$ is odd, $R=-R$.

From $P(1,-1)$ and oddness,

$$
f(f(1)-f(1)^2)=0.
$$

Since $0$ is the unique zero and $f(1)\ne0$, this gives $f(1)=1$. Therefore $P(1,y)$ gives

$$
f(1+f(y))=1+f(y),
$$

so $R+1\subseteq R$. Since $R=-R$, this also gives $R-1\subseteq R$, and hence $R+1=R$. In particular, every integer belongs to $R$.

For $x\ne0$, define

$$
T_x:R\to R,\qquad T_x(r)=f(x^2)+xr.
$$

This maps $R$ to $R$ by $P(x,y)$, and it is injective because $x\ne0$. Put

$$
g(x)=f(x^2)+xf\left(\frac1{x^2}\right).
$$

Then

$$
T_x(T_{1/x}(r))=r+g(x).
$$

Thus $r+g(x)\in R$ for all $r\in R$. Since $R=-R$, also $r-g(x)\in R$. Consequently every $r\in R$ lies in the image of $T_x$, so $T_x$ is bijective.

For $x\ne0$ and $r\in R$,

$$
T_x(T_{-x}^{-1}(r))=2f(x^2)-r.
$$

Using oddness gives the same conclusion with $f(-x^2)$ in place of $f(x^2)$. Since every element of $R$ is of the form $f(t)$, we obtain

$$
2s-r\in R\qquad\text{for all }r,s\in R.
$$

Now take any $s\in R$. Since $T_2$ is bijective, there exists $r\in R$ such that

$$
s=T_2(r)=f(4)+2r=4+2r.
$$

Because $4\in R$ and $R+1=R$, this gives $s/2=2+r\in R$. Hence for $r,s\in R$,

$$
s-r=2\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)-r\in R.
$$

So $R$ is an additive subgroup of $\mathbb R$.

Finally, $f(x^2)+f(x)\in R$. Applying $P(x,1)$ and using $f(1)=1$, the left side is fixed by $f$, so

$$
f(x^2)+f(x)=f(x^2)+x.
$$

Thus $f(x)=x$ for every $x$. Therefore the complete solution set is

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x}.
$$
