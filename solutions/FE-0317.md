Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(x^2+f(xy))=x^2+f(xy).
$$

If $x\ne0$, then $xy$ runs over all real numbers. Thus

$$
f(t+f(u))=t+f(u)\qquad(t>0,\ u\in\mathbb R).
$$

If $f$ is unbounded below, this immediately gives $f(z)=z$ for every real $z$.

Otherwise, let $c=\inf f(\mathbb R)$. The previous identity gives

$$
f(z)=z\qquad(z>c).
$$

Conversely, any solution with finite infimum must have $f(x)\ge c$ for all $x$, and the only extra condition comes from $P(0,0)$:

$$
f(f(0))=f(0).
$$

Therefore the solutions are exactly the following.

First, $f(x)=x$ for all $x$.

Second, for $c>0$ and $a>c$,

$$
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x>c,\\
a,&x=0,\\
g(x),&0\ne x\le c,
\end{cases}
$$

where $g:(-\infty,c]\setminus\{0\}\to[c,\infty)$ is arbitrary.

Third, for $c>0$,

$$
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\ge c,\\
c,&x=0,\\
g(x),&0\ne x<c,
\end{cases}
$$

where $g:(-\infty,c)\setminus\{0\}\to[c,\infty)$ is arbitrary.

Finally, for $c\le0$,

$$
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x>c,\\
g(x),&x\le c,
\end{cases}
$$

where $g:(-\infty,c]\to[c,\infty)$ is arbitrary.

It is immediate to check that each listed function works: for $x\ne0$, the input $x^2+f(xy)$ is always strictly bigger than $c$, so the outer $f$ fixes it; for $x=0$, the condition is precisely $f(f(0))=f(0)$.
