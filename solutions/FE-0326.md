We claim that the unique solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

From $P(1,y)$,

$$
f(1+f(y))=y+f(1)^2,
$$

so $f$ is bijective. Also

$$
P(x,0):\qquad f(x^2+f(0))=f(x)^2.
$$

Comparing $x$ and $-x$, we get $f(-x)^2=f(x)^2$. Since $f$ is injective, $f(-x)=f(x)$ can happen only for $x=0$, so $f$ is odd away from $0$.

If $f(u)=0$, then $P(u,0)$ and $P(-u,0)$ imply $f(-u)=0$, and injectivity gives $u=0$. Hence $f(0)=0$, and $f$ is odd on all of $\mathbb R$.

Now

$$
f(x^2)=f(x)^2,
$$

so $f(1)=1$. From $P(1,y)$ and $P(-1,y)$,

$$
f(f(y)+1)=y+1,\qquad f(f(y)-1)=y-1.
$$

Thus

$$
f(y+2)=f(y)+2.
$$

Using $P(2,x/2)$ and this shift by $4$, we get $f(f(x))=x$ for all $x$. Then $P(1,f(x))$ gives

$$
f(x+1)=f(x)+1.
$$

Finally, since $f(x)^2=f(x^2)$, the original equation is

$$
f(x^2+f(xy))=f(x^2)+xy. \tag{1}
$$

For $a>0$, choose $x=\sqrt a$ in (1) and vary $y$ to get

$$
f(a+f(t))=f(a)+t\qquad(a>0,\ t\in\mathbb R).
$$

For $a=0$ this is $f(f(t))=t$, and for $a<0$ it follows by oddness from the positive case. Therefore

$$
f(a+f(t))=f(a)+t
$$

for all real $a,t$. Replacing $t$ by $f(t)$ gives additivity:

$$
f(a+t)=f(a)+f(t).
$$

An additive function with $f(x^2)=f(x)^2\ge0$ for all $x$ is bounded below on every bounded interval of nonnegative inputs, hence is linear. Since $f(1)=1$, we get $f(x)=x$, which works.
