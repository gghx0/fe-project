Let

$$
P(x,y):\qquad f(f(x)f(y)+f(xy))=xy+f(xy).
$$

The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=-x}.
$$

Put $c=f(1)$. From $P(x,1)$,

$$
f((c+1)f(x))=x+f(x). \tag{1}
$$

Thus $f$ is injective: if $f(a)=f(b)$, then (1) gives $a+f(a)=b+f(b)$, hence $a=b$.

Comparing $P(x,y)$ with $P(xy,1)$ gives

$$
cf(xy)=f(x)f(y). \tag{2}
$$

If $c=0$, then (2) gives $f(x)f(y)=0$ for all $x,y$, so $f\equiv0$, not a solution. Hence $c\ne0$.

Define

$$
g(x)=\frac{f(x)}c.
$$

Then

$$
g(xy)=g(x)g(y). \tag{3}
$$

If $c=-1$, then $P(1,1)$ gives $f(0)=0$, and (1) gives $x+f(x)=0$. Thus $f(x)=-x$.

Now assume $c\ne-1$. Let

$$
d=f\left(\frac1{c^2+c}\right).
$$

Multiplying (1) by $d$ and using (2), we get

$$
g(g(x))=ag(x)+bx, \tag{4}
$$

where

$$
b=\frac d{c^2},
\qquad
a=bc.
$$

From (3), since $g$ is nonconstant,

$$
g(0)=0,\qquad g(1)=1.
$$

Substituting $x=1$ in (4) gives

$$
b=1-a,
$$

so

$$
g(g(x))=ag(x)+(1-a)x. \tag{5}
$$

Also $a\ne0,1$. Indeed, if $a=0$, then by the definitions $b=0$, contradicting $a+b=1$. If $a=1$, then $b=0$, again contradicting the definitions.

For $x\ne0$, multiplicativity gives $g(x)\ne0$ and

$$
g\left(\frac1x\right)=\frac1{g(x)}.
$$

Applying (5) to $1/x$ gives

$$
\frac1{ag(x)+(1-a)x}
=
\frac a{g(x)}+\frac{1-a}x.
$$

Clearing denominators,

$$
a(1-a)(x-g(x))^2=0.
$$

Since $a\ne0,1$, we have $g(x)=x$ for all $x\ne0$, and also $g(0)=0$. Hence $f(x)=cx$.

Testing $f(x)=cx$ in the original equation gives

$$
c^2(c+1)xy=(c+1)xy.
$$

The case $c=-1$ has already given $f(x)=-x$; otherwise $c^2=1$, so $c=1$. Therefore $f(x)=x$.

Both solutions work.
