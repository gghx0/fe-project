Solution by **P2nisic**.
***

Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f((x+f(x))f(y))=yf(x)+f(xy).
$$

For any real $a,b$, applying $P(a,b)$ gives

$$
f((a+f(a))f(b))=bf(a)+f(ab).
\tag{1}
$$

Now set

$$
u=(a+f(a))f(b).
$$

Applying $P(u,x)$ and using (1), the left-hand side becomes

$$
f((u+f(u))f(x))
=f((af(b)+f(a)f(b)+bf(a)+f(ab))f(x)).
$$

The expression inside $f$ is symmetric in $a$ and $b$. Therefore the corresponding right-hand side of $P(u,x)$ is also symmetric, so

$$
xf((a+f(a))f(b))+f(x(a+f(a))f(b))
=xf((b+f(b))f(a))+f(x(b+f(b))f(a)).
$$

Putting $x=1$ gives

$$
2f((a+f(a))f(b))=2f((b+f(b))f(a)).
$$

Using (1) on both sides,

$$
2bf(a)+2f(ab)=2af(b)+2f(ab),
$$

and hence

$$
bf(a)=af(b)
$$

for all real $a,b$. Taking $b=1$, we obtain

$$
f(a)=af(1)
$$

for all real $a$. Thus $f(x)=cx$ for some constant $c$.

Substituting $f(x)=cx$ into the original equation gives

$$
c^2(1+c)xy=2cxy
$$

for all real $x,y$. Hence

$$
c(c+2)(c-1)=0,
$$

so $c\in\{0,1,-2\}$.

Therefore the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=0,\qquad f(x)=x,\qquad f(x)=-2x}.
$$

Direct substitution verifies that all three functions work.
