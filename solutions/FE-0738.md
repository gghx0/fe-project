$f(xf(y)+f(x)f(y))=xf(y)+yf(x)$

$\boxed{f(x)=0}$ is a solution, else there is some $j$ with $f(j)\ne0$ and $P(j,x)$ implies $f$ is injective:

If $f(a)=f(b)$:

$P(j,a)\Rightarrow f(jf(a)+f(j)f(a))=jf(a)+af(j)$

$P(j,b)\Rightarrow f(jf(a)+f(j)f(a))=jf(a)+bf(j)$

and so $a=b$.

Swapping $x,y$ in the original equation we have:

$$
f(xf(y)+f(x)f(y))=xf(y)+yf(x)=f(yf(x)+f(x)f(y)),
$$

so $xf(y)+f(x)f(y)=yf(x)+f(x)f(y)$ which becomes upon setting $y=1$ the identity $f(x)=xf(1)$. Testing, our solutions are (noting the existence of $j$) $\boxed{f(x)=-2x}$ and $\boxed{f(x)=x}$.
