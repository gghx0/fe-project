Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(x^2+f(y^2))=x^2+yf(y).
$$

From $P(x,0)$,

$$
f(x^2+f(0))=x^2.
$$

Thus

$$
f(t)=t-f(0)
$$

for every $t\ge f(0)$.

Now fix $y$. Choose $x$ large enough that both $x^2+f(y^2)$ and $x^2$ are at least $f(0)$. Then

$$
x^2+f(y^2)-f(0)=f(x^2+f(y^2))=x^2+yf(y),
$$

so

$$
f(y^2)=yf(y)+f(0)
\tag{1}
$$

for all $y$.

Putting $y=1$ in (1) gives $f(1)=f(1)+f(0)$, so $f(0)=0$. Hence $f(t)=t$ for all $t\ge0$.

Equation (1) now becomes

$$
f(y^2)=yf(y).
$$

Since $f(y^2)=y^2$, we have $yf(y)=y^2$, and therefore $f(y)=y$ for every $y\ne0$. Also $f(0)=0$, so the unique solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$
