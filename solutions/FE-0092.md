$$
f(f(x)+f(y))=f(x)+f(y).
$$

Let $\emptyset\ne S\subseteq\mathbb R$ be closed under addition, then all functions $f$ are characterized as:

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if }x\in S+S\\g(x)&\text{if }x\notin S+S\end{cases}}
$$

where $S+S=\{s_1+s_2\mid s_1,s_2\in S\}$ and $g:\mathbb R\setminus(S+S)\to S$ is any function.

Easy to verify that these functions work, and to show that all functions with $f(f(x)+f(y))=f(x)+f(y)$ are of this form we choose $S=f(\mathbb R)$.

For instance, with $S=[1,\infty)$ and $g(x)=x^2+1$ we get $f(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if }x\ge2\\x^2+1&\text{if }x<2\end{cases}$.
