Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(f(x)+f(y))=f(x)+f(y).
$$

We claim that the solutions are exactly the functions with the following property. If

$$
A=f(\mathbb R),
$$

then

$$
A+A\subseteq A
$$

and

$$
f(t)=t
$$

for every $t\in A+A$.

Indeed, if $f$ is a solution, then for any $a,b\in A$, choose $x,y$ with $a=f(x)$ and $b=f(y)$. The equation gives

$$
f(a+b)=a+b,
$$

so $a+b\in A$ and $f$ fixes every element of $A+A$.

Conversely, suppose $A=f(\mathbb R)$ satisfies $A+A\subseteq A$, and suppose $f(t)=t$ for every $t\in A+A$. Since $f(x),f(y)\in A$, we have $f(x)+f(y)\in A+A$, and therefore

$$
f(f(x)+f(y))=f(x)+f(y).
$$

Thus the condition is both necessary and sufficient.

Equivalently, one may choose a nonempty set $A\subseteq\mathbb R$ with $A+A\subseteq A$, define $f$ to be the identity on $A+A$, and define the remaining values of $f$ inside $A$, provided that the resulting image is exactly $A$.
