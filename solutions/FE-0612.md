Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

We claim that the unique solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

From $P(0,y)$,

$$
f(f(0))=yf(0),
$$

so $f(0)=0$. Also $P(-1,1)$ gives $f(-1)=-1$, and $P(-1,1-x)$ shows that $f$ is surjective.

We first prove that $f$ has a nonzero translation. If $f(1)\ne1$, then $P(-1,x)$ gives

$$
f(f(1)-f(x))=1-x,
$$

so

$$
f(f(1)-1+x)=1-f(1)+f(x).
$$

If $f(1)=1$, then $P(1,x)$ gives

$$
f(1+f(x))=1+x,
$$

and hence

$$
f(x+2)=f(x)+2.
$$

Thus in all cases there are nonzero $a,b$ such that

$$
f(x+a)=f(x)+b
$$

for all $x$.

Using $P(x,y+a)$ and this translation,

$$
f(f(x^2)+xf(y)+bx)=f(f(x^2)+xf(y))+af(x).
$$

Since $f$ is surjective, for $x\ne0$ the input $f(x^2)+xf(y)$ spans all real numbers. Thus

$$
f(t+bx)=f(t)+af(x)
$$

for all $t,x$. Taking $t=0$ gives $f(bx)=af(x)$, so

$$
f(t+bx)=f(t)+f(bx).
$$

As $b\ne0$, this proves that $f$ is additive.

Now $P(x,0)$ gives

$$
f(f(x^2))=x^2.
$$

Using additivity, the original equation reduces to

$$
f(xf(y))=yf(x).
$$

Then

$$
f(x+xf(y))=f(x)+yf(x).
$$

By FE-0046, the only possibilities are $f\equiv0$ and $f(x)=x$. The zero function does not satisfy the present equation, so

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$
