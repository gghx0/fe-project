$f\left(f\left(x^2\right)+xf(y)\right)=x^2+yf(x)$.

$P(0,x)\Rightarrow f(f(0))=xf(0)$, so varying $x$ we find that $f(0)=0$.

$P(-1,1)\Rightarrow f(-1)=-1$.

$P(-1,1-x)\Rightarrow f(f(1)-f(1-x))=x$, so $f$ is surjective.

**Claim:** there are some $a,b\ne0$ such that $f(x+a)=f(x)+b$ for all $x$.

If $f(1)\ne1$:

$P(-1,x)\Rightarrow f(f(1)-f(x))=1-x$ so:

$$
f(f(1)-1+x)=f(f(1)-f(f(1)-f(x)))=1-f(1)+f(x),
$$

and so we can take $a=f(1)-1$ and $b=1-f(1)$.

If $f(1)=1$:

$P(1,x)\Rightarrow f(1+f(x))=1+x$ so:

$$
f(2+x)=f(1+f(1+f(x)))=2+f(x),
$$

and so we can take $a=b=2$.

$P(x,y+a)\Rightarrow f\left(f\left(x^2\right)+xf(y)+bx\right)=x^2+yf(x)+af(x)=f\left(f\left(x^2\right)+xf(y)\right)+af(x)$

and since $f\left(x^2\right)+xf(y)$ can take any value for $x\ne0$ (by surjectivity):

$$
f(y+bx)=f(y)+af(x)
$$

for all $x\ne0$. It's true for $x=0$ as well, so taking $y=0$ we have $f(bx)=af(x)$ and so:

$$
f(y+bx)=f(y)+f(bx)\Rightarrow f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y),
$$

that is, $f$ is additive.

$P(x,0)\Rightarrow f\left(f\left(x^2\right)\right)=x^2$.

Original equation reduces to $f(xf(y))=yf(x)$, now it's easy to obtain by additivity that:

$$
f(x+xf(y))=f(x)+yf(x),
$$

which by #46 has solutions $f(x)=0$ (doesn't fit the original equation) and $\boxed{f(x)=x}$.
