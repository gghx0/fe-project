Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(f(x)^2+xf(y))=x^2+xf(y).
$$

We first show that $f$ is surjective. Since $x^2+xf(y)$ always lies in the range of $f$, taking $x=0$ shows $0\in f(\mathbb R)$. Hence there is some $u$ with $f(u)=0$, and then $x^2+xf(u)=x^2$ shows $[0,\infty)\subseteq f(\mathbb R)$. Finally, every real number can be written in the form $a^2+ab$ with $b\ge0$, so every real number lies in the range of $f$.

Therefore the equation is exactly FE-0564 after replacing $f(y)$ by an arbitrary real number:

$$
f(f(x)^2+xy)=x^2+xy.
$$

FE-0564 has the unique solution $f(x)=x$, which indeed works here. Hence

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$
