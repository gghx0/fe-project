Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(2xf(y))=xf(y)+xy.
$$

The zero function is not a solution, so choose $j$ with $f(j)\ne0$. Then

$$
P\left(\frac{x}{2f(j)},j\right)
$$

gives

$$
f(x)=\frac{x}{2}+\frac{xj}{2f(j)}.
$$

Thus $f$ is linear, say $f(x)=cx$. Substituting this into the equation gives

$$
2c^2xy=(c+1)xy
$$

for all real $x,y$. Hence

$$
2c^2-c-1=0,
$$

so $c=1$ or $c=-\frac12$. Therefore the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=-\frac{x}{2}\text{ for all }x}.
$$

Both are checked directly to work.
