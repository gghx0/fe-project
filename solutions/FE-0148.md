From the original equation, we can immediately obtain that $f$ is bijective. Hence there exists only $c$ such that $f(c)=0$. Substitute $x=c$, $y=0$ into the problem, we have:

$$
f(f(c)+f(c)f(0))=c+c.0 \Rightarrow f(0)=c \Rightarrow f(f(0))=0.
$$

 Replace $x=f(0)$ into the problem, this gives us:

$$
f(f(f(0))+f(0)f(y))=f(0)+yf(0) \Rightarrow f(0)=f(0)+yf(0) \Rightarrow f(0)=0.
$$

 Substitute $y=0$ into the problem, it yields that:

$$
f(f(x)+f(x)f(0))=x+x.0 \Rightarrow f(f(x))=x, \forall x \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

 Then replace $x \to f(x)$, $y \to f(y)$ and using the last result, this follows that:

$$
f(x+xy)=f(x)+f(x)f(y), \forall x,y \in \mathbb{R} \Rightarrow f(y+1)=f(y)f(1)+f(1), \forall y \in \mathbb{R}. \textbf{(1)}
$$

 From $\textbf{(1)}$ we have $f(y)+1=\dfrac{f(y+1)}{f(1)}, \forall y \in \mathbb{R}$. Therefore, this results in:

$$
f(x+xy)=f(x)(f(y)+1)=f(x).\dfrac{f(y+1)}{f(1)} \Rightarrow f(xy)f(1)=f(x)f(y), \forall x,y \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

 Hence this gives us

$$
f(x+xy)=f(x)+f(xy)f(1) \Rightarrow f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)f(1)=f(y)+f(x)f(1), \forall x,y \in \mathbb{R}.
$$

 From that, we can easily obtain that $f(1)=1$ by choosing $x \ne y$. Then $f$ is a multiplicative and additive function, so $\boxed{f(x)=x, \forall x \in \mathbb{R}.}$
