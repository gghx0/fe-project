Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

We claim that the solutions are exactly

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\ge0,\\
x\varepsilon(x),&x<0,
\end{cases}}
$$

where $\varepsilon:(-\infty,0)\to\{-1,1\}$ is arbitrary.

First note that

$$
f(f(x^2+f(y)^2))=f(f(x)^2+y^2)=x^2+f(y)^2.
$$

Thus $f(f(t))=t$ for every sufficiently large $t$.

Now fix $x$. For all sufficiently large $y$, we may compare

$$
P(f(y),x)
$$

and

$$
P(f(x),y).
$$

The left sides are the same, and the first equation uses $f(f(y))=y$, so we get

$$
x^2+y^2=f(f(x))^2+y^2.
$$

Hence

$$
f(f(x))^2=x^2
$$

for every $x$. In particular, $f(f(0))=0$.

From $P(0,f(0))$ we get $f(0)=2f(0)^2$, so $f(0)\in\{0,\frac12\}$. If $f(0)=\frac12$, then $f(\frac12)=0$, and

$$
P\left(1,\frac12\right)
$$

gives $f(1)=f(1)^2+\frac14$, so $f(1)=\frac12$. Then $P(0,1)$ gives $f(\frac14)=\frac54$, while $P(0,0)$ gives $f(\frac14)=\frac14$, a contradiction. Thus $f(0)=0$.

Now $P(x,f(y))$ gives

$$
f(x^2+y^2)=f(x)^2+f(y)^2.
$$

By the $f(0)=0$ case of FE-0182, either $f\equiv0$, or $f(x)=x$ for $x\ge0$ and $f(x)\in\{x,-x\}$ for $x<0$. The zero function does not satisfy the present equation, so only the stated family remains. Direct substitution verifies every function in this family.
