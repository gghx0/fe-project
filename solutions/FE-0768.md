The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\boxed{f(x)=0}.
$$

Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(xf(y)+f(xy))=xf(y)+yf(x).
$$

If $f(1)\ne0$, then $P(1,x)$ gives

$$
f(2f(x))=f(x)+xf(1),
$$

so $f$ is injective. Comparing $P(x,y)$ and $P(y,x)$, whose right sides are equal, injectivity gives

$$
xf(y)+f(xy)=yf(x)+f(xy),
$$

so $xf(y)=yf(x)$. Taking $y=1$ gives $f(x)=xf(1)$. Substitution then gives $f(1)=1$, so $f(x)=x$.

Now suppose $f(1)=0$. If $f\not\equiv0$, choose $j$ with $f(j)\ne0$. From $P(-1,0)$ we get $f(0)=0$, and from $P(x,1)$,

$$
f(f(x))=f(x).
$$

Next,

$$
P\left(\frac1{f(j)},f(j)\right)
$$

gives $f(1/f(j))=-1/f(j)$, and hence $f(-1/f(j))=-1/f(j)$. Then $P(-1,1/f(j))$ gives $f(-1)=-1$, while $P(-1,-1/f(j))$ gives $f(-1)=1$, a contradiction. Therefore $f\equiv0$ in this case.

Both listed functions are directly checked.
