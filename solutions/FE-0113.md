***

The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x},
$$

and, for any constant $c\le0$,

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\ge c,\\
-x,&x<c,
\end{cases}}
$$

or

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x>c,\\
-x,&x\le c.
\end{cases}}
$$

First, if $f(a)=f(b)$, then comparing $P(x,a)$ and $P(x,b)$ gives $a^2=b^2$.

Putting $x=y=0$ gives

$$
f(f(0)+f(0)^2)=f(0),
$$

so $f(0)+f(0)^2=0$, and therefore $f(0)\in\{0,-1\}$.

Suppose first that $f(0)=0$. From $P(x,0)$,

$$
f(f(x))=f(x),
$$

so the first observation gives $f(x)=\pm x$ for each $x$.

Now $f(y)^2=y^2$, so the equation says

$$
f(f(x)+y^2)=f(x)+y^2.
$$

Thus every number of the form $f(x)+y^2$ belongs to the set where $f(t)=t$. Hence this set is a ray, either all of $\mathbb R$, $[c,\infty)$, or $(c,\infty)$, with $c\le0$ because $0$ is fixed. This gives exactly the listed families.

It remains to exclude $f(0)=-1$. In this case, $P(x,0)$ gives

$$
f(f(x)+1)=f(x),
$$

so $|f(x)+1|=|x|$ for every $x$. Hence $f(x)=x-1$ or $f(x)=-x-1$ pointwise. Taking $x=0$ in the original equation gives

$$
f(f(y)^2-1)=y^2-1.
$$

But $f(y)^2-1=y^2\pm2y$, and applying $f(t)=\pm t-1$ to this expression cannot equal $y^2-1$ for all real $y$. This contradiction removes the case $f(0)=-1$.

The listed functions are then directly checked.
