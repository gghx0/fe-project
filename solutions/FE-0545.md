We claim that the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=|x|}.
$$

Both work. First, if $f(a)=f(b)$, then comparing $P(a,1)$ and $P(b,1)$ gives $a^2=b^2$.

Putting $x=y=0$ gives

$$
f(2f(0)^2)=f(0).
$$

Using the previous observation with the inputs $2f(0)^2$ and $0$ gives $f(0)=0$. Similarly, any zero of $f$ must be $0$.

From $P(x,0)$,

$$
f(f(x)^2)=x^2.
$$

Thus $[0,\infty)\subseteq f(\mathbb{R})$, and

$$
f(x^4)=f(x)^4.
$$

Putting $x=1$ gives $f(1)=1$. Then

$$
P(1,x):\quad f(f(x)+1)=f(x)+1.
$$

Since $f(x)+1$ takes every value at least $1$, we get $f(t)=t$ for all $t\ge1$. For $0<t<1$, choose $y$ large enough that $y>1$, $yt>1$, and $y^2+yf(t)>1$. Then $P(y,t)$ gives $f(t)=t$. Hence

$$
f(t)=t\qquad(t\ge0).
$$

Now $f(f(x)^2)=x^2$ implies $f(x)^2=x^2$, so $f(x)\in\{x,-x\}$ for all $x$.

If $f(-k)=k$ for some $k>0$, then comparing $P(x,k)$ and $P(x,-k)$ gives $f(xk)=f(-xk)$ for all $x$, so $f$ is even and therefore $f(x)=|x|$. Otherwise every negative input is fixed, and $f(x)=x$.
