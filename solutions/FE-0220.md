Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

Let

$$
c=\inf_{t\ge0} f(t).
$$

First, $c\ge0$. Indeed, if some $k\ge0$ had $f(k)<0$, then putting

$$
x=\sqrt{k-f(k)},\qquad y=\sqrt{k}
$$

would give

$$
f(k)=k,
$$

a contradiction.

The equation says that $f(u)=u$ for every

$$
u\ge f(y^2)
$$

for some $y$. Therefore $f(x)=x$ for all $x>c$, and also at $x=c$ exactly when the infimum is attained by a value $f(y^2)$.

Hence the complete solution set is the union of the following two families:

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\ge c,\\
g(x),&0\le x<c,\\
h(x),&x<0,
\end{cases}}
$$

where $c\ge0$, $g:[0,c)\to[c,\infty)$ is arbitrary, and $h:(-\infty,0)\to\mathbb{R}$ is arbitrary; and

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x>c,\\
g(x),&0\le x\le c,\\
h(x),&x<0,
\end{cases}}
$$

where $c\ge0$, $g:[0,c]\to(c,\infty)$ is arbitrary, and $h:(-\infty,0)\to\mathbb{R}$ is arbitrary.

It is immediate to check that every function in either family satisfies

$$
f(x^2+f(y^2))=x^2+f(y^2),
$$

because the input $x^2+f(y^2)$ always lies in the identity region.
