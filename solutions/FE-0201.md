The answer is $f(x)=x$ for nonnegative $x$ and $f(-x)=\pm f(x)=\pm x (x>0)$,which can easily be seen to work.

Denote the given assertion with $P(x,y)$.

Notice that $P(0,0) \implies f(f(0)^2)=0$.Let $t=f(0)^2$ 

Claim 1: $f(x)=x+t^2$ for nonnegative $x$

Proof: $P(x \ge 0,t)$ gives the desired result.

Claim 2: $t=0$ which also means $f(0)=0$

Proof: Since the term inside the function in LHS is nonnegative,after setting $y$ nonnegative we get $x^2+y^2=f(x^2+f(y)^2)=x^2+f(y)^2+t^2=x^2+(y+t)^2+t^2=x^2+y^2+2yt+2t^2 \implies 2yt+2t^2=0 \implies t=0$.

From the two claims we get $f(x)=x$ for nonnegative $x$.

Claim 3: $f(a)=\pm a$ for negative $a$.

Proof:Notice that 

$$
f(x^2+f(-a)^2)=x^2+a^2=f(x^2+f(a)^2)
$$

 and from injectivity for nonnegative reals we get $x^2+f(-a)^2=x^2+f(a)^2 \implies f(a)=\pm f(-a)=\pm a$.

From the three claims we get our characterized solution, done.$\blacksquare$
