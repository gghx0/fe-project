We claim the solutions are given by the following: Either $f(x)=\frac12$ for all $x$, or let $S$ be a subfield of $\mathbb R$. Let $\{a_i\}$ be a Hamel basis of $\mathbb R$ over $S$. Then for each $a_i$, force $f(a_i)\in S$ and for $k_i\in S$ we have $f\left(\sum k_ia_i\right)=\sum k_if(a_i)$. To see that these work, if $f(x)=\frac12$ then both sides are $\frac12$, otherwise we will get that $f$ is additive, has range $\subseteq S$, and that $f(kx)=kf(x)$ for any $k\in S$, hence $f(xf(x)+yf(x))=f(x+y)f(x)=f(x)^2+f(x)f(y)$ as desired.

Now we show these are the only solutions. Denote the assertion by $P(x,y)$. Taking $P(0,0)$ gives $f(0)=2f(0)^2$, so $f(0)=\frac12$ or $f(0)=0$. Taking $P(x,-x)$ and $P(-x,x)$ give $f(0)=f(x)^2+f(x)f(-x)=f(-x)^2+f(x)f(-x)$, hence $f(x)=\pm f(-x)$ and hence $f(0)=0$ or $2f(x)^2$. If $f(0)=\frac12$, then we get $f(x)=\pm\frac12$ for all $x$, however $f(x)=-\frac12$ fails by $P(0,y)$. Hence we get the $f(x)=\frac12$ solution here.

Otherwise, $f(0)=0$. Then $P(x,-x)+P(-x,x)$ gives $(f(x)+f(-x))^2=0$, hence $f$ is odd.

Suppose there exist $a,b$ such that $f(a)=f(b)$. Taking $P(a,k-a)$ and $P(b,k-b)$ gives $f(kf(a))=f(a)^2+f(a)f(k-a)=f(b)^2+f(b)f(k-b)$, hence $f(a)=f(b)=0$ or $f(k-a)=f(k-b)$, the latter of which implies $f$ is periodic with period $a-b$.

Take $P(x,0)$ to get $f(xf(x))=f(x)^2$. Now suppose that $f(r)=0$ for some $r$. Then if $f(x)\ne 0$ we get from $P(x,r)$ that $f(xf(x)+rf(x))=f(x)^2=f(xf(x))\ne 0$, hence $rf(x)$ is a period of $f$ for all $x$.

Suppose $f(x)\ne 0$. Note that if we set $xf(x)+yf(x)=x$, then from $P(x,y)$ we get $f(x)+f(y)=1$. Hence the range of $f$ is symmetric over $\frac12$, except one is not necessarily in the range.

Suppose now that $f$ is not constantly zero, so pick $x$ with $f(x)\ne 0$. Then if $r$ is a root of $f$, then $rf(x)$ is a period, but $1-f(x)$ is also in the range, hence $r-rf(x)$ is also a period. Thus $r$ is a period. In particular $f(nr)=0$ for all $n\in\mathbb Z$.

Now take $P(x,-2x)$ to get $f(-xf(x))=f(x)^2+f(x)f(-2x)$, but by oddness this becomes $-f(xf(x))=f(x)^2-f(x)f(2x)$. We have $f(xf(x))=f(x)^2$, so plugging in we get either $f(2x)=2f(x)$ or $f(x)=0$. However if $f(x)=0$ then $f(2x)=0$ by above, hence $f(2x)=2f(x)$ always. In particular, if $k$ is in the range, then so are $2k$ and $\frac k2$.

Let $S$ be the range of $f$. We claim $S$ is a field. To do this, suppose $a,b\ne 0\in S$. We can find $x,y$ with $f(x)=b$ and $f(xf(x)+yf(x))=a$, since the inside of the latter spans all real numbers. This then gives $f(y)=\frac ab-b$. Next, note that $2b\in S$, and taking $f(x)=2b,f(y)=\frac ab-b$ gives $f(xf(x)+yf(x))=2a+2b^2$. Hence half of this which is $a+b^2$ is also in $S$. Now we can find $x,y$ with $f(x)=b$ and $f(xf(x)+yf(x))=a+b^2$, which gives $f(y)=\frac ab$ upon plugging in. Hence $S$ is closed under division.

In particular $a=b$ gives $1\in S$, hence $a=1$ gives $\frac1b\in S$, and then we have $\frac a{1/b}=ab\in S$, hence $S$ is closed under multiplication as well. Additionally, $S$ is closed under addition since when $a,b\ne 0$ we get that for $f(x)=a,f(y)=b$ we have $a+b=\frac{f(xf(x)+yf(x))}{f(x)}\in S$. From $f$ odd we get $S$ is closed under subtraction, hence $S$ is a field as desired.

Suppose now that $b$ is in the range, with $f(a)=b$, and that $b\ne 1$. Taking $P(a,y)$ gives $f(ab+by)=b^2+bf(y)$. Next $P\left(a,\frac ab-a\right)$ gives $f\left(\frac ab-a\right)=1-b$.

Let $c$ be such that $f(c)=1$. Then $P(c,y)$ gives $f(c+y)=1+f(y)$. Hence, setting $y=-a$ gives $f(c-a)=1+f(-a)=1-f(a)=1-b$. Hence from above we have that $c-\frac ab$ is a period of $f$, hence $1+f(y)=f(c+y)=f\left(\frac ab+y\right)$.

Now $P\left(a,\frac ab+y\right)$ gives $f(ab+a+by)=b^2+b+f(y)$. However this equals $f(ab+by)+b$. Since $ab+by$ takes on any real value, we get $f(x+a)=f(x)+b$ for all $x$. Hence $f(ab+by)=b^2+bf(y)=bf(a+y)$. Since $a+y$ takes on any real value, we thus have that for any $k\in S$ we have $f(kx)=kf(x)$.

Hence when $f(x)\ne 0$ we get $f(xf(x)+yf(x))=f(x+y)f(x)=f(x)^2+f(x)f(y)$, so $f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)$. This is true when $f(x)=0$ as well, since then $x$ is a period. Hence $f$ is additive.

Thus if we take some Hamel basis $\{a_i\}$ of $\mathbb R$ over $S$, we see that $f(a_i)\in S$ by definition of $S$, and that $f\left(\sum k_ia_i\right)=\sum f(k_ia_i)=\sum k_if(a_i)$, giving our solution set as desired.
