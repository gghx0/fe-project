We claim that the complete solution set is precisely the family of functions obtained as follows. Let $T$ be a set of real numbers, and for each $t\in T$, let $B_t\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ be a set such that the collection ${B_t-t:t\in T}$ is a partition of $\mathbb{R}$. Let $S=\bigcup_{t\in T}B_t$, and suppose that $a\in B_t,\ b\in S\Longrightarrow a(1+b)\in B_t$ for every $t\in T$. Define $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ by $f(x)=x+t$ whenever $x\in B_t-t$. Then $f$ satisfies $f(x+f(x)f(y))=f(x)+f(x)f(y)$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$, and every solution arises in exactly this way. We now prove this characterization. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion $f(x+f(x)f(y))=f(x)+f(x)f(y)$.

**Lemma 1.** For a function $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$, define $g(x)=f(x)-x$. Then $P(x,y)$ is equivalent to $g(x+f(x)f(y))=g(x)$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$.

*Proof.* We have

$$
\begin{aligned} 
g(x+f(x)f(y)) &=f(x+f(x)f(y))-(x+f(x)f(y))\\ 
&=f(x)+f(x)f(y)-x-f(x)f(y)\\ 
&=f(x)-x\\ 
&=g(x). 
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $P(x,y)$ implies the stated identity. Reversing the calculation shows that the stated identity implies $P(x,y)$. Hence the two conditions are equivalent. $\square$

**Lemma 2.** Let $T=g(\mathbb{R})$, and for each $t\in T$ define $A_t={x\in\mathbb{R}:g(x)=t}$. Then the sets $A_t$ form a partition of $\mathbb{R}$, and for every $x\in A_t$, $f(x)=x+t$.

*Proof.* Since $T$ is the image of $g$, every $x\in\mathbb{R}$ belongs to at least one set $A_t$. Since $g(x)$ has a unique value, $x$ belongs to exactly one such set. Thus the $A_t$ form a partition of $\mathbb{R}$. If $x\in A_t$, then $g(x)=t$, so $f(x)-x=t$, and therefore $f(x)=x+t$. $\square$

**Lemma 3.** For each $t\in T$, define $B_t=f(A_t)$. Then $A_t=B_t-t$, and if $a\in B_t$ and $b\in f(\mathbb{R})$, then $a(1+b)\in B_t$.

*Proof.* Let $a\in B_t$. Then there exists $x\in A_t$ such that $f(x)=a$. Since $x\in A_t$, Lemma $2$ gives $f(x)=x+t$, so $a=x+t$, and hence $x=a-t$. Thus $B_t-t\subseteq A_t$. Conversely, if $x\in A_t$, then $f(x)\in B_t$, and Lemma $2$ gives $x=f(x)-t$, so $x\in B_t-t$. Hence $A_t=B_t-t$. Now let $a\in B_t$ and $b\in f(\mathbb{R})$. Choose $x\in A_t$ and $y\in\mathbb{R}$ such that $f(x)=a,\qquad f(y)=b$. By Lemma $1$, $g(x+f(x)f(y))=g(x)$, so $g(x+ab)=t$. Thus $x+ab\in A_t$. Applying $f$ and using $P(x,y)$,

$$
\begin{aligned} 
f(x+ab) &=f(x)+f(x)f(y)\\ 
&=a+ab\\ 
&=a(1+b). 
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $x+ab\in A_t$, its image belongs to $B_t$. Therefore $a(1+b)\in B_t$. $\square$

**Lemma 4.** Every solution $f$ can be reconstructed from the sets $B_t$ of Lemma $3$ by $f(x)=x+t\forall x\in B_t-t$.

*Proof.* By Lemma $3$, the sets $B_t-t$ are exactly the sets $A_t$, and these sets partition $\mathbb{R}$. If $x\in B_t-t=A_t$, then Lemma $2$ gives $f(x)=x+t$. Thus the original function is completely determined by the family $(B_t)_{t\in T}$. $\square$

**Lemma 5.** Conversely, suppose that $T$ and the sets $B_t$ satisfy ${B_t-t:t\in T}$ is a partition of $\mathbb{R}$ and $a\in B_t,\ b\in S:=\bigcup_{t\in T}B_t\Longrightarrow a(1+b)\in B_t$. Define $f$ by $f(x)=x+t$ whenever $x\in B_t-t$. Then $f$ satisfies $P(x,y)$.

*Proof.* Because the sets $B_t-t$ form a partition of $\mathbb{R}$, the definition of $f$ is well-defined. Fix arbitrary $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$. Let $t\in T$ be the unique element such that $x\in B_t-t$. Then there exists $a\in B_t$ such that $x=a-t$. Hence, by definition, $f(x)=a$. Since $f(y)\in S$, the assumed closure property gives $a(1+f(y))\in B_t$. Therefore $a(1+f(y))-t\in B_t-t$. But, note that

$$
\begin{aligned} 
a(1+f(y))-t &=a+af(y)-t\\ 
&=x+f(x)f(y). 
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $x+f(x)f(y)\in B_t-t$. By the definition of $f$,

$$
\begin{aligned} 
f(x+f(x)f(y)) &=x+f(x)f(y)+t\\ 
&=(x+t)+f(x)f(y)\\ 
&=f(x)+f(x)f(y). 
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence $P(x,y)$ holds for every $x,y\in\mathbb{R}$. $\square$

Therefore the complete solution set is

$$
\boxed{ f(x)=x+t\forall x\in B_t-t, }
$$

where ${B_t-t:t\in T}$ is a partition of $\mathbb{R}$ and

$$
\boxed{ a\in B_t,\ b\in\bigcup_{t\in T}B_t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a(1+b)\in B_t. }
$$

Note that this characterization is necessary and sufficient, so it describes every solution and no others as desired. $\blacksquare$
