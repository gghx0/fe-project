The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

If $f(k)=0$ for some $k\ne0$, then $P(k,y)$ gives $f(ky)=0$ for all $y$, so $f\equiv0$.

Now suppose that $f(x)\ne0$ for every $x\ne0$. From $P(-f(0),0)$ we get

$$
f(f(-f(0))^2)=0,
$$

so $f(0)=0$. Also

$$
P(x,0):\qquad f(f(x)^2)=xf(x).
$$

Hence, if $f(a)=f(b)\ne0$, then $af(a)=bf(a)$, so $a=b$. In particular, $f(1)^2=1$.

If $f(1)=1$, then $P(1,y)$ gives $f(y+1)=f(y)+1$. Comparing $P(x,y+1)$ with $P(x,y)$ gives

$$
f(f(x)^2+xy+x)=f(f(x)^2+xy)+f(x).
$$

For $x\ne0$, the input $f(x)^2+xy$ ranges over all real numbers, so

$$
f(t+x)=f(t)+f(x)
$$

for all real $t$ and all $x\ne0$. Thus $f$ is additive. The original equation and $P(x,0)$ then give

$$
f(xy)=f(x)f(y),
$$

so $f$ is also multiplicative. Since $f(1)=1$, the usual additive and multiplicative argument gives $f(x)=x$.

If $f(1)=-1$, then $P(1,y)$ gives

$$
f(y+1)=-f(y)-1,
$$

so $f(y+2)=f(y)$. In particular $f(3)=f(1)\ne0$, contradicting the injectivity on nonzero values. Therefore only the two listed functions remain, and both work.
