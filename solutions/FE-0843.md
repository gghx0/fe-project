Let

$$
P(x,y):\qquad f(2f(xy))=f(x)f(y)+xy.
$$

The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=-x}.
$$

If $f(1)=0$, then $P(x,x)$ and $P(x^2,1)$ give

$$
f(2f(x^2))=f(x)^2+x^2
$$

and

$$
f(2f(x^2))=x^2.
$$

Thus $f(x)=0$ for all $x$, which is not a solution. Hence $f(1)\ne0$.

Put $c=f(1)$ and

$$
g(x)=\frac{f(x)}c.
$$

Comparing $P(x,y)$ with $P(xy,1)$ gives

$$
cf(xy)=f(x)f(y),
$$

so

$$
g(xy)=g(x)g(y). \tag{1}
$$

Let

$$
a=f\left(\frac1{2c}\right).
$$

From $P(x,1)$,

$$
f(2f(x))=cf(x)+x.
$$

Multiplying by $a$ and using $cf(uv)=f(u)f(v)$ gives

$$
g(g(x))=ag(x)+bx, \tag{2}
$$

where

$$
b=\frac a{c^2}.
$$

Since $g$ is nonconstant and multiplicative,

$$
g(0)=0,\qquad g(1)=1.
$$

Putting $x=1$ in (2),

$$
b=1-a,
$$

so

$$
g(g(x))=ag(x)+(1-a)x. \tag{3}
$$

Also $a\ne0,1$. If $a=0$, then $b=0$, contradicting $a+b=1$. If $a=1$, then $b=0$, contradicting $b=a/c^2$.

For $x\ne0$, multiplicativity gives $g(x)\ne0$ and

$$
g\left(\frac1x\right)=\frac1{g(x)}.
$$

Applying (3) to $1/x$ gives

$$
\frac1{ag(x)+(1-a)x}
=
\frac a{g(x)}+\frac{1-a}x.
$$

Clearing denominators,

$$
a(1-a)(x-g(x))^2=0.
$$

Thus $g(x)=x$ for every $x$, so $f(x)=cx$.

Testing $f(x)=cx$ in the original equation gives

$$
2c^2xy=(c^2+1)xy,
$$

hence $c^2=1$. Therefore

$$
f(x)=x
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
f(x)=-x.
$$

Both are directly checked.
