$f\left(f(x)^2+f(x)f(y)\right)=x^2+xy$.

$P(1,x)\Rightarrow f\left(f(1)^2+f(1)f(x)\right)=1+x$, so $f$ is bijective.

Let $f(k)=0$:

$P(k,x)\Rightarrow f(0)=k^2+kx$, so $k=0$ and therefore $f(0)=0$.

$P(x,0)\Rightarrow f\left(f(x)^2\right)=x^2$ from which we obtain:

$$
f(x)^4=f\left(f\left(f(x)^2\right)^2\right)=f\left(x^4\right).
$$

In particular, $f(1)=f(1)^4$, and since $f(1)\ne f(0)$ we have $f(1)=1$.

$P(1,x)$ now becomes $f(1+f(x))=1+x$, so as before:

$$
2+f(x)=f(1+f(1+f(x)))=f(2+x).
$$

$$
P(x,y+2)\Rightarrow f\left(f(x)^2+f(x)f(y)+2f(x)\right)=x^2+xy+2x=f\left(f(x)^2+f(x)f(y)\right)+2x.
$$

For $x\ne0$, $f(x)^2+f(x)f(y)$ can take any value (by surjectivity) so:

$$
Q(x,y):f(y+2f(x))=f(y)+2x
$$

for all $x\ne0$, but it's also true for $x=0$.

$Q(x,0)\Rightarrow f(2f(x))=2x$.

$Q(2f(x),0)\Rightarrow f(4x)=4f(x)$.

$Q(2f(x),y)\Rightarrow f(4x+y)=f(4x)+f(y)$, so $f$ is additive.

$P(2f(x),0)\Rightarrow f\left(4x^2\right)=4f(x)^2$.

$P(2f(x),2f(y))\Rightarrow f\left(4x^2\right)+f(4xy)=4f(x)^2+4f(x)f(y)$.

Using the fact that $f(4x)=4f(x)$, we have that $f$ is multiplicative, and since we already established $f\not\equiv0$ we must have $\boxed{f(x)=x}$.
