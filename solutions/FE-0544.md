We claim that the unique solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

From $P(1,x)$,

$$
f(f(1)^2+f(1)f(x))=1+x,
$$

so $f$ is bijective and $f(1)\ne0$. Let $f(k)=0$. Then $P(k,x)$ gives

$$
f(0)=k^2+kx
$$

for all $x$, hence $k=0$ and $f(0)=0$.

From $P(x,0)$,

$$
f(f(x)^2)=x^2.
$$

Therefore

$$
f(x)^4=f(x^4).
$$

Putting $x=1$ and using $f(1)\ne0$ gives $f(1)=1$. Hence

$$
P(1,x):\quad f(1+f(x))=1+x.
$$

Applying this twice gives

$$
f(x+2)=f(x)+2.
$$

Now $P(x,y+2)-P(x,y)$ gives

$$
f(f(x)^2+f(x)f(y)+2f(x))-f(f(x)^2+f(x)f(y))=2x.
$$

Since $f$ is surjective, for $x\ne0$ the input $f(x)^2+f(x)f(y)$ spans all real numbers. Thus

$$
f(t+2f(x))=f(t)+2x
$$

for all $t$ and all $x$. Taking $t=0$ gives

$$
f(2f(x))=2x.
$$

Then

$$
f(4x)=4f(x),
$$

and substituting into the shift identity gives additivity. The original equation then yields multiplicativity. Since $f(1)=1$, an additive and multiplicative real function is nonnegative on squares and therefore order-preserving; hence it is the identity. Thus

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$
