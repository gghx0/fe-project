Let $P(x, y)$ denote the functional equation $f(x + f(y)) = f(x) + f(y)$. Recall that #7 is the FE $P(x, y)$ for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$. Then the functions satisfying $P(x, y^2)$ for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$ are either those satisfying #7 or those satisfying $f(y) = 0$ for all $y \geq 0$.

It is easy to check that both works. Now suppose that $f(c) \neq 0$ for some $c \geq 0$. First, by induction on $k$, we get $$f(x + kf(c)) = f(x) + kf(c)$$ for any $x \in \mathbb{R}$ and $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Now for any $y \in \mathbb{R}$, since $f(c) \neq 0$, there exists an integer $k$ such that $y + kf(c) \geq 0$. Then for any $x \in \mathbb{R}$,
$$ f(x + f(y)) = f(x - kf(c) + f(y + kf(c))) = f(x - kf(c)) + f(y + kf(c)) = f(x) + f(y). $$
This proves that $P(x, y)$ holds for all $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$.
