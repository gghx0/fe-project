I claim that the solution is $\boxed{f(x) = x}$, verifying is easy.
Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion. Taking $P(x,0)$ gives
$$
f(f(x)) = x
$$
meaning $f$ is an involution. Thus $f$ is surjective and injective. $P(f(x), y)$ gives
$$
f(f(f(x))+y^2) = f(x)+y^2 \iff f(x+y^2) = f(x)+y^2.
$$
Let $a<b$ be two arbitrary reals, $P(a, \sqrt{b-a})$ gives
$$
f(a+b-a) = f(a)+(b-a) \iff f(b)-b = f(a)-a.
$$
Thus $f(x)-x$ is constant for all reals, and so $f(x) = x+c$. Substituting this back into the original equation gives $f(x) = x$ as desired.
