Either

$$
f(x)=0
$$

or

$$
f(x)=x
$$

for all $x$.

If

$$
f(0)\ne0,
$$

then from

$$
P\left(0,\frac{z}{f(0)}-f(0)\right),
$$

we get

$$
f(z)=f(0)
$$

for all $z$. But the only constant that solves the equation is zero, a contradiction. So

$$
f(0)=0.
$$

Suppose

$$
f(u)\ne u
$$

for some $u\ne0$. Then pick $v$ such that

$$
uv=f(u)^2+v\cdot f(u).
$$

Then $P(u,v)$ gives

$$
u\cdot f(u)=0,
$$

and thus

$$
f(u)=0.
$$

It follows from

$$
P\left(u,\frac{z}{u}\right)
$$

that

$$
f(z)=0
$$

for all $z$.
