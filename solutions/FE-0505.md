Solution by **Midmogus**.
***

The unique solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

Let $t=f(0)$. From $P(0,y)$,

$$
f(t^2)=t.
$$

Suppose $f(r)=t$. Comparing $P(x,0)$ and $P(x,r)$ gives

$$
f(rx)=t
$$

for every $x$. If $r\ne0$, then $f$ is constant, impossible. Thus $r=0$ is the only preimage of $t$. Since $f(t^2)=t$, we get $t^2=0$, so

$$
f(0)=0.
$$

Now

$$
P(x,0):\qquad f(f(x)^2)=x^2,
$$

so $f$ takes every nonnegative value. To see that it also takes every negative value, fix $s<0$ and choose $x<0$ with $x^2=-s$. Since $f$ takes all positive values, choose $y$ with

$$
f(y)=-\frac{f(x)^2}{x}.
$$

Then $f(x)^2+xf(y)=0$, and $P(x,y)$ gives $0=x^2+f(xy)$, so $f(xy)=s$. Hence $f$ is surjective.

Using $P(1,y)$ and surjectivity, for every real $z$ we have

$$
f(z)=z+1-f(1)^2.
$$

Thus $f(z)=z+k$ for a constant $k$. Substituting this into the original equation gives

$$
(x+k)^2+x(y+k)+k=x^2+xy+k,
$$

so $k(3x+k)=0$ for all $x$. Hence $k=0$, and $f(x)=x$.
