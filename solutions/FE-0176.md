Solution by **AshAuktober**.
***

$$
x=0:\qquad f(y^2)=yf(y). \tag{1}
$$

Also, $f$ is odd.

Now,

$$
f(x^2+y^2)=x^2+f(y^2),
$$

and, by symmetry,

$$
f(x^2+y^2)=y^2+f(x^2).
$$

Hence

$$
f(x^2)-x^2=f(y^2)-y^2.
$$

Thus $f(x^2)-x^2$ is constant; say

$$
f(x^2)-x^2=c.
$$

Therefore, for $x\ge0$,

$$
f(x)=x+c.
$$

Since $f(x)\ge0$ for $x\ge0$, we have $c\ge0$.

Substituting $f(x)=x+c$ into (1), for $y\ge0$,

$$
y^2+c=y(y+c),
$$

so

$$
c(y-1)=c.
$$

In particular, taking $y=0$ gives $c=0$.

Hence

$$
f(x)=x\qquad\forall x\ge0.
$$

Since $f$ is odd, it follows that

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb R}.
$$
