Let $P(x,y)$ be the given assertion

$$
f\bigl(f(x^2)+xf(y)\bigr)=x^2+xf(y).
$$

From $P(0,y)$, we get

$$
f(f(0))=0.
$$

Let $a=f(0)$. Then $f(a)=0$.

Now $P(x,a)$ gives

$$
f(f(x^2))=x^2.
$$

Since $x^2$ takes every nonnegative real value, we have

$$
f(f(t))=t
$$

for every $t\geq 0$. In particular, every nonnegative real number is in

the range of $f$:

$$
[0,\infty)\subseteq f(\mathbb R).
$$

We now prove that $f$ is surjective. Let $r\in\mathbb R$.

If $r\geq 1$, then $r-1\geq0$, so there is some $y\in\mathbb R$ such

that

$$
f(y)=r-1.
$$

Using $P(1,y)$, we obtain

$$
f\bigl(f(1)+r-1\bigr)=1+r-1=r.
$$

If $r\leq1$, then $1-r\geq0$, so there is some $y\in\mathbb R$ such

that

$$
f(y)=1-r.
$$

Using $P(-1,y)$, we obtain

$$
f\bigl(f(1)-(1-r)\bigr)=1-(1-r)=r.
$$

Therefore every real number belongs to the range of $f$, so $f$ is

surjective.

Using surjectivity in $P(1,y)$, the number $f(y)$ may be any real

number. Hence, for every $u\in\mathbb R$,

$$
f\bigl(f(1)+u\bigr)=1+u.
$$

Let

$$
z=f(1)+u.
$$

Then $u=z-f(1)$, and therefore

$$
f(z)=1+z-f(1)=z+C,
$$

where

$$
C=1-f(1).
$$

Thus

$$
f(x)=x+C
$$

for every real $x$.

In particular,

$$
f(0)=C
$$

and hence

$$
f(f(0))=f(C)=2C.
$$

But $P(0,y)$ gave $f(f(0))=0$. Therefore

$$
2C=0,
$$

so $C=0$. Consequently,

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}
$$

for every $x\in\mathbb R$.

Finally, it's easy to check that this function indeed satisfies the equation. Therefore the unique

solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb R}.
$$
