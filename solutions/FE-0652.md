The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\boxed{f(x)=-x}.
$$

The function is nonconstant. Choose $a$ with $f(a)\ne0$. Then $P(a,y)$ shows that $f$ is surjective, and comparing $P(a,u)$ and $P(a,v)$ shows that $f$ is injective.

From $P(x,0)$ and $P(-x,0)$,

$$
f(x)f(0)=f(-x)f(0).
$$

If $f(0)\ne0$, injectivity gives $x=-x$ for all $x$, impossible. Hence $f(0)=0$, and

$$
f(f(x^2))=x^2. \tag{1}
$$

Comparing $P(x,-x)$ and $P(-x,x)$ gives $f(-x)=-f(x)$, so $f$ is odd. Thus (1) implies

$$
f(f(t))=t
$$

for every real $t$.

Now compare $P(f(x),x)$ and $P(x,f(x))$. The right sides are both $f(x)^2+x^2$, so injectivity gives

$$
f(f(x)^2)=f(x^2).
$$

Applying injectivity once more yields

$$
f(x)^2=x^2. \tag{2}
$$

Squaring the original equation and using (2), we get

$$
\bigl(f(x^2)+f(x)f(y)\bigr)^2=\bigl(x^2+yf(x)\bigr)^2.
$$

After cancellation,

$$
f(x^2)f(x)f(y)=x^2yf(x).
$$

For $x\ne0$, divide by $f(x)$ and then put $x=1$:

$$
f(1)f(y)=y.
$$

Since $f(1)=\pm1$, we get $f(y)=y$ for all $y$, or $f(y)=-y$ for all $y$. Both functions work.
