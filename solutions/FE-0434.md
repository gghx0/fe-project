We claim the solutions are $f(x)=0$ and $f(x)=x$, which we can see work.

Denote the assertion by $P(x,y)$. Note that $P(-f(0),0)$ gives $f(f(0)^2)=0$, and $P(0,0)$ gives $f(f(0)^2)=2f(0)$, hence $f(0)=0$. Thus $P(x,0)$ gives $f(xf(x))=f(x^2)$.

Suppose $f(a)=f(b)$. Comparing $P(a,y)$ and $P(b,y)$ gives $f(ay)=f(by)$ for any $y$. Hence, by taking $a=xf(x),b=x^2,y=\frac1x$ we get $f(f(x))=f(x)$. In particular $f(yf(x))=f(xy)$ for all $x,y$.

Note that $f(f(x)^2)=f(xf(x))=f(x^2)$. Hence comparing $P(x,y)$ with $P(f(x),y)$ we get $f(xf(x)+f(x)f(y))=f(f(x)^2+f(x)f(y))$. By scaling both insides by $\frac1{f(x)}$, we get $f(x+f(y))=f(f(x)+f(y))$, which by symmetry equals $f(f(x)+y)$. Scaling back up by $f(x)$ inside gives that $f(f(x)^2+yf(x))=f(xf(x)+f(x)f(y))=f(x^2)+f(xy)$. However from $f(yf(x))=f(xy)$ we get $f(f(x)^2+yf(x))=f(xf(x)+xy)$.

Note that if $f(x)=0$ and $x\ne 0$ then from $f(yf(x))=f(xy)$ we get $f$ is constantly zero, so assume $f$ is injective at zero. In our previous equation, force $xf(x)+xy=x^2$ when $x\ne 0$, which implies $f(xy)=0$, hence $xy=0$ and thus $y=x-f(x)=0$, so $f(x)=x$ as desired.
