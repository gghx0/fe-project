The answer is $f(x)=x$ only, which clearly works. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion.

If $f(t)=0$, compare $P(0,t)$ and $P(t,t)$ to get $t^2=2t^2$, hence $t=0$. Then from $P(0,0)$, we get that $f(f(0)^2)=0$, hence $f(0)^2=0$ and $f(0)=0$.

Now suppose that $f(a)=f(b)$ but $a \neq b$. Compare $P(0,a)$ with $P(0,b)$ to get that $a^2=b^2$, hence $b=-a$. Then compare $P(a,1)$ with $P(-a,1)$ to get $f(-af(a)+f(1)^2)=f(af(a)+f(1))^2$. Since $a,f(a) \neq 0$, we can't have $-af(a)+f(1)^2=af(a)+f(1)^2$, but since $f(1)^2 \neq 0$, we can't have $-af(a)+f(1)^2=-(af(a)+f(1)^2)$ either. Therefore $f$ is injective.

Comparing $P(x,0)$ and $P(0,x)$ therefore gives us $xf(x)=f(x)^2$. If $x \neq 0$, then $f(x) \neq 0$ and we have $f(x)=x$. We already know that $f(0)=0$, so combining these yields $f(x)=x$ as the only solution.
