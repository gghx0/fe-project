We claim the answers are $f(x)=0$ or $f(x)=x$ for all $x$, which work.

Denote the assertion by $P(x,y)$. First $P(0,y)$ gives $f(f(0))=f(0)f(y)$, so $f$ is constant, which implies $f(x)=0$, or $f(0)=0$.

Next $P(x,0)$ gives $f(f(x^2))=xf(x)$. Swapping $x,-x$ implies $f$ odd. Also setting $x=1$ gives $f(f(1))=f(1)$.

Suppose $f(c)=0$ for $c\ne 0$. Taking $P(c,y)$ gives $f(f(c^2)+cy)=0$, so $f$ is constantly zero. Hence assume no $c$ exists.

Take $P(1,-f(1))$ to get $f(-f(1))=-1$. By oddness $f(f(1))=1$, hence with above we have $f(1)=1$.

Take $P(1,y)$ to get $f(y+1)=f(y)+1$. Now comparing $P(x,y)$ with $P(x,y+1)$ gives $f(f(x^2)+xy+x)=f(f(x^2)+xy)+f(x)$. However, for fixed $x\ne 0$, by varying $y$ we see that $f(x^2)+xy$ takes any real value, hence we have $f(z+x)=f(z)+f(x)$ for $x\ne 0$. Since $f(0)=0$, we get $f$ additive.

From the original equation we hence have $f(f(x^2))+f(xy)=xf(x)+f(x)f(y)$, hence $f$ multiplicative. Then $f(x^2)=f(x)^2\ge 0$, so $f$ is bounded on a nontrivial interval, so $f$ is linear. Since $f(1)=1$ we get $f(x)=x$ for all $x$ as desired.
