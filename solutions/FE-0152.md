$$
f(f(x)+f(x)f(y))=f(x)+f(x)f(y).
$$

We claim that the solutions are: Let $\emptyset\ne S\subseteq\mathbb R$ with the closure property that if $a,b\in S$ then $a+ab\in S$. Then $\boxed{f(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if }x\in T\\g(x)&\text{if }x\notin T\end{cases}}$ where $T=\{a+ab\mid a,b\in S\}$ and $g:\mathbb R\setminus T\to S$ is any function.

To see that these work, note that $T\subseteq S$, so $f(x)\in S$ whether $x\in T$ or $x\notin T$. Then $f(x)+f(x)f(y)\in T$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb R$, so $f(f(x)+f(x)f(y))=f(x)+f(x)f(y)$.

To see that these are all of the solutions, take $S=f(\mathbb R)$, it's cleary that $\emptyset\ne S\subseteq\mathbb R$ and that $S$ satisfies the aforementioned closure property. So for $T$ derived from this $S$, $f(x)=x$ for all $x\in T$ and $f(x)\in S$ for all $x\notin T$, hence the result.
