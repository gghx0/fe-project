Solution by **OronSH**.
***

We claim that the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=x}.
$$

Both are easy to check. From $P(0,y)$,

$$
f(f(0))=f(0)^2+yf(0),
$$

so $f(0)=0$.

Now $P(-1,1)$ gives

$$
f(-1)^2+f(-1)=0,
$$

so $f(-1)=0$ or $f(-1)=-1$.

If $f(-1)=0$, then $P(-1,-y)$ gives $f(f(y))=0$ for all $y$. Applying $f$ to both sides of the original equation gives

$$
f(f(x)^2+yf(x))=0.
$$

If some $f(x)\ne0$, then the argument $f(x)^2+yf(x)$ spans all real numbers as $y$ varies, forcing $f\equiv0$, a contradiction to $f(x)\ne0$. Hence $f\equiv0$.

Now assume $f(-1)=-1$. Then $P(-1,-y)$ gives

$$
f(1+f(y))=1+y,
$$

so $f$ is bijective. Taking $y=-2$ and $y=-3$ in this relation gives

$$
f(-2)=-2,\qquad f(-3)=-3.
$$

Using $P(-2,-y/2)$ and comparing with $f(1+f(y+3))=y+4$ gives

$$
f(y+3)=f(y)+3.
$$

Similarly, $P(-3,-y/3)$ gives

$$
f(y+8)=f(y)+8.
$$

Therefore

$$
f(y+1)=f(y+9-8)=f(y)+1.
$$

Comparing $P(x+1,0)$ with $P(x,2)$, and using this shift identity, gives

$$
f(2x)=f(x)+x.
$$

Comparing $P(x+2,0)$ with $P(x,4)$ similarly gives

$$
f(4x)=2f(x)+2x.
$$

But the previous relation also gives

$$
f(4x)=f(2x)+2x=f(x)+3x.
$$

Thus $f(x)=x$ for every $x$.
