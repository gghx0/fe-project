The functions $f(x)=ax$, where $a$ is any real constant, all work. We prove that there are no others.

Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(x^2+y^2)=xf(x)+f(y^2).
$$

Putting $x=1$ and $y=0$ gives $f(0)=0$. Then $P(x,0)$ gives

$$
f(x^2)=xf(x).
$$

Therefore the original equation becomes

$$
f(x^2+y^2)=f(x^2)+f(y^2),
$$

so $f$ is additive on $\mathbb R_{\ge 0}$.

Let $a=f(1)$. For $x\ge 0$, additivity on the nonnegative reals gives $f(x+1)=f(x)+a$ and $f(2x)=2f(x)$. Thus

$$
(x+1)f(x+1)=f((x+1)^2)=f(x^2)+f(2x)+f(1)=xf(x)+2f(x)+a.
$$

Substituting $f(x+1)=f(x)+a$ and simplifying yields $f(x)=ax$ for every $x\ge 0$.

For $x>0$,

$$
-xf(-x)=f((-x)^2)=f(x^2)=xf(x),
$$

so $f(-x)=-f(x)=-ax$. Hence

$$
\boxed{f(x)=ax\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb R}
$$

with $a\in\mathbb R$, and direct verification shows that all such functions work.
