To begin with, denote $c=f(0)$ and $d=f(1)$. From $x=y=0$ we get $c=c^2$, from $x=1$ and $y=0$ we get $d=d^2$, while from $x=0$ and $y=1$ we get $f(d)=c^2+1$. Quick case check on $c$ and $d$ now leads to $f(0)=0$ and $f(1)=1$.

Now taking $y=0$ yields

$$
f(x^2)=f(x)^2
$$

for all $x$. From here $f(t)\geq0$ for $t\geq0$. Now suppose $f(t)\geq0$ for some $t<0$ - then $y=t$ and $x=\sqrt{-tf(t)}$ yields

$$
0=f(x)^2+t^2\geq t^2>0,
$$

contradiction - hence $f(t)<0$ for any $t<0$. Now from $f(x^2)=f(x)^2$ we get (by replacing $x$ with $-x$) $f(x)^2=f(-x)^2$ and because of the sign constrains we deduce $f(-x)=-f(x)$ for all $x$, i.e. $f$ is odd.

It now suffices to determine $f$ on $\mathbb{R}_{>0}$. The main equation is now

$$
f(x^2+yf(y))=f(x)^2+y^2=f(x^2)+y^2,
$$

i.e.

$$
f(t+yf(y))=f(t)+y^2
$$

for any $t\geq0$ and $y\in\mathbb{R}$. Since $f(1)=1$, we deduce

$$
f(t+1)=f(t)+1,
$$

so

$$
f(t)=f(\left\lfloor t \right\rfloor)+f(\{t\})\geq f(\left\lfloor t \right\rfloor)=\left\lfloor t \right\rfloor
$$

for any $t\geq0$. We also get by induction

$$
f(nyf(y))=ny^2
$$

for any positive integer $n$. Hence

$$
ny^2=f(nyf(y))\geq \left\lfloor nyf(y) \right\rfloor>nyf(y)-1,
$$

so $y>f(y)-\frac{1}{ny}$ and setting $n\to \infty$ leads to $f(y)\leq y$ for all $y>0$. Now in $f(yf(y))=y^2$ we deduce $y^2\leq yf(y)$, i.e. $f(y)\geq y$ for all $y>0$. Therefore $f(x)=x$ for all $x>0$, as well as $f(0)=0$, and since $f$ is odd, we conclude $f(x)=x$, which indeed works.
