The solutions are precisely the additive involutions:

$$
\boxed{f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)\quad\text{and}\quad f(f(x))=x}
$$

for all real $x,y$.

For $x\ne 0$, the value $xy$ can be any real number. Hence the equation is equivalent to

$$
f(u+f(t))=f(u)+t \tag{1}
$$

for every $u\in\mathbb R$ and every $t>0$.

Let $G$ be the additive subgroup generated by the numbers $f(t)$ with $t>0$. Relation (1) lets us define a homomorphism $\varphi:G\to\mathbb R$ by

$$
\varphi(f(t))=t \qquad (t>0);
$$

it is well-defined because if a sum of generators is $0$, applying the corresponding translations in (1) to any input forces the same sum of the corresponding positive numbers to be $0$. Thus

$$
f(u+h)=f(u)+\varphi(h) \tag{2}
$$

for all $u\in\mathbb R$ and $h\in G$. In particular, $\varphi$ is surjective, since all positive real numbers lie in its image.

Now take any $h\in G$ and choose $t>0$ so large that $t+h>0$. Since both $t$ and $t+h$ are positive, $f(t)$ and $f(t+h)$ lie in $G$. From (2),

$$
f(t+h)-f(t)=\varphi(h),
$$

so $\varphi(h)\in G$. Applying $\varphi$ gives

$$
\varphi^2(h)=\varphi(f(t+h))-\varphi(f(t))=(t+h)-t=h.
$$

Since $\varphi$ is onto and $\varphi(G)\subseteq G$, we get $G=\mathbb R$. Therefore (2), with $u=0$, gives

$$
f(x)=f(0)+\varphi(x) \tag{3}
$$

for every real $x$, where $\varphi$ is an additive involution.

For $t>0$, we also have $\varphi(f(t))=t$. Using (3),

$$
t=\varphi(f(t))=\varphi(f(0))+\varphi^2(t)=\varphi(f(0))+t,
$$

so $\varphi(f(0))=0$. Since $\varphi^2=\mathrm{id}$, this gives $f(0)=0$. Hence $f=\varphi$, so $f$ is additive and $f(f(x))=x$ for all $x$.

Conversely, any additive involution satisfies

$$
f(f(x^2)+xy)=x^2+f(xy),
$$

so the characterization is complete. Equivalently, as in FE-0006, these functions are obtained from a decomposition $\mathbb R=A\oplus B$ into $\mathbb Q$-vector spaces by $f(a+b)=a-b$.
