We claim $f(x)=0$ and $f(x)=x$ are the solutions, which work.

Assume $f$ is not constantly zero, so $f(x)\ne 0$ for some $x$. Then varying $y$ gives $f$ surjective, and plugging in for $y$ gives $f$ injective.

Fix some $x$ such that $f(x)\ne 0$. Pick $y$ by surjectivity such that $f(x^2)+f(x)f(y)=x^2$, which gives $y=0$, so $f(x^2)+f(x)f(0)=x^2$ whenever $f(x)\ne 0$.

Since at most one value goes to zero, there exists nonzero $x$ such that $f(x),f(-x)$ are both nonzero. Then $f(x^2)+f(x)f(0)=f(x^2)+f(-x)f(0)=x^2$, but $f(x)\ne f(-x)$, hence $f(0)=0$.

Hence for all nonzero $x$ we have $f(x)\ne 0$ by injectivity, hence $f(x^2)=x^2$.

Now fix a negative $k$ and let $f(y)=k$. Choose $x$ positive with $x>-k$. Then $f(f(x^2)+f(x)f(y))=f(x^2+xk)=x^2+xk=f(x^2)+yf(x)=x^2+xy$, hence $k=y$ and thus $f(k)=k$ for all negatives.

Together we get $f(x)=x$ for all $x$, as desired.
