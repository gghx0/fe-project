Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f\left(f(x)+f(y)^2\right)=x+f\left(y^2\right).
$$

First, $f$ is injective. Indeed, if $f(u)=f(v)$, comparing $P(u,y)$ and $P(v,y)$ gives $u=v$. Let $a=f(0)$. From $P(t-a,0)$,

$$
f\left(f(t-a)+a^2\right)=t,
$$

so $f$ is also surjective.

Now $P(0,y)$ gives

$$
f\left(a+f(y)^2\right)=f\left(y^2\right),
$$

and injectivity yields

$$
a+f(y)^2=y^2.
$$

Putting $y=0$ gives $a+a^2=0$. If $a=-1$, then $f(y)^2=y^2+1$ for all $y$, contradicting surjectivity since $0$ is not in the image. Hence $a=0$.

Therefore $f(f(x))=x$ and $f(y)^2=y^2$ for all $x,y$. Substituting $x\mapsto f(x)$ into the original equation gives

$$
f(x+y^2)=f(x)+f(y^2).
$$

So $f(x+t)=f(x)+f(t)$ for every $t\ge 0$. Taking $x=-t$ gives $f(-t)=-f(t)$, and the same identity then extends to all real increments; hence $f$ is additive on $\mathbb R$.

Since $f(x)^2=x^2$, each value satisfies $f(x)=x$ or $f(x)=-x$. Additivity forces the sign to be constant on the positive reals, so

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x}
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=-x\text{ for all }x}.
$$

Both functions are checked directly to work.
