***

We claim $f(x)=x$ is the only solution.

Denote the assertion by $P(x,y)$. Note $P(0,y)$ gives $f(2f(0))=yf(0)$, so $f(0)=0$.

Assume $x,y\ne 0$ and take $P(x,\frac{y^2}x)$. We get $f(f(x^2)+f(y^2))=x^2+\frac{y^2}xf(x)$, so by symmetry the right side is equal to itself with $x,y$ swapped. Simplifying this equation we get $\frac{f(x)}{x^3}-\frac1{x^2}=\frac{f(y)}{y^3}-\frac1{y^2}$, hence $f(x)=cx^3+x$ for some $c$.

Now $P(x,0)$ gives $f(f(x^2))=x^2$, but if $c\ne 0$ then the left side has degree eighteen and the right has degree two, contradiction. Hence $c=0$ so $f(x)=x$ as desired.
