Solution by **Midmogus**.
***

$P(x,0): f(x^2)=x f(x)$, and from

$P(-x,0): f(x)=-f(-x)$.

$P(0,0): f(0)=0$.

First case: there exist $t\ne 0$, such that $f(t)=0$.

$P(t,y): f(t^2)=t f(y)$ or $f(y)=\dfrac{f(t^2)}{t}=\mathrm{const}$. Plugging $y=t$ it gives that $f(t)=0$, so $f(y)=0$.

Second case: if $t\ne 0$, then $f(t)\ne 0$.

$P(x,-x): f(x^2-f(x)^2)=0$, so $x^2=f(x)^2$ or $f(x)\in\{x,-x\}$.

Let there exist $x_1=f(x_1)\ne 0$ and $x_2=-f(x_2)\ne 0$.

Then choose $Y_1=f(y_1)$ or $f(-y_1)$ so that

$$
x_1^2+Y_1x_1=x_2^2=r\ne 0.
$$

Then from $P(x_1,\pm y_1)$ and $P(x_2^2,0)$:

$$
f(r)=x_1^2+Y_1x_1=-x_2^2
$$

or

$$
r=-r\ne 0,
$$

which is a contradiction. Therefore the solutions are $f(x)=x$, $f(x)=-x$ and $f(x)=0$.

Notes: $\pm y_1$ means $f(\pm y_1)=Y_1$.
