Solution by **p_square** and **Supercali**.
***
We claim the only solutions are $f(x)\equiv x$ and $f(x)\equiv 0$, which obviously work. We now show they are the only solutions. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion that $f(x^2+yf(x))=xf(x)+xf(y)$. 
***
$P(0,0) \implies f(0) = 0$

$P(x,0) \implies f(x^2) = xf(x)$ which further tells us that $f(x) = -f(-x)$

If $f(x) = 0, x \neq 0$, $P(x,y) \implies 0 = xf(y)$ and $f$ is constant $0$ which is one function. We now assume $f$ isn't constant.

$P(x,-\frac{y}{f(x)}) + P(x,\frac{y}{f(x)}) \implies f(x^2 - y) + f(x^2 + y) = 2xf(x) = 2f(x^2)$

For $z>0, f(z-y) + f(z+y) = 2f(z) = f(2z) + f(0) = f(2z)$. From oddness, this follows for negatives too, and $f$ is additive.
***

We have $f((x+1)^2)=(x+1)f(x+1)$ $$\implies f(x^2)+2f(x)+f(1)=xf(x)+f(x)+xf(1)+f(1)$$ $$\implies f(x)=xf(1)$$
for all $x$. Substituting in the original, we get $f(1)=0$ or $1$, which gives the required solutions.
