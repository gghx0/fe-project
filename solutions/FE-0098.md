Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(f(x)+y^2)=f(x)+yf(y).
$$

The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=0\quad\text{for all }x}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=x\quad\text{for all }x}.
$$

First,

$$
P(-1,-1)\Rightarrow f(f(-1)+1)=0.
$$

Put $z=f(-1)+1$. Then $P(z,0)$ gives $f(0)=0$, and $P(z,x)$ gives

$$
f(x^2)=xf(x). \tag{1}
$$

Thus $xf(x)=(-x)f(-x)$ for all $x$, so $f$ is odd.

Using (1), the original equation becomes

$$
f(f(x)+y^2)=f(x)+f(y^2).
$$

Hence

$$
f(f(x)+t)=f(x)+f(t) \tag{2}
$$

for all $t\ge0$.

For $t\ge0$, oddness gives

$$
-f(f(x)-t)=f(-f(x)+t)=f(f(-x)+t)=f(-x)+f(t)=-f(x)+f(t),
$$

so

$$
f(f(x)-t)=f(x)+f(-t).
$$

Together with (2), this proves

$$
f(u+f(v))=f(u)+f(v) \tag{3}
$$

for all real $u,v$.

Now substitute $v=y^2$ in (3). Since $f(y^2)=yf(y)$, we get

$$
f(u+yf(y))=f(u)+yf(y).
$$

This is FE-0021. By the classification there, either $xf(x)=0$ for all $x$, or $f(x)=x+c$ for all $x$. Since here $f(0)=0$, these give respectively

$$
f\equiv0
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
f(x)=x.
$$

Both are directly checked.
