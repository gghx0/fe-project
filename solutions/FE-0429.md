Solution by **OronSH**.
***

The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=0},\qquad \boxed{f(x)=x},\qquad \boxed{f(x)=-x}.
$$

The zero function works. Now assume $f$ is not identically zero. If $f(a)=0$ for some $a\ne0$, then $P(a,y)$ gives $f(ay)=f(0)$ for every $y$, so $f$ is constant, impossible. Hence $0$ is the unique zero. Also $P(-f(0),0)$ gives $f(-f(0))=0$, so $f(0)=0$.

Putting $y=1$ in

$$
P(-f(y),y)
$$

gives

$$
f(-f(1))^2=-f(-f(1)).
$$

The value $f(-f(1))$ cannot be $0$, so it is $-1$. Thus $-1$ is in the range. Moreover, if $f(d)=-1$, then $P(d,1)$ gives

$$
f(-d-f(1))=0,
$$

so $d=-f(1)$. Therefore $-1$ has the unique preimage $c=-f(1)$.

If $f(a)=f(b)$ with $a,b\ne0$, comparing

$$
P\left(\frac ca,a\right)
\quad\text{and}\quad
P\left(\frac ca,b\right)
$$

gives $f(bc/a)=-1$, hence $bc/a=c$ and $a=b$. Thus $f$ is injective.

Now $P(1,0)$ and $P(c,0)$ give

$$
f(f(1))=f(1)^2=1,
$$

so $f(1)=\pm1$, and in either case $f(-1)=-f(1)$. Comparing $P(1,y)$ and $P(-1,-y)$ then gives $f(-y)=-f(y)$.

Using $P(-f(x),x)$ together with oddness and $P(x,0)$ yields

$$
f(-f(x))^2=f(x)^2.
$$

Therefore $f(-f(x))=\pm f(x)$. By injectivity and oddness, this implies pointwise

$$
f(x)\in\{x,-x\}.
$$

Thus $f$ is surjective. If $f(1)=1$, then $P(1,y)$ and surjectivity give $f(t+1)=t+1$ for all $t$, hence $f(x)=x$. If $f(1)=-1$, the same equation gives $f(-1-t)=1+t$, hence $f(x)=-x$. The listed functions all work.
