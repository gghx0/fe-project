Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(x^2+xf(y))=f(x^2)+yf(x).
$$

If $f(k)=0$ for some $k\ne0$, then $P(x,k)$ gives $f\equiv0$, which is indeed a solution. Now assume $f(x)\ne0$ for every $x\ne0$.

First,

$$
P(0,-1):\quad f(0)=0.
$$

Then

$$
P(1,-1):\quad f(1+f(-1))=0,
$$

so $f(-1)=-1$. Also,

$$
P(-1,f(1)):\quad f(1-f(f(1)))=0,
$$

so $f(f(1))=1$.

Next,

$$
P(1,f(1)):\quad f(2)=f(1)+f(1)^2,
$$

while

$$
P(-1,-1):\quad f(2)=f(1)+1.
$$

Thus $f(1)\in\{-1,1\}$. If $f(1)=-1$, then $f(2)=0$, impossible by the assumption. Hence $f(1)=1$.

Now

$$
P(1,x):\quad f(1+f(x))=1+x,
$$

so $f$ is surjective. It follows that

$$
f(x+2)=f(x)+2.
$$

Using $P(x,y+2)$ gives

$$
f(x^2+xf(y)+2x)=f(x^2+xf(y))+2f(x).
$$

Since $f$ is surjective, for $x\ne0$ the expression $x^2+xf(y)$ ranges over all real values. Therefore

$$
f(t+2x)=f(t)+2f(x)
$$

for all real $t,x$. Taking $t=0$ gives $f(2x)=2f(x)$, and hence

$$
f(t+2x)=f(t)+f(2x),
$$

so $f$ is additive.

The original equation now becomes

$$
f(xf(y))=yf(x).
$$

With $x=1$, we get $f(f(y))=y$. Replacing $y$ by $f(y)$ then gives

$$
f(xy)=f(x)f(y),
$$

so $f$ is multiplicative. Since $f(1)=1$ and $f(x^2)=f(x)^2\ge0$, the additive function $f$ is order-preserving; hence $f(x)=x$ for all real $x$.

Therefore the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=x}.
$$
