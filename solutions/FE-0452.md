Solution by **OronSH**.
***

Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(xf(x)+xy)=f(x^2)+f(x)f(y).
$$

The functions $f\equiv0$ and $f(x)=x$ both work.

Suppose first that $f(a)=0$ for some $a\ne0$. Then $P(a,y)$ gives

$$
f(ay)=f(a^2)
$$

for all real $y$. Since $a\ne0$, this makes $f$ constant; checking constant solutions gives only $f\equiv0$.

Now assume $0$ has the unique preimage $0$. Put $y=x-f(x)$ in the original equation. Then

$$
f(x^2)=f(x^2)+f(x)f(x-f(x)),
$$

so

$$
f(x)f(x-f(x))=0.
$$

If $f(x)=0$, then $x=0$. Otherwise, $f(x-f(x))=0$, so $x-f(x)=0$. Hence every $x$ satisfies $f(x)=x$, and the nonzero solution is $f(x)=x$.

Together with the constant case, the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=x}.
$$
