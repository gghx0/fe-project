Let $P(x,y)$ be

$$
f(xf(y)+f(xy))=2xy.
$$

First, $f$ is injective. If $f(a)=f(b)$, then $P(1,a)$ and $P(1,b)$ give

$$
f(2f(a))=2a,\qquad f(2f(b))=2b,
$$

so $a=b$.

Swapping $x$ and $y$ in the original equation gives

$$
f(yf(x)+f(xy))=2xy.
$$

By injectivity,

$$
xf(y)+f(xy)=yf(x)+f(xy),
$$

so $xf(y)=yf(x)$ for all $x,y$. Taking $y=1$ gives $f(x)=cx$, where $c=f(1)$.

Substituting $f(x)=cx$ into the original equation,

$$
c(2cxy)=2xy,
$$

so $c^2=1$. Therefore the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\boxed{f(x)=-x\text{ for all }x}.
$$

Both work by direct substitution.
