Denote the assertion as $P(x,y)$.

If

$$
f\equiv 0,
$$

it satisfies.

If not, there exists some real $a$ such that

$$
f(a)\ne0.
$$

From $P(a,y)$,

$$
f\bigl(af(a)+f(a)f(y)\bigr)=af(a)+yf(a),
$$

therefore $f$ is bijective.

From $P(0,1)$,

$$
f(f(0)f(1))=f(0),
$$

so

$$
f(0)f(1)=0.
$$

From $P(1,0)$,

$$
f(f(1)+f(1)f(0))=f(1),
$$

so

$$
f(1)+f(1)f(0)=1.
$$

By combining the above two, we get

$$
f(1)=1,\qquad f(0)=0.
$$

Now, from $P(-f(y),y)$,

$$
0=f(0)=-f(y)f(-f(y))+yf(-f(y)),
$$

so

$$
f(y)f(-f(y))=yf(-f(y)).
$$

Therefore,

$$
f(y)=y
$$

or

$$
f(-f(y))=0.
$$

But in the second case,

$$
-f(y)=0
$$

for all $y\in\mathbb R$, so contradiction. Therefore

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}
$$

for all $x\in\mathbb R$.
