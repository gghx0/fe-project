Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(f(x)+f(y)^2)=x+y^2.
$$

From $P(0,0)$, the function has a zero. Let $c$ be such that $f(c)=0$. Then $P(x,c)$ gives

$$
f(f(x))=x+c^2.
$$

Hence $f$ is bijective, and $c$ is the unique zero of $f$.

Let $a=f(0)$. Since $P(0,0)$ gives $f(a+a^2)=0$, uniqueness of the zero gives

$$
c=a+a^2.
$$

On the other hand, substituting $x=c$ into $f(f(x))=x+c^2$ gives

$$
a=f(0)=c+c^2.
$$

Combining these two relations, the only real possibility is $c=0$, and hence $a=0$.

Therefore $f(f(x))=x$. Replacing $x$ by $f(x)$ and $y$ by $f(y)$ in the original equation gives

$$
f(x+y^2)=f(x)+f(y)^2.
$$

Taking $x=0$ gives $f(y^2)=f(y)^2$, so

$$
f(x+y^2)=f(x)+f(y^2).
$$

Thus $f$ is additive over all nonnegative increments. Taking $x=-t$ with $t\ge 0$ gives $f(-t)=-f(t)$, so this additivity extends to all real increments. Hence $f$ is additive on $\mathbb R$.

Finally, $f(y^2)=f(y)^2\ge 0$ for every $y$, so this additive function is nonnegative on the nonnegative reals. Therefore $f(x)=kx$ for some $k\ge 0$. Since $f(f(x))=x$, we get $k^2=1$, so $k=1$. Hence

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\text{ for all }x}.
$$

This function is checked directly to work.
