We claim that the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=|x|}.
$$

If $f(a)=f(b)$, then comparing $P(a,1)$ and $P(b,1)$ gives $a^2=b^2$.

Also, $P(0,x)$ shows that $f(0)=0$; otherwise $f$ would be constant, which is impossible. Similarly, $f(k)=0$ implies $k=0$.

From $P(x,0)$,

$$
f(f(x)^2)=x^2.
$$

Thus $[0,\infty)\subseteq f(\mathbb{R})$, and

$$
f(x^4)=f(x)^4.
$$

Putting $x=1$ gives $f(1)=1$, and then

$$
P(1,x):\quad f(f(x)+1)=f(x)+1.
$$

Since $f(x)+1$ takes every value at least $1$, $f(t)=t$ for all $t\ge1$. For $0<t<1$, choose $y$ large enough that $y>1$ and $ty>1$. Then $P(t,y)$ gives

$$
f(t)^2+ty=t^2+yf(t).
$$

Varying $y$ gives $f(t)=t$. Hence $f(t)=t$ for all $t\ge0$.

Now $f(f(x)^2)=x^2$ implies $f(x)^2=x^2$, so $f(x)=\pm x$. Finally,

$$
P(-1,x):\quad f(-1)f(x)=-x.
$$

Since $f(-1)\in\{-1,1\}$, the two possibilities are exactly $f(x)=x$ and $f(x)=|x|$.
