Solution by **AIME4096**.
***

The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=kx\quad(x\in\mathbb R,\ k\in\mathbb R)}
$$

and the additional family

$$
\boxed{
f(0)=k,\qquad f(x)=kx\ (x\ne0),\qquad k>0.}
$$

Both families are checked by direct substitution.

Let $c=f(0)$. Comparing $P(x,y)$ and $P(-x,y)$ gives

$$
f(-x)=-f(x)\qquad(x\ne0). \tag{1}
$$

Also

$$
f(c)=c^2,\qquad f(x^2+c)=xf(x)+c^2,\qquad f(f(y^2))=f(y)^2. \tag{2}
$$

One first obtains $c\ge0$. If $c<0$, write $c=-a^2$ with $a>0$. Then (1) and (2) give incompatible values when $P(a^2,a)$ is evaluated.

For the main step, call $(h,k)$ a tail-translation vector if

$$
f(t+h)=f(t)+k
$$

for all sufficiently large $t$. Such vectors form an additive subgroup, and for each $h$ there is at most one corresponding $k$. From (2),

$$
\bigl(f(y^2)-c,\ f(y)^2-c^2\bigr)
$$

is always a tail-translation vector.

If $f\not\equiv0$, there is a tail-translation vector $(h,k)$ with $h>0$ and $k\ne0$. Following the orbit $u_n=\sqrt{x^2+nh}$ and comparing the resulting translation vectors shows that $f(u_n)^2$ has constant first difference. Equivalently,

$$
\frac{(xf(x)+nk)^2}{x^2+nh}
$$

is affine in $n$. Comparing coefficients forces

$$
f(x)=\frac{k}{h}x
$$

for all sufficiently large $x>0$.

Thus every nonzero solution is eventually linear, say $f(x)=rx$ for all large positive $x$. From (2), the tail slope satisfies $rc=c^2$.

If $c=0$, then choosing $y$ large in the original equation gives $f(x)=rx$ for every $x\ne0$, and hence for all $x$. If $c>0$, then $r=c$, and choosing $y$ large gives $f(x)=cx$ for every $x\ne0$, while $f(0)=c$.

This is exactly the two families stated above.
