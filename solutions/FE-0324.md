Solution by **AIME4096**.
***

The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

Let $P(x,y)$ denote

$$
f(x^2+f(xy))=f(x)^2+yf(x).
$$

Clearly $f(0)=0$. If $f\not\equiv0$, choose $a\ne0$ with $f(a)\ne0$. For every point $(z,f(z))$ on the graph $\Gamma$ of $f$,

$$
T_a(z,f(z))=
\left(a^2+f(z),\ f(a)^2+\frac{f(a)}a z\right)
$$

also lies on $\Gamma$. This immediately implies that $f$ is injective and surjective.

Comparing the equations for $x$ and $-x$ shows that $f$ is odd, and then $P(x,0)$ gives

$$
f(x^2)=f(x)^2.
$$

Hence $f(x)>0$ for $x>0$ and $f(x)<0$ for $x<0$.

For $a\ne0$, the affine map $T_a$ is in fact a bijection of $\Gamma$. Its square is the homothety

$$
S_a(X)=c_aX+W_a,\qquad c_a=\frac{f(a)}a>0.
$$

If all $c_a=1$, then $f(x)=x$. Otherwise, two such homotheties with distinct ratios either immediately give a nonzero translation symmetry of $\Gamma$, or their commutator does. A nonzero vertical translation cannot preserve the graph of a function, so this translation has nonzero horizontal component.

Conjugating that translation by powers of one homothety produces a dense additive subgroup $H\subseteq\mathbb R$ and a constant $\lambda$ such that

$$
f(x+t)=f(x)+\lambda t\qquad(t\in H).
$$

Because $f$ preserves sign, density forces $f(x)=\lambda x$ for every $x$. Substitution into the original equation gives $\lambda=1$. Thus every nonzero solution is the identity, and the zero solution is also valid.
