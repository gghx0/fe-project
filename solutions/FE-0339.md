Solution by **AIME4096**.
***

Find all functions $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ such that

$$
f\bigl(xf(x)+yf(y)\bigr)=xf(x)+f(y^2)
$$

for any reals $x,y$.

We claim that the complete solution set is obtained as follows. Let $f(0)=0$ and $f(x)=xq(|x|)$ for all $x\neq0$ where $q:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb R$ satisfies the following properties:

$$
\begin{align*}
q(t)&=q(t^2)\forall t>0, \\
uq(u)+vq(v)&\neq0\Longrightarrow q\bigl(|uq(u)+vq(v)|\bigr)=1.
\end{align*}
$$

for every $u,v\ge0$, where in the second condition terms of the form $0q(0)$ are interpreted as $0$. We will refer to the first condition as the **Scale Invariance** condition, so $q$ is constant along the orbits of the squaring map, while the second will be referred to as the **Value Restriction** condition. We now prove that these conditions are necessary and sufficient. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion $f\bigl(xf(x)+yf(y)\bigr)=xf(x)+f(y^2)$.

**Lemma 1.** We have $f(0)=0$, $f(x^2)=xf(x)$ for every $x\in\mathbb R$, and $f$ is odd.

*Proof.* Let $c=f(0)$. Assertion $P(x,0)$ gives $f(xf(x))=xf(x)+c$, while $P(0,x)$ gives $f(xf(x))=f(x^2)$. Hence $f(x^2)=xf(x)+c$ for every $x$. Taking $x=1$ gives $c=0$, so $f(x^2)=xf(x)$. Applying such identity to $x$ and $-x$ gives $xf(x)=(-x)f(-x)$, hence $f(-x)=-f(x)$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.** For every $x,y\in\mathbb R$, $f\bigl(f(x^2)+f(y^2)\bigr)=f(x^2)+f(y^2)$.

*Proof.* By Lemma $1$, $xf(x)=f(x^2)$ and $yf(y)=f(y^2)$. Substituting these into $P(x,y)$ gives the desired identity. $\square$

**Lemma 3.** Define $q:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb R$ by $q(t)=\frac{f(t)}{t}$. Then $q(t)=q(t^2)$ for every $t>0$ and, if $u,v>0$ satisfy $uq(u)+vq(v)\neq0$, $q\bigl(|uq(u)+vq(v)|\bigr)=1$.

*Proof.* From Lemma $1$, $f(t^2)=tf(t)$ for every $t>0$, so $q(t^2)=\frac{f(t^2)}{t^2}=\frac{f(t)}{t}=q(t)$. Now let $u,v>0$ and put $a=uq(u)=f(u)$ and $b=vq(v)=f(v)$. By Lemma $2$, $f(a+b)=a+b$. If $a+b\neq0$, then if $a+b>0$ we have $q(a+b)=1$, while if $a+b<0$, oddness gives $f(|a+b|)=|a+b|$, so again $q(|a+b|)=1$. Thus $q\bigl(|uq(u)+vq(v)|\bigr)=1$. $\square$

**Lemma 4.** Every solution is uniquely of the form $f(0)=0$ and $f(x)=xq(|x|)$ for $x\neq0$, where $q$ satisfies the two structural properties above.

*Proof.* Given a solution $f$, define $q(t)=\frac{f(t)}{t}$ for $t>0$. Lemma $1$ gives $f(-x)=-f(x)$, so $f(x)=xq(|x|)$ for every $x\neq0$, while Lemma $3$ gives the two required properties. Uniqueness is immediate from $q(t)=\frac{f(t)}{t}$. $\square$

**Lemma 5.** Conversely, let $q:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb R$ satisfy $q(t)=q(t^2)\forall t>0$ and $uq(u)+vq(v)\neq0\Longrightarrow q\bigl(|uq(u)+vq(v)|\bigr)=1\forall u,v>0$. Define $f(0)=0$ and $f(x)=xq(|x|)$ for $x\neq0$. Then $f$ is a solution.

*Proof.* The definition makes $f$ odd. For $x\neq0$, Scale Invariance gives $f(x^2)=x^2q(x^2)=x^2q(|x|)=xf(x)$, and the same identity is trivial for $x=0$. Thus $xf(x)=f(x^2)$ for every $x$. Set $a=xf(x)$ and $b=yf(y)$. If $a+b=0$, then $f(a+b)=f(0)=0=a+b$. Otherwise, writing $u=x^2$ and $v=y^2$ when $x,y\neq0$, we have $a=uq(u)$ and $b=vq(v)$, so the Value Restriction gives $q(|a+b|)=1$. Hence $f(a+b)=a+b$. Therefore $f\bigl(xf(x)+yf(y)\bigr)=xf(x)+yf(y)=xf(x)+f(y^2)$, as required. $\square$

We have therefore proven that our desired parametrization is well-defined, necessary, and sufficient, and the corresponding $q$ is uniquely determined by $f$. $\blacksquare$
