$$
f\left(x^2+f(x)f(y)\right)=f(x)^2+yf(x).
$$

First, $\boxed{f(x)=0}$ is a solution, and so we relegate our search to functions for which some $j$ exists such that $f(j)\ne0$.

$P(j,x)\Rightarrow f\left(j^2+f(j)f(x)\right)=f(j)^2+xf(j)$.

From this alone, $f$ is bijective: if $f(a)=f(b)$ then:

$$
f(j)^2+af(j)=f\left(j^2+f(j)f(a)\right)=f\left(j^2+f(j)f(b)\right)=f(j)^2+bf(j)
$$

so $a=b$, and $f(j)^2+xf(j)$ can take any real value for $x\in\mathbb R$.

$P\left(f^{-1}(x),f^{-1}(y)\right)\Rightarrow f\left(f^{-1}(x)^2+xy\right)=x^2+xf^{-1}(y)$, so $f^{-1}$ satisfies:

$$
f^{-1}\left(x^2+xf^{-1}(y)\right)=f^{-1}(x)^2+xy
$$

which is equation #246 with unique solution $f^{-1}(x)=x$, and therefore $\boxed{f(x)=x}$.
