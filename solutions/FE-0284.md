Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

The zero function is a solution. Assume $f$ is not identically zero, and choose $j$ with $f(j)\ne0$.

From $P(j,x)$,

$$
f(j^2+f(j)f(x))=f(j)^2+xf(j).
$$

This shows that $f$ is injective and surjective. Indeed, if $f(a)=f(b)$ then the displayed equation gives $a=b$, and its right side takes every real value.

Let $h=f^{-1}$. Substituting $x=h(u)$ and $y=h(v)$ in the original equation gives

$$
f(h(u)^2+uv)=u^2+uh(v).
$$

Applying $h$,

$$
h(u^2+uh(v))=h(u)^2+uv.
$$

Thus $h$ satisfies FE-0246, whose unique solution is $h(x)=x$. Hence the nonzero solution is $f(x)=x$.

Therefore the complete solution set is

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=x}.
$$
