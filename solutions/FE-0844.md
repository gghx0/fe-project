where $c\in(-\infty,-2)\cup(-2,-1)\cup(0,\infty)$ is a constant such that $\frac{\ln\lvert2c\rvert}{\ln\lvert c+1\rvert}\notin\mathbb Q\setminus\{1\}$, $V_1,V_2$ are $\mathbb  Q$-supplementary subvectorspaces of $\mathbb R$ such that $\ln\lvert c+1\rvert\in V_1$ and $\ln\left\lvert\frac12+\frac1{2c}\right\rvert\in V_2$, and $v_1:\mathbb R\to V_1$ and $v_2:\mathbb R\to V_2$ are the projections of a real in $V_1,V_2$. where $c\in(-1,\infty)\setminus\{0\}$ is a constant such that $\frac{\ln\lvert2c\rvert}{\ln\lvert c+1\rvert}\notin\mathbb Q\setminus\{1\}$, $V_1,V_2$ are $\mathbb  Q$-supplementary subvectorspaces of $\mathbb R$ such that $\ln\lvert c+1\rvert\in V_1$ and $\ln\left\lvert\frac12+\frac1{2c}\right\rvert\in V_2$, and $v_1:\mathbb R\to V_1$ and $v_2:\mathbb R\to V_2$ are the projections of a real in $V_1,V_2$. This includes the particular solution $f(x)=\lvert x\rvert$ when $c=1$ and $V_1=\mathbb R$.

If $f(1)=0$, then $P(x,x)\Rightarrow f\left(2f\left(x^2\right)\right)=f(x)^2+f\left(x^2\right)$ while $P\left(x^2,1\right)\Rightarrow f\left(2f\left(x^2\right)\right)=f\left(x^2\right)$ so $f(x)=0$ for all $x$ which is one of the aforementioned solutions.

Otherwise, $f(1)\ne0$. Let $g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ such that $g(x)=\frac{f(x)}{f(1)}$ for all $x$, note that no other constant solutions exist for $f$ so $g$ is nonconstant.

$P(xy,1)\Rightarrow f(x)f(y)+f(xy)=f(2f(xy))=f(xy)f(1)+f(xy)$, we can calculate $g(xy)=g(x)g(y)$.

$P(x,1)\Rightarrow f\left(\frac1{2f(1)}\right)f(2f(x))=(f(1)+1)f\left(\frac1{2f(1)}\right)f(x)\Rightarrow g(g(x))=ag(x)$ where $a=f\left(\frac1{2f(1)}\right)\left(1+\frac1{f(1)}\right)$.

Let $Q(x,y)$ be the assertion $g(xy)=g(x)g(y)$.

$Q(x,0)\Rightarrow g(0)(g(x)-1)=0\Rightarrow g(0)=0$ since $g$ is nonconstant.

$Q(x,1)\Rightarrow g(x)(g(1)-1)=0\Rightarrow g(1)=1$ since $g$ is nonconstant.

Therefore $a=ag(1)=g(g(1))=1$.

If $g(k)=0$ for some $k\ne0$, then:

$Q\left(\frac xk,k\right)\Rightarrow g(x)=0$ which contradicts that $g$ is nonconstant.

Hence $g(x)\ne0$ for all $x\ne0$, so for all $x>0$ we have:

$Q\left(\sqrt x,\sqrt x\right)\Rightarrow g(x)=g\left(\sqrt x\right)^2>0$.

Now we can define $h:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ such that $h(x)=\ln g\left(e^x\right)$, the equations $g(xy)=g(x)g(y)$ and $g(g(x))=g(x)$ transform into:

$$
h(x+y)=h(x)+h(y)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad h(h(x))=h(x).
$$

Let $V_1=\{x\mid h(x)=x\}$ and $V_2=\{x\mid h(x)=0\}$. Since $h(x-h(x))=h(x)+h(-h(x))=h(x)-h(h(x))=0$, any element decomposes as:

$$
h(x)=h(v_1)+h(v_2)=v_1+0=v_1
$$

where $v_1=h(x)\in V_1$ and $v_2=x-h(x)\in V_2$. Finally, $V_1\cap V_2=\{0\}$ and $V_1,V_2$ are closed under addition and multiplication by a rational scalar, so $\mathbb R=V_1\oplus V_2$ where $\oplus$ denotes direct sum and $\mathbb R$ is a $\mathbb Q$-vectorspace. So if $v_1:\mathbb R\to V_1$ and $v_2:\mathbb R\to V_2$ are the projections of a real in $V_1,V_2$ then $h(x)=v_1(x)$ for all $x$.

Let $c=f(1)$. Back to the original equation, substitution of $f(x)=cg(x)$ gives $g(2c)=c+1$.

$Q(-1,-1)\Rightarrow g(-1)\in\{-1,1\}$.

Let $u=\ln\lvert2c\rvert$ and $v=\ln\lvert c+1\rvert$.

**Case 1:** $g(-1)=-1$

$Q(x,-1)\Rightarrow g(-x)=-g(x)$, so $g(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)\exp(h(\ln\lvert x\rvert))$.

From $g(x)>0$ for all $x>0$ we have $g(x)<0$ for all $x<0$. Given $g(2c)=c+1$, we have $\operatorname{sgn}(2c)=\operatorname{sgn}(c+1)$ and so this restricts $c\in(-\infty,-1]\cup(0,\infty)$. Now we have $h(u)=v$. Therefore $v\in V_1$ and by additivity $h(v-u)=0$ so $v-u\in V_2$.

If $v=0$ then (since $c\ne0$) $c=-2$, we obtain the first nontrivial solution set, since $-u=-\ln4\in V_2\Rightarrow\ln2\in V_2$.

Otherwise, suppose $u,v$ are $\mathbb Q$-linearly dependent with $u \ne v$. We obtain $v-u=(1-q)v$ so since $v\in V_1$, $v-u\in V_1$ as well and since $V_1\cap V_2=\{0\}$ we must have $v-u=0$, i.e. $u=v$.

In the case that either $u=v$ or $u,v$ are $\mathbb Q$-linearly independent, we have the second nontrivial solution set, ensuring that $v=\ln\lvert c+1\rvert\in V_1$ and $v-u=\ln\left\lvert\frac12+\frac1{2c}\right\rvert\in V_2$.

**Case 2:** $g(-1)=1$

$Q(x,-1)\Rightarrow g(-x)=g(x)$, so $g(x)=\exp(h(\ln\lvert x\rvert))$.

From $g(x)>0$ for all $x>0$ we have $g(x)>0$ for all $x\ne0$. Given $g(2c)=c+1$, we have $c\in(-1,\infty)\setminus\{0\}$. As before, $h(u)=v$ so $v\in V_1$ and $v-u\in V_2$. Our $v=0$ case is impossible this time, since it implies $c=-2$.

If $u,v$ are $\mathbb Q$-linearly dependent with $u\ne v$ then as before this is impossible.

If $u,v$ are $\mathbb Q$-linearly independent, or dependent with $u=v$, then we have the third nontrivial solution set. Verification of the solution sets follows by working backwards.
