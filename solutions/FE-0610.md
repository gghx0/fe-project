Solution by **jasperE3**.
***

The solutions are as follows. Let $\emptyset\ne S\subseteq\mathbb R$ be a set satisfying the closure property

$$
a,b\in S,\ a+b\ge0\implies a+b\in S.
$$

Then

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,& x\in S+S,\\
g(x),& x\in[0,\infty)\setminus(S+S),\\
h(x),& x\in(-\infty,0)\setminus(S+S),
\end{cases}}
$$

where

$$
S+S=\{a+b\mid a,b\in S\},
$$

and $g:[0,\infty)\setminus(S+S)\to S$ and $h:(-\infty,0)\setminus(S+S)\to\mathbb R$ are arbitrary functions.

First we show that every function of this form works. For every $x\ge0$, we have $f(x)\in S$: if $x\in S+S$, then $x=a+b\ge0$ for some $a,b\in S$, so the closure property gives $x\in S$; otherwise $f(x)=g(x)\in S$.

Thus $f(x^2),f(y^2)\in S$ for all real $x,y$. Hence

$$
f(x^2)+f(y^2)\in S+S,
$$

and therefore

$$
f\left(f(x^2)+f(y^2)\right)=f(x^2)+f(y^2).
$$

Conversely, suppose $f$ is a solution and define

$$
S=f([0,\infty)).
$$

Clearly $S$ is nonempty. If $a,b\in S$, then $a=f(u)$ and $b=f(v)$ for some $u,v\ge0$. Writing $u=x^2$ and $v=y^2$, the equation gives

$$
f(a+b)=a+b.
$$

So if additionally $a+b\ge0$, then $a+b=f(a+b)\in f([0,\infty))=S$. This proves the required closure property.

Also, the same argument shows that $f(t)=t$ for every $t\in S+S$. For every $t\in[0,\infty)\setminus(S+S)$, we have $f(t)\in S$ by the definition of $S$, and outside $[0,\infty)\cup(S+S)$ there is no further restriction. This is exactly the claimed form.
