Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(f(x)+xf(y))=f(x)+f(xy).
$$

First,

$$
P(-1,-1):\quad f(0)=f(-1)+f(1).
$$

Also,

$$
P(0,0):\quad f(f(0))=2f(0),
$$

while

$$
P(1,-1):\quad f(f(0))=f(0).
$$

Thus $f(0)=0$, and therefore

$$
f(-1)=-f(1).
$$

If $f(k)=0$ for some $k\ne0$, then comparing $P(x,k)$ and $P(x,0)$ gives $f(xk)=0$ for all $x$, so $f\equiv0$. This is indeed a solution.

Now assume that $f(x)\ne0$ for every $x\ne0$. From $P(x,0)$,

$$
f(f(x))=f(x).
$$

In particular,

$$
f(-f(1))=-f(1).
$$

Using $P(f(1),-1)$ gives

$$
f(f(1)+f(1)f(-1))=0.
$$

Since $0$ has the unique preimage $0$, we obtain

$$
f(1)+f(1)f(-1)=0.
$$

Together with $f(-1)=-f(1)$ and $f(1)\ne0$, this gives

$$
f(1)=1,\qquad f(-1)=-1.
$$

Now

$$
P(-1,x):\quad f(-1-f(x))=-1+f(-x),
$$

and

$$
P(1,x):\quad f(1+f(x))=1+f(x).
$$

Applying $P(1+f(x),-1)$ then gives

$$
f(-x)=-f(x),
$$

so $f$ is odd.

Finally,

$$
P(x,-1):\quad f(f(x)-x)=f(x)+f(-x)=0.
$$

Again using the uniqueness of the preimage of $0$, we get $f(x)-x=0$ for all $x$. Thus the solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=x}.
$$
