$$
f\left(f(x)^2+f(y)^2\right)=xf(x)+f(y)^2.
$$

The only such $f$ are of the form

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\begin{cases}x&\text{if }x\in S\\0&\text{if }x\notin S\end{cases}},
$$

where $\{0\}\subseteq S\subseteq\mathbb R$ is any set with the closure property that if $a,b\in S$ then $a^2+b^2\in S$.

All such $f$ are indeed solutions, by taking cases on whether $x\in S$ and whether $y\in S$.

Now we prove the form.

$P(x,0)\Rightarrow f\left(f(x)^2+f(0)^2\right)=xf(x)+f(0)^2$.

$P(0,x)\Rightarrow f\left(f(x)^2+f(0)^2\right)=f(x)^2$.

Comparing, $f(x)^2=xf(x)+f(0)^2$.

Squaring both sides of $P(x,y)$ we have:

$$
\begin{align*}
&\left(f(x)^2+f(y)^2\right)f\left(f(x)^2+f(y)^2\right)+f(0)^2=\left(xf(x)+f(y)^2\right)^2\\\Rightarrow&\left(f(x)^2+f(y)^2\right)\left(xf(x)+f(y)^2\right)+f(0)^2=\left(xf(x)+f(y)^2\right)^2\\\Rightarrow&xf(x)^3+f(x)^2f(y)^2+f(0)^2=x^2f(x)^2+xf(x)f(y)^2.
\end{align*}
$$
and we can set $x=y=0$ to get $f(0)^4+f(0)^2=0\Rightarrow f(0)=0$. Therefore $f(x)^2=xf(x)$ for all $x$ so if $S=\{x\mid f(x)=x\}$ we indeed have $\{0\}\subseteq S\subseteq\mathbb R$, $f(x)=x$ for all $x\in S$, and $f(x)=0$ for all $x\notin S$. It remains to show that if $a,b\in S$ then $a^2+b^2\in S$, but this is obviously true from the original equation.
