We claim that the solutions are exactly

$$
\boxed{
f(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\in S,\\
0,&x\notin S,
\end{cases}}
$$

where $\{0\}\subseteq S\subseteq\mathbb{R}$ and

$$
a,b\in S\implies a^2+b^2\in S.
$$

Every such function works by checking whether $x$ and $y$ lie in $S$.

Now suppose $f$ is a solution. Comparing $P(x,0)$ and $P(0,x)$ gives

$$
f(x)^2=xf(x)+f(0)^2.
$$

Also,

$$
f(t)^2=tf(t)+f(0)^2
$$

for every $t$. Applying this with $t=f(x)^2+f(y)^2$ and using the original equation gives

$$
(f(x)^2+f(y)^2)(xf(x)+f(y)^2)+f(0)^2=(xf(x)+f(y)^2)^2.
$$

Putting $x=y=0$ gives $f(0)=0$. Hence

$$
f(x)^2=xf(x),
$$

so $f(x)=0$ or $f(x)=x$ for every $x$.

Let

$$
S=\{x:f(x)=x\}.
$$

Then $0\in S$, and $f$ has the displayed form. Finally, if $a,b\in S$, then the original equation gives

$$
f(a^2+b^2)=a^2+b^2,
$$

so $a^2+b^2\in S$.
