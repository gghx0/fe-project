Plugging $y=0$ gives

$$
f(0)=0.
$$

If

$$
f(k)=0
$$

for some nonzero $k$, then plugging $x=k$ gives

$$
f(ky)=0,
$$

so $f$ is constantly $0$, which works.

Otherwise,

$$
f(x)=0
$$

if and only if $x=0$.

For a nonzero $x$, if

$$
f(x)\ne x,
$$

then plugging

$$
y=\frac{x^2}{x-f(x)}
$$

satisfies

$$
x^2+yf(x)=xy.
$$

Hence

$$
f(x^2)=0,
$$

so

$$
x^2=0,
$$

a contradiction. Therefore

$$
f(x)=x
$$

for all $x$, which works.

The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)=0\quad\text{and}\quad f(x)=x}.
$$
