We claim that the unique solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

This function clearly works. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(f(x)+yf(y))=f(x)+y^2.
$$

First we show that $f$ has a zero. If $f(0)=0$, this is immediate. If $f(0)<0$, then

$$
P\left(0,\sqrt{-f(0)}\right)
$$

gives a value at which $f$ is zero.

It remains to rule out $f(0)>0$. From $P(-1,-1)$ and $P(0,-1)$ we get

$$
f(-1)=f(0)-1,\qquad f(1)=f(0)+1.
$$

Then $P(-1,1)$ gives

$$
f(2f(0))=f(0).
$$

On the other hand, putting $y=\sqrt{f(0)}$ in $P(0,y)$ gives some input $A$ with

$$
f(A)=2f(0).
$$

Applying $P(A,0)$ gives

$$
f(2f(0))=2f(0),
$$

contradicting $f(0)>0$. Hence there exists $k$ such that $f(k)=0$.

Now $P(k,x)$ gives

$$
f(xf(x))=x^2.
$$

Using this in $P(xf(x),y)$ gives

$$
f(x^2+yf(y))=x^2+y^2.
$$

By FE-0185, the unique solution of this equation is $f(x)=x$. Therefore the unique solution here is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$
