**Note: we show the solution for the statement in the AoPS thread, which is $f(f(x)f(y)+x^2)=f(xy)+xf(x)$.**
***
Solution by **Mr. C**, **gghx** and **jasperE3**.
***
We claim the only solutions are $f(x)\equiv x$, $f(x)\equiv -x$ and $f(x)\equiv 0$, which obviously work. We now show they are the only solutions. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion that $f(f(x)f(y)+x^2)=f(xy)+xf(x)$. 
***
Suppose $f(u)=0$. Then, $P(u,y)$ gives $f(u^2)=f(uy)$, so if $u\ne 0$ then $f(x)=0$. Suppose from now on that $f$ is non-constant and hence injective at 0.

From $P(-1,1)$, $f(f(-1)f(1)+1)=0$, hence $f(0)=0$ and $f(-1)f(1)=-1$.

From $P(x,0)$, $f(x^2)=xf(x)$, hence $-xf(-x)=xf(x)$ so $f$ is odd. This means that $f(1)^2=-f(-1)f(1)=1$,so $f(1)=1$ or $-1$. 
***
Note that if $f$ is a solution, then so is $-f$. Hence, WLOG $f(1)=1$.

From $P(x,-x)$ and using injectivity at 0, $f(x)f(-x)+x^2=0$. From oddness, this implies that $f(x)^2=x^2$ so $f(x)=x$ or $-x$ for each real $x$.

Suppose $f(a)=a$ and $f(b)=-b$ for non-zero reals $a,b$.

$P(a,b)\Rightarrow f(-ab+a^2)=f(ab)+a^2\Rightarrow (-ab+a^2)^2=(ab)^2+2a^2f(ab)+a^4\Rightarrow f(ab)=-ab$

$P(b,a)\Rightarrow f(-ab+b^2)=f(ab)-b^2\Rightarrow (-ab+b^2)^2=(ab)^2-2b^2f(ab)+b^4\Rightarrow f(ab)=ab$

So $ab=0$, contradiction. The only solutions now are $\boxed{f(x)=x}$ and $\boxed{f(x)=-x}$, which both work.
