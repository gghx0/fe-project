Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(x^2+xf(y))=xf(x)+xf(y).
$$

First, $P(0,0)$ gives $f(0)=0$. Also,

$$
P(x,0):\quad f(x^2)=xf(x).
$$

Comparing this with $P(-x,0)$ gives $f(-x)=-f(x)$, so $f$ is odd.

Now

$$
P(-1,x):\quad f(1-f(x))=f(1)-f(x).
$$

Applying $P(1-f(x),x)$ and using the previous relation gives

$$
f(1)-f(x)=(1-f(x))(f(1)-f(x))+(1-f(x))f(x).
$$

If $f(1)\ne1$, this forces $f(x)=0$ for all $x$, which is one solution. Now assume $f(1)=1$.

Then

$$
P(1,x):\quad f(1+f(x))=1+f(x).
$$

Comparing $P(x-f(y),y)$ and $P(-x,y)$ gives

$$
(x+f(y))f(x+f(y))=xf(x)+2xf(y)+f(y)^2.
$$

Putting $y=-x$ yields

$$
(x-f(x))f(x-f(x))=f(x)^2-xf(x).
$$

Thus if $f(x)\ne x$, then

$$
f(x-f(x))=-f(x).
\tag{2}
$$

For such an $x$, we have $x\ne0$. Since

$$
f(x^2)=xf(x)\ne x^2
$$

and

$$
P(x,1):\quad f(x^2+x)=xf(x)+x\ne x^2+x,
$$

we may apply (2) to both $x^2$ and $x^2+x$. This gives the same left-hand argument $x^2-xf(x)$, but right-hand sides differing by $x$, a contradiction. Therefore $f(x)=x$ for every $x$.

The solutions are

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=x}.
$$
