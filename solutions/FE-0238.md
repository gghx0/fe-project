$f\left(x^2+xf(y)\right)=xf(x)+xf(y)$.

$P(0,0)\Rightarrow f(0)=0$.

$P(x,0)\Rightarrow f\left(x^2\right)=xf(x)$ so $f$ is odd.

$P(-1,x)\Rightarrow f(1-f(x))=f(1)-f(x)$.

$P(1-f(x),x)\Rightarrow f(1)-f(x)=(1-f(x))(f(1)-f(x))+(1-f(x))f(x)$.

If $f(1)\ne1$, this simplifies to $\boxed{f(x)=0}$ which is indeed a solution. Otherwise, assume $f(1)=1$.

$P(1,x)\Rightarrow f(1+f(x))=1+f(x)$.

$P(-x-f(y),y)\Rightarrow f\left(x^2+xf(y)\right)=(x+f(y))f(x+f(y))-xf(y)-f(y)^2$.

Comparing with $P(x,y)$, we obtain:

$$
(x+f(y))f(x+f(y))=xf(x)+2xf(y)+f(y)^2.
$$

Set $y=-x$, this becomes:

$$
(x-f(x))f(x-f(x))=f(x)^2-xf(x),
$$

so $f(x-f(x))=-f(x)$ for all $x$ such that $f(x)\ne x$.

In particular, if $f(x)\ne x$ then $x\ne0$ and so:

$P(x,1)\Rightarrow f\left(x^2+x\right)=xf(x)+x\ne x^2+x$ and $f\left(x^2\right)=xf(x)\ne x^2$ so substituting $x^2$ and $x^2+x$ into $f(x-f(x))=-f(x)$ we have:

$$
x^2\Rightarrow f\left(x^2-xf(x)\right)=-xf(x),
$$

$$
x^2+x\Rightarrow f\left(x^2-xf(x)\right)=-xf(x)-x.
$$

Comparing, we obtain $x=0$, contradiction.

Therefore no such $x$ exists, so we must have $\boxed{f(x)=x}$ for all $x$.
