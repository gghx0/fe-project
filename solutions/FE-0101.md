**Note: we show the solution for the statement of IMO 1992 Q2, which has $x$ and $y$ swapped.**
***
Solution by **Mr. 1**.
***

We claim the only solutions are $f(x)\equiv x$ and $f(x)\equiv -x$, which obviously work. We now show they are the only solutions. Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion that $f(xf(x)+f(y))=x^2+y$. 
***
$P(0,y)\implies f(f(t))=y$

$P(x,y)-P(f(x),y)\implies f(f(x)x+f(y))-f(xf(x) + f(y))=f(x)^2+y-x^2-y=0\implies f(x)^2=x^2$ so $f(x)=\pm x$.

If $f(a)=a$ and $f(b)=-b$ where $a,b\neq 0$, $P(a,b)\implies f(a^2-b)=a^2+b=\begin{cases} a^2-b \\ b-a^2\end{cases}\implies a=0$ or $b=0\implies$ contradiction so $f(x)=x$ or $f(x)=-x$
