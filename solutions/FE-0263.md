***

Let $P(x,y)$ denote the assertion

$$
f(x^2+yf(x))=f(x)^2+xf(y).
$$

It is clear that

$$
f(x)\equiv 0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
f(x)=x
$$

are solutions.

Now $P(0,0)$ gives

$$
f(0)=f(0)^2,
$$

so $f(0)\in\{0,1\}$. If $f(0)=1$, then $P(0,y)$ gives $f(y)=1$ for all $y$, but then the original equation gives $1=1+x$, a contradiction. Hence $f(0)=0$.

Then $P(x,0)$ gives

$$
f(x^2)=f(x)^2.
$$

Therefore the original equation becomes

$$
f(x^2+yf(x))=f(x^2)+xf(y).
$$

This is exactly FE-0268, whose solutions are $f(x)\equiv0$ and $f(x)=x$. Hence the solutions here are

$$
\boxed{f(x)\equiv0\quad\text{or}\quad f(x)=x}.
$$
