We claim that the unique solution is

$$
\boxed{f(x)=x}.
$$

From $P(0,y)$,

$$
f(f(0)^2)=f(0)f(y).
$$

There are no constant solutions, so $f(0)=0$. From $P(1,0)$,

$$
f(f(1)^2)=1.
$$

Using $P(t,0)$ in the form $f(f(t)^2)=t^2$, with $t=f(1)^2$, gives $f(1)^4=f(1)$. Since $f(1)\ne0$, we have $f(1)=1$.

Thus

$$
P(1,y):\quad f(y+1)=f(y)+1.
$$

Now compare $P(x,y+1)$ and $P(x,y)$:

$$
f(f(x)^2+xy+x)=f(f(x)^2+xy)+f(x).
$$

For $x\ne0$, the expression $f(x)^2+xy$ spans all real numbers as $y$ varies, so

$$
f(t+x)=f(t)+f(x)
$$

for all $t$ and all $x\ne0$. This is also true for $x=0$, hence $f$ is additive.

Finally, $P(x,0)$ gives $f(f(x)^2)=x^2$. By additivity, the original equation becomes

$$
f(f(x)^2)+f(xy)=x^2+f(x)f(y),
$$

so

$$
f(xy)=f(x)f(y).
$$

Thus $f$ is both additive and multiplicative, with $f(1)=1$. Since $f(t^2)=f(t)^2\ge0$, it is order-preserving, so $f(x)=x$ for every real $x$.
