$$
f(f(x)f(y)+f(xy))=2xy.
$$

We claim the following solution set:

$$
\boxed{f(x)=\begin{cases}c\operatorname{sgn}(x)\exp(v_1(\ln\lvert x\rvert)-v_2(\ln\lvert x\rvert))&\text{if }x\ne0\\0&\text{if }x=0\end{cases}}
$$

where $c\in(-1,\infty)\setminus\{0,2\}$ is any constant with $\frac{\ln\left\lvert c^2+c\right\rvert}{\ln2-\ln\lvert c\rvert}\notin\mathbb Q\setminus\{-1,1\}$, $V_1,V_2$ are two $\mathbb Q$-supplementary subvectorspaces of $\mathbb R$ with $\ln(2c+2)\in V_1$ and $\ln\left(\frac12c^3+\frac12c^2\right)\in V_2$, and $v_1:\mathbb R\to V_1$ and $v_2:\mathbb R\to V_2$ are the projections of a real in $V_1,V_2$.

This includes the particular solution $f(x)=x$ when $c=1$ and $V_1=\mathbb R$, and the particular solution $f(x)=\begin{cases}-\frac1{2x}&\text{if }x\ne0\\0&\text{if }x=0\end{cases}$ when $c=-\frac12$ and $V_2=\mathbb R$.

Suppose $f(1)=0$.

$P(x,1)\Rightarrow f(f(x))=2x\Rightarrow f(2x)=f(f(f(x)))=2f(x)$.

$P(x,x)\Rightarrow f\left(f(x)^2+f\left(x^2\right)\right)=2x^2\Rightarrow f\left(2x^2\right)=2f(x)^2+2f\left(x^2\right)$, using $f(2x)=2f(x)$ we obtain $f(x)=0$ for all $x$ which contradicts $f(f(x))=2x$.

So $f(1)\ne0$.

$P(x,1)\Rightarrow f(f(x)f(1)+f(x))=2x$, so $f$ is injective.

$P(xy,1)\Rightarrow f(f(xy)f(1)+f(xy))=2xy=f(f(x)f(y)+f(xy))\Rightarrow f(xy)f(1)=f(x)f(y)$by injectivity. Let $g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ such that $g(x)=\frac{f(x)}{f(1)}$, we have that $g$ is multiplicative. From $f(xy)f(1)=f(x)f(y)$ we can reduce $f(f(x)f(1)+f(x))=2x$ to $f\left(f(1)^2+f(1)\right)g(g(x))=2x$. Then $f\left(f(1)^2+f(1)\right)\ne0$ so we can let $a=\frac2{f\left(f(1)^2+f(1)\right)}\ne0$ and we have $g(g(x))=ax$.

Let $Q(x,y)$ be the assertion $g(xy)=g(x)g(y)$, from $g(g(x))=ax$ we have that $g$ is bijective.

$Q(x,0)\Rightarrow g(0)(g(x)-1)=0\Rightarrow g(0)=0$ by surjectivity.

$Q(x,1)\Rightarrow g(x)(g(1)-1)=0\Rightarrow g(1)=1$ by surjectivity.

Now $a=g(g(1))=1$.

$Q(-1,-1)\Rightarrow g(-1)^2=1\Rightarrow g(-1)=-1$ by injectivity.

$Q(x,-1)\Rightarrow g(-x)=-g(x)$ so $g$ is odd.

For all $x\ne0$, we have $g(x)\ne0$ (injectivity) and so:

$Q\left(\sqrt x,\sqrt x\right)\Rightarrow g(x)=g\left(\sqrt x\right)^2>0$ for all $x>0$.

Now we can define $h:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ such that $h(x)=\ln g\left(e^x\right)$, the above equations transform into:

$$
h(x+y)=h(x)+h(y)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad h(h(x))=x.
$$

which implies #6. Therefore, $h(x)=v_1(x)-v_2(x)$ where $V_1=\{x\mid h(x)=x\}$ and $V_2=\{x\mid h(x)=-x\}$ are two $\mathbb Q$-supplementary subvectorspaces of $\mathbb R$ and $v_1:\mathbb R\to V_1$ and $v_2:\mathbb R\to V_2$ are the projections of a real in $V_1,V_2$, and we can go further back: $g(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)\exp(v_1(\ln\lvert x\rvert)-v_2(\ln\lvert x\rvert))$ and therefore

$$
f(x)=c\operatorname{sgn}(x)\exp(v_1(\ln\lvert x\rvert)-v_2(\ln\lvert x\rvert)).
$$

where $c=f(1)$. It remains to deal with $c$.

Substituting $f(x)=cg(x)$ into $P(x,y)$, we have:

$$
cg\left(c^2g(x)g(y)+cg(xy)\right)=2xy\Rightarrow cg\left(c^2+c\right)g(g(xy))=2xy
$$

using multiplicativity. Recall $g(g(x))=x$, so setting $xy\ne0$ this is $g\left(c^2+c\right)=\frac2c.$ From $g(x)>0$ for all $x>0$ and the oddness of $g$, $\operatorname{sgn}\left(c^2+c\right)=\operatorname{sgn}\left(\frac2c\right)$. This places $c\in(-1,\infty)\setminus\{0\}$.

Let $u=\ln\left\lvert c^2+c\right\rvert=\ln(c+1)+\ln\lvert c\rvert$ and $v=\ln\left\lvert\frac2c\right\rvert=\ln2-\ln\lvert c\rvert$. We have $h(u)=v$ by its definition, and since $h$ is an involution, $h(v)=u$. By additivity $h(u+v)=u+v$ so $u+v\in V_1$, and $h(u-v)=v-u$ so $u-v\in V_2$.

Since $h(u)=v$ and $h(v)=u$, $u=0\Leftrightarrow v=0$. If $u=v=0$ then $\left\lvert\frac2c\right\rvert=\left\lvert c^2+c\right\rvert=1$ which has no solutions, so we must have $u,v\ne0$.

Suppose $u,v$ are $\mathbb Q$-linearly dependent with $u\notin\{v,-v\}$.

Let $q=\frac uv\ne1$, then $u+v=\frac{q+1}{q-1}(u-v)$ so since $u-v\in V_2$, $u+v\in V_2$ as well and since $V_1\cap V_2=\{0\}$ we must have $u+v=0$, contradicting $u\ne-v$.

Therefore they are either $\mathbb Q$-linearly independent (where we have $\frac uv=\frac{\ln\left\lvert c^2+c\right\rvert}{\ln2-\ln\lvert c\rvert}\notin\mathbb Q$ and $u+v=\ln(2c+2)\in V_1$ and $u-v=\ln\left(\frac12c^3+\frac12c^2\right)\in V_2$) or $\mathbb Q$-linearly dependent with $\frac uv\in\{-1,1\}$. Verification of the solution sets follows by working backwards.
